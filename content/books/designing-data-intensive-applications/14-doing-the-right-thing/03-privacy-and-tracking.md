When tracking serves advertisers instead of users, data collection becomes surveillance. Consent is
often not meaningful, privacy is a decision right transferred to companies, data is a hazardous asset,
and we need both regulation and a culture shift.

## Learning objectives

- Explain when tracking serves the user and when it becomes surveillance
- Critique consent as practiced, and GDPR's consent requirements
- Define privacy as a decision right and explain its transfer to companies
- Explain why data is a "toxic asset" and why future governments matter
- Draw lessons from the Industrial Revolution and weigh legislation against self-regulation

## In a nutshell

- Replace "data" with "surveillance" and see how the sentence sounds.
- Users can't consent meaningfully to what they can't understand; data about one user reveals others.
- Opting out of essential services isn't a free choice.
- "Data is the pollution problem of the information age."
- Minimize collection, purge data you no longer need — data you don't have can't leak.

## Connections to today

- **AI training on user content** has revived debates over consent and opt-outs across major platforms.
- **Data brokers** remain the subject of regulatory action in the US and EU.
- **Retention policies and TTLs** in modern data platforms (e.g., Delta/Iceberg vacuum, Kafka retention)
  are the technical side of data minimization.
