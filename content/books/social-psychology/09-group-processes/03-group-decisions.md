## Learning objectives

- Define process loss and give examples
- Explain the failure to share unique information and how transactive memory helps
- Define groupthink, its antecedents, symptoms and remedies
- Define group polarization and explain its two causes

## Key ideas

**Process loss** (p. 245): group interaction that inhibits good problem solving, such as failing to find the expert, communication problems or pressure to agree.

**Failure to share unique information** (p. 245): groups discuss shared information and neglect what only one member knows (Stasser & Titus's hidden profiles). Fixes: more time, assigning expertise, asking each person what they uniquely know. **Transactive memory** (p. 245): a group's combined memory, where members know who knows what, is more efficient than individuals'.

**Groupthink** (Janis; p. 246): cohesiveness and solidarity outweigh realistic appraisal. Antecedents: high cohesiveness, isolation, a directive leader, high stress. Symptoms: illusion of invulnerability, belief in moral correctness, stereotyping outsiders, self-censorship, direct pressure on dissenters, mindguards, illusion of unanimity. Examples: the Bay of Pigs, Challenger and Columbia. Remedies: impartial leaders, outside opinions, subgroups, anonymous opinions, a devil's advocate.

**Group polarization** (p. 248): groups make more extreme decisions than members' initial inclinations, due to **persuasive arguments** (new arguments in the dominant direction) and **social comparison** (wanting to appear to hold the valued position, slightly more so).
