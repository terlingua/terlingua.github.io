## Learning objectives

- Define a social dilemma and explain the prisoner's dilemma
- Explain how to increase cooperation, including tit-for-tat
- Explain why threats escalate conflict (the trucking game)
- Explain when communication helps
- Define negotiation and integrative solutions

## Key ideas

**Social dilemma** (p. 254): the most beneficial action for an individual, if chosen by most people, harms everyone. The **prisoner's dilemma** (p. 255) models it. Trust, being labelled a cooperative situation (the Community Game, chapter 1), and the **tit-for-tat** strategy increase cooperation (p. 256).

**Threats** (p. 256): Deutsch and Krauss's trucking game: when one or both players had gates (threat capacity), both earned less; bilateral threat was worst.

**Communication** (p. 258) helps only when it builds trust; in the trucking game, required communication did not help when players were in a threatening, competitive frame, but tutoring on fair communication did.

**Negotiation** (p. 259): offers and counteroffers until both agree. People assume fixed pies and miss **integrative solutions**, trading off issues with different priorities. Naïve realism makes each side think the other is biased.
