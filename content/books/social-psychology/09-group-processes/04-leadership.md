## Learning objectives

- Evaluate the great person theory of leadership
- Distinguish transactional and transformational leadership
- Explain Fiedler's contingency theory
- Explain barriers women face in leadership, including role congruity and the glass cliff

## Key ideas

**Great person theory** (p. 250): certain traits make a good leader regardless of situation. Evidence is weak: traits like intelligence and extraversion correlate only modestly with leadership effectiveness.

**Leadership styles** (p. 250): **transactional** leaders set clear short-term goals and reward people who meet them; **transformational** leaders inspire focus on long-term common goals. Good leaders often do both.

**Contingency theory** (Fiedler; p. 250): effectiveness depends on whether the leader is **task-oriented** or **relationship-oriented** and on how much situational control the leader has. Task-oriented leaders do best in very high or very low control situations; relationship-oriented leaders in moderate control.

**Women and leadership** (p. 252): role congruity theory says the leader role is associated with agentic traits and the female gender role with communal traits, so women leaders face a double bind; women are also more often appointed in crises (the glass cliff).
