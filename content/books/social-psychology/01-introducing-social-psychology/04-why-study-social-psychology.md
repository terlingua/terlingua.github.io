## Learning objectives

- Explain the two reasons social psychologists study social influence
- Explain Kurt Lewin's legacy of applying research to social problems
- Pull together the chapter's concepts: situation, construal, motives, culture

## Key ideas

Social psychologists study social influence because it is fascinating, and because understanding it helps solve social problems, a goal present since Kurt Lewin, who fled Nazi Germany and wanted to understand how his country had been transformed (p. 19). The field has studied violence, prejudice, conservation, health, juries and relationships. Social media is called a "dream laboratory" because the whole range of social behavior appears there (p. 19).

The chapter's thread: situations are powerful, but their power runs through **construal**, which is shaped by the motives to **feel good about ourselves** and to **be accurate**, and by **culture**.
