## Learning objectives

- Define social perception and nonverbal communication
- Distinguish encoding and decoding
- Explain the evidence for universal facial expressions of emotion
- Explain why decoding is sometimes difficult, including affect blends
- Define display rules and emblems and give cultural examples

## Key ideas

**Social perception** is how we form impressions of and make inferences about other people (p. 137). **Nonverbal communication** includes facial expressions, tone of voice, gestures, posture, movement, touch and gaze (p. 139). We **encode** (express) and **decode** (interpret) these signals.

**Facial expressions** (p. 140): Darwin proposed they are evolved and universal; Ekman and colleagues found six basic emotions (anger, happiness, surprise, fear, disgust, sadness) are encoded and decoded similarly across cultures, with pride, embarrassment and others studied as candidates. **Decoding is hard** (p. 142) because of **affect blends**, partial expressions, and expressions shaped by culture.

**Culture** (p. 143): **display rules** dictate which emotions may be shown where; **emblems** are gestures with culture-specific meanings (thumbs-up, the "OK" sign) that can offend across cultures. Eye contact, personal space and touch also vary.
