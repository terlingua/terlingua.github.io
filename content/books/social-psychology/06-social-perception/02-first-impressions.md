## Learning objectives

- Explain thin-slicing and how fast impressions form
- Explain the primacy effect and why first impressions linger
- Connect first impressions to belief perseverance and self-fulfilling prophecies

## Key ideas

People form impressions from faces in as little as a tenth of a second (Willis & Todorov, 2006) and draw conclusions from brief samples of behavior, **thin-slicing** (Ambady & Rosenthal, 1993), sometimes with surprising accuracy for traits like extraversion, less for others (p. 145).

**The lingering influence** (p. 146): the **primacy effect**, in which the first traits we perceive shape how we interpret later information (Asch, 1946: "intelligent, industrious, impulsive, critical, stubborn, envious" versus the reverse order). Once formed, impressions persist through **belief perseverance** and can create **self-fulfilling prophecies** (chapter 5).
