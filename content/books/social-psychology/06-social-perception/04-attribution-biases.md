- id: bias-blind-spot
  prompt: Most engineers in a survey say colleagues are influenced by favoritism in code reviews, but that they themselves are not. Which concept describes this?

## Learning objectives

- Explain the fundamental attribution error and the Jones and Harris essay study
- Explain perceptual salience and the actor–observer difference
- Describe the two-step attribution process and when the second step happens
- Define self-serving attributions and defensive attributions such as belief in a just world
- Explain the bias blind spot

## Key ideas

**Fundamental attribution error (FAE)** (p. 152): overestimating dispositional causes and underestimating situational ones. Jones and Harris (1967): people inferred an essay writer's attitude toward Castro from the essay even when told the position was assigned.

**Perceptual salience** (p. 154): the person is what we attend to, so the person seems causal. Taylor and Fiske (1975) found whoever observers faced was judged more influential; Storms (1973) reversed actors' and observers' attributions by switching video perspective. Actors explain their own behavior more situationally than observers do.

**Two-step attribution** (p. 156): first an automatic internal attribution; then, given time, motivation and cognitive resources, an adjustment for the situation (Gilbert). Busy observers stop at step one.

**Self-serving attributions** (p. 156): success is mine, failure is the situation's. **Defensive attributions** protect us from feeling vulnerable, including **belief in a just world**.

**Bias blind spot** (p. 159): thinking others are more biased than we are (Pronin).
