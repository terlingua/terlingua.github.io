## Learning objectives

- Define cultural scripts and cultural fluency, and explain how they connect to naïve realism and belief superiority
- Describe cross-cultural differences in seeking and following advice, and in reading product reviews
- Explain how class shapes decision-making within the United States
- Explain cultural influences on the self-concept, including the Masako Owada example and baby names
- Describe what research shows about computer-mediated communication in multicultural teams and why techno-solutionism falls short

## Key ideas

Culture helps people make sense of ambiguous situations (how much to tip, p. 65). **Cultural scripts** are norms, values and practices insiders understand; **cultural fluency** is the ease of knowing what to expect (pp. 65–66). Fluency feeds **naïve realism**; in U.S. politics it shows up as **belief superiority**, which is not backed by superior knowledge (pp. 66–67).

**Decisions**: Russians offer and ask for advice more than Americans; Indians are more likely to follow advice and feel closer to advisers; Japanese readers remember and weigh negative product-review information more than North Americans (pp. 67–68). Working-class Americans prefer to involve others in decisions, like people in India, Japan and Russia (p. 68).

**Self-concept** (pp. 69–70): Masako Owada's choice looked like coercion to people with independent selves and natural to people with interdependent selves. Recently settled U.S. states give rarer baby names, a sign of independence (Varnum & Kitayama, 2011).

**Technology** (pp. 71–75): in U.S.–Chinese teams, more fluent English speakers talked more and were seen as more competent though they were not, which hurt team performance; computer-mediated communication buffered this (Li et al., 2019). But **techno-solutionism** is not the answer for bridging divides: face-to-face conversations with strangers go better than people expect (Atir et al., 2022; Kardas et al., 2021), and deep canvassing conversations durably reduced transphobia (Broockman & Kalla, 2016).
