## Learning objectives

- Define conformity and explain why it is neither good nor bad in itself
- Define informational social influence and describe Sherif's autokinetic studies
- Distinguish private acceptance from public compliance
- Explain when informational conformity backfires (contagion, mass psychogenic illness)
- Explain when people conform to informational influence

## Key ideas

**Conformity** is a change in behavior due to the real or imagined influence of others (p. 264). Two motives: to be right (**informational**) and to be liked (**normative**).

**Informational social influence** (p. 267): using others as information in ambiguous situations. Sherif (1936): in the autokinetic effect (a stationary light appearing to move in a dark room), group members' estimates converged on a shared norm that persisted when they were later alone, showing **private acceptance** rather than mere **public compliance**.

**Accuracy** (p. 269): when the task is important and ambiguous, people conform more (Baron et al., 1996). **Backfire** (p. 270): when others are wrong, informational influence spreads errors, as in **contagion** and **mass psychogenic illness**, and in the 1938 *War of the Worlds* panic (much exaggerated in later retellings).

**When** (pp. 272–273): when the situation is ambiguous, when it is a crisis, and when others are experts.
