## Learning objectives

- Explain how culture shapes which schemas people hold
- Distinguish analytic and holistic thinking styles and describe evidence for them
- Explain that these styles can be primed and vary within cultures

## Key ideas

**Cultural determinants of schemas** (p. 126): culture supplies many of the schemas we use, so people from different cultures notice and remember different things.

**Analytic thinking** focuses on objects' properties apart from context, common in Western cultures; **holistic thinking** focuses on the whole context and relationships, common in East Asian cultures (p. 127). Masuda and Nisbett (2001) showed underwater scenes: Japanese participants mentioned background and relationships more, Americans the focal fish. Eye tracking shows similar differences. Thinking style can be primed (for example by photos of Japanese vs. U.S. towns) and varies within cultures (rice versus wheat regions in chapter 3; Brazilians are more holistic than Americans).
