## Learning objectives

- Define the self-fulfilling prophecy and describe its three steps
- Describe Rosenthal and Jacobson's classroom study
- Explain automatic goal pursuit and how goals can be primed

## Key ideas

A **self-fulfilling prophecy** (p. 117) has three steps: (1) a perceiver has an expectation about a target; (2) the expectation shapes how the perceiver treats the target; (3) the target behaves consistently with the expectation, confirming it. Neither person usually realizes what happened.

Rosenthal and Jacobson (1968): teachers were told some randomly chosen students were "bloomers." By year's end those students' IQ scores had risen more, because teachers gave them more warmth, attention, feedback and challenge.

**Automatic goal pursuit** (p. 119): goals, like schemas, can be activated by cues outside awareness and then guide behavior. People can pursue a primed goal without knowing it was activated.
