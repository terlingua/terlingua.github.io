## Learning objectives

- Explain why close relationships are harder to study than first impressions
- Distinguish companionate and passionate love
- Describe cultural variation in love
- Explain attachment styles and their adult consequences
- Describe what neuroimaging shows about love

## Key ideas

Long-term relationships are hard to study experimentally because people cannot be randomly assigned to partners (p. 388).

**Companionate love**: intimacy and affection without passion; **passionate love**: intense longing with physiological arousal (p. 389). Hatfield's Passionate Love Scale (Try It!, p. 390).

**Culture** (p. 390): love is universal but defined differently. Collectivist cultures emphasize obligations to family and the social network; individualist cultures emphasize personal feelings.

**Attachment styles** (p. 392): expectations about relationships learned from early caregivers. In Hazan and Shaver's (1987) survey, 56% secure, 25% avoidant, 19% anxious (Table 13–2, p. 393). Secure people have the most enduring, satisfying relationships; anxious people the shortest; avoidant people the least commitment. Styles can change with new relationships.

**The brain** (p. 394): thinking about a loved one activates dopamine-rich reward regions.
