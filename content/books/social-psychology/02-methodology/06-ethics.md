- id: irb

## Learning objectives

- Explain the ethical dilemma between realism and participant welfare
- Define informed consent, deception and debriefing
- Describe the role of the Institutional Review Board
- Summarize what research shows about participants' reactions to deception

## Key ideas

Researchers want realistic, engaging situations but must protect participants (p. 44). **Informed consent** resolves the dilemma when feasible; Wilson et al. (2014) obtained consent for mild shocks before leaving people alone with their thoughts for 15 minutes, and two thirds of men and a quarter of women shocked themselves at least once (p. 45). When telling people in advance would ruin the study, researchers use **deception**, which then requires **debriefing**. Studies of participants find they generally accept mild deception and discomfort, and some report learning and enjoying more (p. 45). Every institution receiving U.S. federal research funding has an **IRB** with at least one scientist, one nonscientist and one unaffiliated member (p. 45). APA principles appear in Figure 2–3 (p. 46).
