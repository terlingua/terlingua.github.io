## Learning objectives

- Explain what speed and confidence reveal about identification accuracy
- Explain post-identification feedback and its effects
- Explain the recovered memory debate and false memory syndrome

## Key ideas

No surefire way exists to tell accurate witnesses from inaccurate ones. Witnesses who identify a suspect within about **10 seconds** and with high confidence are more likely to be right (Dunning & Perretta, 2002), though speed and confidence do not guarantee accuracy, as Jennifer Thompson's confident, mistaken identification of Ronald Cotton shows (p. 450).

**Post-identification feedback** (p. 451): telling a witness "Good job, you got the suspect" inflates confidence; observers could no longer tell accurate from inaccurate witnesses (Smalarz & Wells, 2014).

**Recovered memories** (pp. 452–453): memories of events such as abuse that were said to be forgotten or repressed. Some may be accurate, but **false memory syndrome**, believing a false memory, is likely when someone such as a therapist suggests events probably occurred.
