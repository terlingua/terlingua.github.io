## Learning objectives

- Distinguish intrinsic from extrinsic motivation
- Explain the overjustification effect and when rewards do and do not undermine motivation
- Distinguish fixed and growth mindsets and their consequences
- Explain social comparison theory, upward and downward comparison

## Key ideas

**Intrinsic motivation**: doing something because you enjoy it; **extrinsic motivation**: doing it for rewards or pressures (p. 91). The **overjustification effect**: people attribute their behavior to compelling extrinsic reasons and underestimate their intrinsic interest, so rewarding an enjoyable activity can reduce later interest (Lepper, Greene & Nisbett, 1973). Rewards are most damaging when they are given simply for doing the task (task-contingent) and least when they give informative feedback on how well you did (performance-contingent), and they do not undermine activities people did not enjoy to begin with.

**Mindsets** (p. 94; Dweck): a **fixed mindset** sees ability as a set amount; a **growth mindset** sees achievement as the result of effort, new strategies and input from others. Growth mindsets predict persistence after failure.

**Knowing ourselves through other people** (p. 95): **social comparison theory** (Festinger, 1954). **Upward comparison** (to people better than us) can inspire or deflate; **downward comparison** (to people worse off) makes us feel better.
