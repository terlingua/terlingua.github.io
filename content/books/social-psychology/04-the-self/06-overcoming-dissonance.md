## Learning objectives

## Key ideas

**Self-affirmation theory** (Steele, 1988; p. 105): people can reduce threats to self-esteem by affirming themselves in an unrelated area. Affirmed people are less defensive and more open to threatening information. Brief **values-affirmation writing exercises** (Try It!, p. 106) have improved school performance for students facing stereotype threat (Cohen et al., 2006), though effects vary across settings and replications.

Self-justification can escalate: each step of rationalization makes the next easier, the "pyramid of choice" metaphor. Breaking the cycle means separating the mistake from the self: "I did something stupid" is not the same as "I am stupid."

**Concluding thoughts** (p. 107): the self is constructed socially; we learn about ourselves from behavior, from others and from culture, and we protect our self-image, sometimes at the cost of accuracy.
