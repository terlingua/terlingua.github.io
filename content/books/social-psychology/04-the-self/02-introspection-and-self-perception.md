- id: two-factor
  prompt: According to the two-factor theory of emotion, what produces an emotional experience?
  explanation: The two-factor theory is “The idea that emotional experience is the result of a two-step self-perception process in which people first experience physiological arousal and then seek an appropriate explanation for it” (glossary p. 504; section p. 89). In Schachter and Singer's (1962) study, participants injected with epinephrine who were not told about its effects took on the emotion of a confederate (euphoric or angry); those told what to expect attributed their arousal to the drug. Arousal is the fuel; the situation tells us which emotion it is.

## Learning objectives

- Define introspection and explain its limits, including causal theories and "telling more than we can know"
- Explain self-perception theory and when it applies
- Explain the two-factor theory of emotion and misattribution of arousal

## Key ideas

**Introspection** is looking inward at your own thoughts, feelings and motives (p. 87). People introspect less than they think and often lack access to the real causes of their behavior. When asked why, they rely on **causal theories**, often learned from their culture (Nisbett & Wilson, 1977).

**Self-perception theory** (Bem, 1972): when inner states are uncertain or ambiguous, we infer them by observing our own behavior and the situation in which it occurs (p. 89).

**Two-factor theory of emotion** (Schachter & Singer, 1962; p. 89): emotion = physiological arousal + a cognitive label. Because arousal is ambiguous, people can **misattribute** it to the wrong source.
