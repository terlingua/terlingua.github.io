## Learning objectives

- Explain attitude inoculation
- Explain why product placement is persuasive and how to be alert to it
- Describe approaches to resisting peer pressure
- Explain reactance theory and when persuasion backfires

## Key ideas

**Attitude inoculation** (McGuire, 1964; p. 191): exposing people to weak versions of counterarguments, which they refute, builds resistance to later, stronger attacks, like a vaccine.

**Product placement** (p. 191): brands placed in shows are persuasive partly because people do not realize they are being persuaded, so they do not counterargue. Being forewarned helps.

**Peer pressure** (p. 192): programs that teach people to resist peers' pressure (for example, to smoke) by role-playing refusals and inoculating against arguments can help, especially when they address affective appeals.

**Reactance theory** (Brehm; p. 193): threats to freedom create an unpleasant state people reduce by doing the forbidden thing. Pennebaker and Sanders (1976): a sign saying "Do not write on these walls under any circumstances" drew more graffiti than "Please don't write on these walls."
