## Learning objectives

- Explain LaPiere's study and the attitude–behavior gap
- Explain when attitudes predict spontaneous behavior, and define attitude accessibility
- Explain the theory of planned behavior and its three determinants of intentions

## Key ideas

LaPiere (1934) toured the U.S. with a Chinese couple and was refused service only once, yet most establishments later said by letter they would not serve Chinese guests (p. 173). Attitudes do not always predict behavior.

**Spontaneous behaviors** (p. 174) are predicted by **accessible** attitudes, those that come quickly to mind.

**Deliberative behaviors** (p. 174): the **theory of planned behavior** (Ajzen) says intentions are the best predictor, determined by (1) **specific attitudes** toward the behavior (p. 175), (2) **subjective norms** (p. 176), and (3) **perceived behavioral control** (p. 176). Davidson and Jaccard (1979): general attitudes about birth control barely predicted pill use; specific attitudes toward using the pill in the next two years predicted it well.
