## Learning objectives

- Explain how advertising works, including matching ads to attitude types
- Define subliminal messages and evaluate claims about subliminal advertising
- Explain cultural differences in advertising
- Define propaganda

## Key ideas

Advertising works (p. 186): people think others are influenced but not themselves, yet ad spending changes sales. Ads targeting affective attitudes use emotion and conditioning; ads for cognitive products use arguments; ads for personally relevant products work through the central route.

**Subliminal messages** are not consciously perceived (p. 187). James Vicary's 1957 "Eat popcorn/Drink Coca-Cola" claim was a hoax, and subliminal self-help tapes do nothing (p. 188). Lab studies show limited effects under specific conditions, for example a subliminal brand prime increased choice of that drink only for people who were already thirsty (Karremans et al., 2006). Ordinary, conscious advertising is far more powerful.

**Culture** (p. 189): ads in more individualistic countries emphasize personal benefits; in more collectivist countries, family and group benefits (Han & Shavitt, 1994). **Propaganda** manipulates mass attitudes through misleading or emotionally charged information.
