## Learning objectives

- Explain when fear-arousing communications work and when they backfire
- Explain how emotions serve as a heuristic for attitudes
- Explain why matching the message to the attitude's basis works
- Describe effects of body movements on attitude change

## Key ideas

**Fear-arousing communications** (p. 182) work when they moderately frighten people *and* give specific, doable recommendations; extreme fear without an action plan can produce defensiveness and denial (Leventhal's tetanus studies).

**Emotions as a heuristic** (p. 184): people use how they feel as information ("how do I feel about it?"), often misattributing incidental moods to the message.

**Emotion and types of attitudes** (p. 184): affective attitudes are changed more by emotional appeals, cognitive attitudes by arguments (Shavitt, 1990).

**The body** (p. 185): nodding while listening increases agreement with a message, shaking the head decreases it (Wells & Petty, 1980); body movements can also increase confidence in one's own thoughts (Briñol & Petty).
