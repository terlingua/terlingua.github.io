## Learning objectives

- Describe experimental studies of media violence effects
- Describe longitudinal studies and what they add
- Explain the difficulty of determining cause and effect, and the debates about effect sizes

## Key ideas

**Experimental studies** (p. 355): exposure to violent media increases aggressive thoughts, feelings and short-term behavior in the lab, and can desensitize people to violence (less physiological arousal, less helping).

**Longitudinal studies** (p. 357): following children over years, more exposure predicts more aggression later, even controlling for earlier aggression (Huesmann et al., 2003).

**Cause and effect** (p. 358): media effects are real but modest; they interact with other risk factors (family, peers, personality), and researchers debate their size. Effects are strongest for people already prone to aggression. Chapter 2's correlational example (violent TV and aggression) is resolved here: experiments support a causal effect of media violence on aggression, though most viewers do not become violent.
