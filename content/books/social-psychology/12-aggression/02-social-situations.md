## Learning objectives

- Explain frustration–aggression theory and relative deprivation
- Explain provocation and reciprocation
- Explain the weapons effect
- Explain how utilitarian moral reasoning, dehumanization and compassion collapse enable institutional violence

## Key ideas

**Frustration–aggression theory** (p. 349): being blocked from a goal increases the probability of aggression, especially when the goal is close and the frustration seems illegitimate. Barker, Dembo and Lewin (1941): children shown toys but kept from them played destructively when finally allowed. **Relative deprivation**: frustration from comparing oneself with others who have more.

**Provocation and reciprocation** (p. 351): people respond to provocation with aggression; mitigating explanations reduce retaliation.

**Weapons effect** (p. 352): the mere presence of a gun can increase aggression (Berkowitz & LePage, 1967).

**Institutional violence** (pp. 352–354): **utilitarian** reasoning (greatest good for the greatest number) can justify harm; **deontological** thinkers hold absolute moral rules. **Dehumanization** denies victims full humanity. **Compassion collapse**: people feel less compassion for many victims than for one.
