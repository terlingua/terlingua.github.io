## Learning objectives

- Explain when punishment reduces aggression and when it backfires
- Define catharsis and explain why venting increases aggression
- Explain how aggressive acts lead to more aggression through self-justification
- Describe what to do with anger: self-awareness, communication training, apologies
- Explain how empathy counters dehumanization and how inclusion disrupts the rejection–rage cycle

## Key ideas

**Punishment** (p. 359): severe punishment models aggression and works only if prompt and certain; mild punishment can work better for children because it leads to internalization (chapter 4). For adults (p. 360), certainty matters more than severity; harsh punishment such as the death penalty does not reliably deter.

**Catharsis** (p. 360): the idea that blowing off steam reduces aggression. Evidence shows the opposite: venting (punching bags, aggressive sports) increases later aggression (Bushman, 2002). **Aggressive acts** lead to more aggression (p. 361), partly through self-justification and **blaming the victim**.

**What to do with anger** (p. 362): calm down, then express feelings without blame, with self-awareness. **Communication and problem-solving training** (p. 363), **effective apologies** that take responsibility (p. 364), **building empathy** to counter dehumanization (p. 364), and **disrupting the rejection–rage cycle** through inclusion (p. 365; Twenge et al., 2001).
