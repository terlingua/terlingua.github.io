## Learning objectives

- Define coping styles; distinguish fight-flight-or-freeze and tend-and-befriend
- Define social support and explain why invisible support works better than visible support
- Explain cultural differences in seeking support
- Explain how writing about traumatic events helps

## Key ideas

**Coping styles** (p. 436): ways people react to stress. **Fight-flight-or-freeze**: attack, flee or go still. **Tend-and-befriend** (Taylor; p. 437): nurturing (tending) and building social networks (befriending), more common among women.

**Social support** (p. 437): the perception that others are responsive to one's needs. **Invisible support**, help given without the recipient being singled out as needing it, works better than **visible support**, which can signal incompetence (p. 439). People from collectivist cultures seek explicit support less, worrying about burdening others or disrupting harmony, and benefit more from support they don't have to ask for (p. 439).

**Reframing** (pp. 439–440): Pennebaker found writing about traumatic events for a few days led to better health months later; finding meaning helps.
