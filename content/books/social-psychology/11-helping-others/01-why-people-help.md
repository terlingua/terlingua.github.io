## Learning objectives

- Define prosocial behavior and altruism
- Explain kin selection, the norm of reciprocity and group selection
- Explain social exchange theory's account of helping
- Explain the empathy–altruism hypothesis and Batson's evidence

## Key ideas

**Prosocial behavior** is any act meant to benefit another; **altruism** is helping even at a cost to oneself (p. 305).

**Evolutionary psychology** (p. 307): **kin selection** favors helping genetic relatives (Burnstein et al., 1994: people say they would help close kin more in life-or-death situations); the **norm of reciprocity** (p. 308) favors helping those who may help us later; the **Dictator Game** (Try It!, p. 309) shows people often share even when they don't have to; **group selection** (p. 309) suggests groups of cooperators can outcompete groups of selfish individuals.

**Social exchange** (p. 310): helping is driven by rewards (reciprocity, relief of distress, social approval, self-worth) and costs.

**Empathy and altruism** (p. 311): Batson's **empathy–altruism hypothesis**: when we feel empathy, we help for purely altruistic reasons. In the Carol study (Toi & Batson, 1982), high-empathy participants helped regardless of whether they would see Carol later; low-empathy participants helped mainly when they would.
