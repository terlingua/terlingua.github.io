## Learning objectives

- Explain how knowing about the bystander effect increases intervention
- Describe practical ways to make bystanders more likely to help
- Explain how to increase volunteerism without undermining intrinsic motivation

## Key ideas

**Educating bystanders** (p. 330): Beaman and colleagues (1978) found students who had learned about the bystander effect were much more likely to help in a staged emergency weeks later. Teaching people the five steps, encouraging them to single out individuals when they need help, and promoting a sense of shared identity all help.

**Volunteerism** (p. 332): people volunteer for many motives (values, career, social, self-esteem). Mandatory volunteering can undermine future volunteering through the overjustification effect (chapter 4), unless people feel the choice is theirs. Helping others also tends to make the helper happier (see the sustainability chapter).
