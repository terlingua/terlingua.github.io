## Learning objectives

- Evaluate the idea of an altruistic personality
- Describe gender differences in prosocial behavior
- Describe cultural and religious differences in helping
- Explain how positive and negative moods affect helping

## Key ideas

**Altruistic personality** (p. 315): people who help in many situations exist, but personality predicts helping only modestly; situations matter a lot (Hartshorne & May, 1929).

**Gender** (p. 315): men more often help in chivalrous, heroic, short-term ways; women more often in nurturing, long-term ways, consistent with social roles.

**Culture** (p. 317): people help in-group members more, especially in collectivist cultures; cultures differ in norms such as *simpatía* and helping strangers. **Religion** (p. 318): religious people help more within their community; effects on helping strangers are mixed.

**Mood** (p. 319): **feel good, do good**: Isen and Levin (1972) found people who found a dime in a phone booth were far more likely to help someone pick up papers. **Feel bad, do good** (p. 320): guilt increases helping; sadness can too, when helping is expected to lift mood.
