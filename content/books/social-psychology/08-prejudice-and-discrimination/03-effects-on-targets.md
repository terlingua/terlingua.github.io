## Learning objectives

- Explain how prejudice produces self-fulfilling prophecies (Word, Zanna & Cooper)
- Define social identity threat and stereotype threat, and describe Steele and Aronson's study
- Describe strategies for overcoming social identity threat

## Key ideas

**Self-fulfilling prophecy** (p. 212): Word, Zanna and Cooper (1974) found White interviewers sat farther away, made more speech errors and ended interviews sooner with Black applicants; when trained interviewers treated White applicants that way, those applicants performed worse.

**Social identity threat** (p. 214): the threat felt when people think others will evaluate them as a member of their group rather than as individuals. **Stereotype threat** is a form of it: Steele and Aronson (1995) found Black students underperformed on a test described as diagnostic of ability, but not when it was described as non-diagnostic. Similar effects for women in math and in other groups.

**Overcoming** (p. 215): reframing tests, teaching about stereotype threat, role models, values affirmation, and growth mindsets can reduce the threat. Replications show the effect is real but smaller and more context-dependent than early studies suggested.
