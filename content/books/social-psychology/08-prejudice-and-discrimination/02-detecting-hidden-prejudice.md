## Learning objectives

- Explain why prejudice is hard to measure with self-reports
- Describe ways of identifying automatic and implicit prejudice
- Explain the Implicit Association Test and the debate about what it measures and predicts

## Key ideas

Social norms make people reluctant to report prejudice, and some prejudice is outside awareness (p. 208). Researchers use unobtrusive measures, such as the **bogus pipeline**, nonverbal behavior and field audits, to detect **automatic** prejudice (p. 209).

**Implicit prejudice** is the degree to which people associate social identities with "good" and "bad." The **Implicit Association Test (IAT)** measures how quickly people pair faces with positive or negative words (p. 210). Researchers debate how well IAT scores predict individual behavior; associations are better understood as reflecting culture and context than as fixed personal traits.
