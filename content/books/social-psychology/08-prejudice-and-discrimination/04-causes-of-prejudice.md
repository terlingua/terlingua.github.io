## Learning objectives

- Explain how normative rules and conformity maintain prejudice
- Explain social identity theory, ethnocentrism, in-group bias and minimal groups
- Explain out-group homogeneity
- Explain blaming the victim and justifying entitlement
- Explain realistic conflict theory and the Robbers Cave study

## Key ideas

**Normative rules** (p. 216): people adopt the prejudices of their group to fit in; changing norms (for example desegregation laws) can change prejudice.

**Social identity theory** (Tajfel; p. 218): part of self-esteem comes from group memberships, so people favor their groups. **Ethnocentrism**: believing one's group is superior. **In-group bias**: favoring one's group, even in **minimal groups** formed on trivial criteria (Tajfel's dot-estimation studies). **Out-group homogeneity** (p. 219): "they're all alike, we're diverse." **Blaming the victim** (p. 219): just-world beliefs. **Justifying entitlement and superiority** (p. 221).

**Realistic conflict theory** (p. 222): competition for limited resources produces conflict and prejudice. Sherif's Robbers Cave study created hostility between two groups of boys through competition and reduced it with **superordinate goals** requiring cooperation.
