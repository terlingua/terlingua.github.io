## Learning objectives

- State the contact hypothesis and the conditions under which contact reduces prejudice
- Define interdependence and explain the jigsaw classroom
- Explain why jigsaw works and how cooperative learning spread

## Key ideas

**Contact hypothesis** (Allport, 1954; p. 224): contact reduces prejudice under the right conditions: mutual interdependence, a common goal, equal status, informal interpersonal contact, multiple contacts with several out-group members, and supportive social norms. A meta-analysis of over 500 studies found contact reliably reduces prejudice (Pettigrew & Tropp, 2006). Desegregated schools often failed these conditions.

**Jigsaw classroom** (Aronson; p. 226): small diverse groups where each child has a unique piece of the material, so everyone depends on everyone. Results: more liking across groups, higher self-esteem, better performance for minority students, less prejudice.

**Why it works** (p. 228): interdependence makes people listen, empathize and stop competing; helping others increases liking (Ben Franklin effect, chapter 4). **Spread** (p. 229): cooperative and interdependent learning is now widely used.
