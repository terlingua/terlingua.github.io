questions:
  - id: r-kcal
    prompt: A snack contains 10 g protein, 20 g carbohydrate and 10 g fat. Approximately how many kcal does it provide?
    options:
      - text: 160 kcal
      - id: two-ten
        text: 210 kcal
        correct: true
      - text: 400 kcal
    explanation: (10 × 4) + (20 × 4) + (10 × 9) = 40 + 80 + 90 = 210 kcal.
  - id: r-design
    prompt: Which design can best establish that a diet causes a reduction in heart attacks?
    options:
      - text: A cross-sectional survey
      - text: A case report
      - id: rct
        text: A large, long randomised controlled trial with hard outcomes
        correct: true
    explanation: Converging evidence from cohorts and genetics adds confidence, but randomisation is the strongest single tool for causation.
  - id: r-nnt
    prompt: An intervention lowers 10-year risk from 10% to 8%. What is the number needed to treat for 10 years to prevent one event?
    options:
      - text: "2"
      - text: 20%
      - id: fifty
        text: "50"
        correct: true
    explanation: Absolute risk reduction = 2 percentage points = 0.02; NNT = 1 / 0.02 = 50. The relative risk reduction is 20%.
  - id: r-protein-calc
    prompt: Using the RDA of 0.8 g/kg, what is the protein RDA for a 75 kg adult?
    options:
      - id: sixty
        text: 60 g/day
        correct: true
      - text: 7.5 g/day
      - text: 150 g/day
    explanation: 75 × 0.8 = 60 g.
  - id: r-sfa-calc
    prompt: On a 2,000 kcal diet, what is the maximum saturated fat allowed by a "below 10% of energy" limit?
    options:
      - text: About 10 g
      - id: twenty-two

        correct: true
      - text: About 200 g
    explanation: 10% of 2,000 kcal = 200 kcal; 200 ÷ 9 kcal/g ≈ 22 g.

## Learning objectives

- Test knowledge across every chapter
- Practise the calculations that make nutrition claims concrete

## How to use this review

These questions draw on every chapter. If you miss one, the explanation points to the relevant idea; revisit that
chapter before moving on. Several questions involve arithmetic — converting grams to calories, percentages to
grams, and relative to absolute risk — because numbers are the best defence against vague claims.

## Key numbers to remember

| Quantity | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Energy per gram | Carbohydrate 4, protein 4, fat 9, alcohol 7 kcal |
| Protein RDA | 0.8 g/kg/day |
| Fibre AI | 14 g per 1,000 kcal |
| Saturated fat | Below 10% of energy |
| Free sugars (WHO) | Below 10% of energy, ideally below 5% |
| Sodium (WHO) | Below 2 g/day (5 g salt) |
| Caffeine | Up to about 400 mg/day for healthy adults; below 200 mg in pregnancy |
| Folic acid | 400 µg/day before conception and in early pregnancy |
| Vitamin D | 600 IU adults (RDA); 400 IU breastfed infants |
| US standard drink | 14 g alcohol |
| Poultry cooking temperature | 74 °C (165 °F) |
| Pasteurisation (HTST) | 72 °C for 15 seconds |
