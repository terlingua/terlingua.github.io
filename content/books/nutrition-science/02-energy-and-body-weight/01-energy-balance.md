## Learning objectives

- Describe the components of energy expenditure
- Explain why energy balance is true but not the whole story
- Understand metabolic adaptation and its limits

## The calorie

A kilocalorie (kcal, the "Calorie" on food labels) is the energy needed to raise 1 kg of water by 1 °C.
Food labels use the **Atwater factors**:

| Macronutrient | kcal per gram |
|---------------|---------------|
| Carbohydrate | 4 |
| Protein | 4 |
| Fat | 9 |
| Alcohol | 7 |
| Fibre | about 2 (varies) |

These are averages. Whole almonds, for example, deliver fewer calories than labels claim because some fat
stays trapped in cell walls.

## Where energy goes

| Component | Share of total, typical sedentary adult |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| Resting metabolic rate (organs, maintaining body temperature) | 60–70% |
| Thermic effect of food (digestion and processing) | about 10% (protein highest, fat lowest) |
| Physical activity — exercise plus non-exercise activity (NEAT) | 20–30%, highly variable |

## What is certain

- **Established:** body fat changes only when energy intake differs from expenditure. This is the first law
  of thermodynamics applied to a living system; no diet has escaped it in a metabolic ward
- **Established:** expenditure falls when weight falls, both because a smaller body costs less to run and
  because of adaptive changes
- **Strong:** appetite rises during weight loss (higher ghrelin, lower leptin), which makes maintenance hard
- **Uncertain:** the existence and strength of a defended "set point" for body weight

## The carbohydrate–insulin model

This model claims that refined carbohydrate raises insulin, which traps fat in fat cells, causing hunger and
lowered expenditure. It is a legitimate hypothesis that was tested directly. Hall's metabolic-ward studies
(2015, 2016) found no fat-loss advantage of low-carbohydrate diets at equal calories, and the large
DIETFITS trial (2018) found no difference in weight loss between healthy low-fat and healthy low-carbohydrate
diets over 12 months, and no effect of baseline insulin secretion. Its proponents dispute aspects of these
trials. Grade: **Not supported** as a primary explanation of obesity; low-carbohydrate diets remain a valid
*tool* for people who find them easier.

## Metabolic adaptation

Participants in *The Biggest Loser* had resting metabolic rates about 500 kcal/day below prediction six years
later. That was extreme, rapid weight loss. In more typical weight loss the adaptation is smaller — tens to
low hundreds of kcal/day — and its long-term size is still debated.

## Energy expenditure across life

Pontzer et al. (2021), using doubly labelled water data on 6,421 people, found size-adjusted expenditure is
highest in infancy, declines through childhood, is stable from about 20 to 60, and declines thereafter.
