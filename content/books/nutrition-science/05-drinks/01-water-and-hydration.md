## Learning objectives

- Estimate fluid needs realistically
- Recognise over-hydration as a real risk
- Dismiss hydration myths

## What is known

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Thirst is a reliable guide for healthy adults in normal conditions | **Strong** |
| Older adults have a blunted thirst response and are at greater risk of dehydration | **Strong** |
| Eight glasses a day is a requirement | **Not supported** |
| Tea and coffee count towards fluid intake | **Established** |
| Over-drinking during endurance events can kill | **Established** |
| Drinking more water aids weight loss | **Uncertain** — small trials show modest effects, mostly by replacing sugary drinks |
| Alkaline, hydrogen or "structured" water has health benefits | **Not supported** |
| Bottled water is safer than treated municipal tap water | **Not supported** in countries with regulated water supplies |

## Safe drinking water

Treated drinking water, chlorination and sanitation are among the largest public-health advances in history.
Unsafe water still causes hundreds of thousands of diarrhoeal deaths each year (WHO). "Raw water" (untreated
spring water) marketed as healthier carries the same pathogen risks that water treatment was invented to
remove.
