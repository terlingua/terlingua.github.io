## Learning objectives

- Know nutrition priorities in childhood and old age
- Understand undernutrition as well as overnutrition

## Children

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| The first 1,000 days (conception to age two) are critical for growth and brain development | **Established** |
| Repeated exposure increases acceptance of vegetables | **Strong** |
| Sugary drinks contribute to excess weight and tooth decay in children | **Strong** to **Established** |
| Fruit juice should be limited (AAP — none under 12 months; up to 120 ml for ages 1–3) | **Strong** |
| Sugar causes hyperactivity | **Not supported** |
| Children need special "toddler milks" | **Not supported** — these are unnecessary per WHO and AAP |

## Older adults

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Protein needs are higher (about 1.0–1.2 g/kg) | **Strong** |
| B12 deficiency is common and under-diagnosed | **Strong** |
| Vitamin D plus calcium reduces fractures in institutionalised elderly | **Strong** |
| Antioxidant supplements prevent dementia | **Not supported** |
| The MIND diet prevents dementia | **Uncertain** — a 2023 RCT found no significant cognitive benefit over a control diet with mild calorie restriction |
