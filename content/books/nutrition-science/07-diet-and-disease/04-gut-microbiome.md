title: The gut microbiome
questions:
  - id: established-microbiome
    prompt: Which microbiome-based treatment is **Established** by RCTs?
    options:
      - id: fmt
        text: Faecal microbiota transplantation for recurrent Clostridioides difficile infection
        correct: true
      - text: Probiotics to cure autism
      - text: Kombucha to cure depression
    explanation: FMT cures a large majority of recurrent C. difficile infections — the clearest proof that the gut microbiome matters. Most other microbiome claims are early-stage.
  - id: probiotics-aad
    prompt: For which use do probiotics have the most consistent evidence?
    options:
      - id: aad
        text: Reducing antibiotic-associated diarrhoea (moderate certainty)
        correct: true
      - text: Weight loss in healthy adults
      - text: Preventing all infections
    explanation: Effects depend on the strain and dose; "probiotic" is not one thing. A 2018 study found that probiotics after antibiotics delayed the return of a person's normal microbiome compared with no treatment.
  - id: association
    prompt: Many studies show people with a disease have a different microbiome. What is the key limitation?
    options:
      - id: direction
        text: Differences may be a consequence of the disease, its treatment or diet, rather than a cause
        correct: true
      - text: The microbiome cannot be measured
      - text: Bacteria do not live in the gut
    explanation: Reverse causation applies here too. Causality requires interventions that change the microbiome and then change the outcome.
  - id: fibre-feeds
    prompt: What do gut bacteria produce when they ferment dietary fibre?
    options:
      - id: scfa
        text: Short-chain fatty acids such as butyrate, acetate and propionate
        correct: true
      - text: Vitamin C
      - text: Ethanol in dangerous amounts in everyone
    explanation: Butyrate is the main fuel for colon cells. This is one plausible mechanism for fibre's benefits.
  - id: tests
    prompt: What is the evidence grade for direct-to-consumer microbiome tests that recommend personalised diets?
    options:
      - text: Established
      - id: uncertain
        text: Uncertain at best — there is no agreed definition of a "healthy" microbiome, and test results vary between companies
        correct: true
      - text: Strong
    explanation: Research on personalised glucose responses (for example the Weizmann and PREDICT studies) is interesting, but consumer tests are ahead of the evidence.
  - id: leaky-gut
    prompt: Which statement about "leaky gut syndrome" is accurate?
    options:
      - text: It is a well-defined disease with a standard diagnostic test
      - id: not-diagnosis
        text: Increased intestinal permeability is a real, measurable phenomenon in some diseases, but "leaky gut syndrome" as a cause of a wide range of symptoms is not an established diagnosis
        correct: true
      - text: Intestinal permeability does not exist
    explanation: Products sold to "heal the leaky gut" generally lack trial evidence.
  - id: fermented
    prompt: In a 2021 Stanford trial, a diet high in fermented foods for 10 weeks compared with a high-fibre diet did what?
    options:
      - id: diversity
        text: Increased microbiome diversity and reduced several inflammatory markers
        correct: true
      - text: Caused weight gain of 10 kg
      - text: Had no measurable effect on anything
    explanation: It was a small (36 people) study. Interesting and plausible, but clinical outcomes were not measured. Grade - **Uncertain**.

## Learning objectives

- Separate established microbiome science from hype
- Understand the limitations of association studies
- Evaluate probiotic claims

## What is known

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Gut bacteria ferment fibre into short-chain fatty acids | **Established** |
| FMT cures recurrent C. difficile infection | **Established** |
| Antibiotics disrupt the microbiome | **Established** |
| Probiotics reduce antibiotic-associated diarrhoea | **Strong** (strain-dependent) |
| Microbiome differences cause obesity in humans | **Uncertain** |
| Fermented foods improve health outcomes | **Uncertain** |
| Probiotic supplements benefit healthy people | **Uncertain** |
| Consumer microbiome tests guide better diets | **Uncertain** to **Not supported** |
| Detox colon cleanses improve the microbiome | **Not supported** |
