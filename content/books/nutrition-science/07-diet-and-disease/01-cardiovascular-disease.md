## Learning objectives

- Identify dietary contributors to cardiovascular disease
- Understand why LDL causality is established
- Know which dietary interventions have trial support

## What diet changes

| Dietary change | Effect | Grade |
|---------------|--------|-------|
| Replace saturated with unsaturated fat | Lowers LDL and events | **Strong** |
| Eliminate industrial trans fat | Lowers events | **Established** |
| Reduce sodium; increase potassium | Lowers blood pressure and stroke | **Established** (BP), **Strong** (events) |
| Mediterranean pattern | Fewer events in PREDIMED, Lyon, CORDIOPREV | **Strong** |
| More fibre, whole grains, legumes, nuts | Lower LDL and risk | **Strong** |
| Weight loss in obesity | Lowers BP, LDL, glucose | **Established** for risk factors |
| Plant sterols/stanols 2 g/day | Lowers LDL about 10% | **Established** for LDL; outcomes not tested |
| Fish-oil supplements in healthy people | No event reduction | **Not supported** |
| Antioxidant supplements | No event reduction | **Not supported** |
| Garlic pills, turmeric, apple cider vinegar | Small or no effects on risk factors | **Uncertain** to **Not supported** |

## The role of medicine

Statins, PCSK9 inhibitors, ezetimibe, blood-pressure drugs and GLP-1 agonists reduce events in RCTs.
Diet is foundational, and it is not a substitute for medication when medication is indicated. Advising
people to stop proven drugs in favour of diet alone is dangerous.
