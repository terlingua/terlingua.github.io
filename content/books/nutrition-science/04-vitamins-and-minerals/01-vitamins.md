## Learning objectives

- Connect each vitamin to its deficiency disease and food sources
- Know who genuinely needs supplements
- Recognise toxicity limits

## History in brief

Nutrition science grew out of deficiency diseases: scurvy (vitamin C), beriberi (thiamin), pellagra (niacin),
rickets (vitamin D) and pernicious anaemia (B12). These are among the most **Established** findings in all of
medicine — each has a known molecule, mechanism, cure and prevention.

## The vitamins

| Vitamin | Main roles | Good sources | Deficiency |
|---------|-----------|--------------|------------|
| A (retinol; beta-carotene) | Vision, immunity, epithelia | Liver, dairy, orange and dark-green vegetables | Night blindness, xerophthalmia, infection deaths |
| D | Calcium absorption, bone | Sunlight on skin, oily fish, fortified foods | Rickets, osteomalacia |
| E | Antioxidant in membranes | Vegetable oils, nuts, seeds | Rare; neuropathy |
| K | Blood clotting, bone proteins | Leafy greens; K2 in fermented foods | Bleeding (especially newborns) |
| C | Collagen synthesis, antioxidant | Citrus, peppers, berries, potatoes | Scurvy |
| B1 thiamin | Carbohydrate metabolism | Whole grains, pork, legumes | Beriberi; Wernicke encephalopathy in alcohol use disorder |
| B2 riboflavin | Energy metabolism | Dairy, eggs, meat | Cracked lips, sore tongue |
| B3 niacin | Energy metabolism | Meat, fish, peanuts; made from tryptophan | Pellagra |
| B6 | Amino-acid metabolism | Poultry, fish, potatoes, bananas | Anaemia, neuropathy |
| Folate (B9) | DNA synthesis | Leafy greens, legumes, fortified grains | Megaloblastic anaemia, neural tube defects |
| B12 | DNA synthesis, nerve myelin | Animal foods, fortified foods | Anaemia, irreversible nerve damage |
| Biotin, pantothenic acid | Metabolism | Widespread | Very rare |

## Selected reference values (US, adults)

| Nutrient | RDA or AI | Tolerable upper limit (UL) |
|----------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Vitamin A | 700 µg RAE (women), 900 µg (men) | 3,000 µg preformed retinol |
| Vitamin D | 15 µg (600 IU); 20 µg (800 IU) over 70 | 100 µg (4,000 IU) |
| Vitamin C | 75 mg (women), 90 mg (men); +35 mg for smokers | 2,000 mg |
| Folate | 400 µg DFE; 600 µg in pregnancy | 1,000 µg from supplements and fortification |
| B12 | 2.4 µg | None set |
| B6 | 1.3–1.7 mg | 100 mg |
