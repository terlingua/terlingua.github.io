## Learning objectives

- Know the minerals of greatest public-health importance
- Understand salt reduction evidence
- Identify populations at risk of mineral deficiencies

## Major and trace minerals

| Mineral | Role | Sources | Notes |
|---------|------|---------|-------|
| Sodium | Fluid balance, nerve signals | Salt; mostly processed and restaurant food | Excess raises blood pressure |
| Potassium | Fluid balance, blood pressure | Vegetables, fruit, legumes, potatoes, dairy | Most people eat too little |
| Calcium | Bone, muscle, nerves | Dairy, fortified plant milks, tofu set with calcium, leafy greens | RDA 1,000 mg, 1,200 mg for women over 50 and men over 70 |
| Magnesium | Enzyme cofactor | Nuts, seeds, whole grains, legumes | Deficiency symptoms rare in healthy people |
| Iron | Oxygen transport | Meat, fish, legumes, fortified cereals | RDA 8 mg men; 18 mg women 19–50; 27 mg pregnancy |
| Zinc | Immunity, growth | Meat, shellfish, legumes, seeds | Deficiency stunts growth in children worldwide |
| Iodine | Thyroid hormones | Iodised salt, dairy, seafood | 150 µg adults; 220 µg pregnancy |
| Selenium | Antioxidant enzymes | Brazil nuts, seafood, grains | Narrow margin; a few Brazil nuts a day can exceed the UL |
| Fluoride | Tooth enamel | Fluoridated water, toothpaste | Prevents caries; excess causes fluorosis |

## Salt: what is known

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Reducing sodium lowers blood pressure | **Established** (DASH-Sodium and many trials) |
| High blood pressure causes stroke and heart disease | **Established** |
| Reducing sodium reduces cardiovascular events | **Strong** (SSaSS and modelling; long-term outcome trials are few) |
| Very low sodium (below about 1.5 g) is harmful | **Uncertain** — the J-shaped curves in some cohorts are likely affected by reverse causation and measurement error |
