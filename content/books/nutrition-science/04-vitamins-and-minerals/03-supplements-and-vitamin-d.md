## Learning objectives

- Know the landmark supplement trials
- Identify who benefits from specific supplements
- Understand how supplements are regulated

## The antioxidant story

Observational studies in the 1980s found people eating more fruit and vegetables — and with higher blood
antioxidant levels — had less cancer and heart disease. The hypothesis that antioxidants explained this was
tested in large RCTs:

| Trial | Supplement | Result |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| ATBC (1994) | Beta-carotene | 18% more lung cancer in smokers |
| CARET (1996) | Beta-carotene + vitamin A | 28% more lung cancer; stopped early |
| HOPE (2000) / HOPE-TOO | Vitamin E | No cardiovascular benefit; possible heart failure increase |
| SELECT (2011) | Vitamin E, selenium | 17% more prostate cancer with vitamin E |
| Physicians' Health Study II (2012) | Multivitamin | Small (8%) reduction in total cancer; no cardiovascular benefit |
| VITAL (2019) | Vitamin D3, omega-3 | No reduction in primary cancer or cardiovascular outcomes |
| B-vitamin homocysteine trials (2006 onwards) | Folate, B6, B12 | Lowered homocysteine, no reduction in heart attacks |

Lesson: **a biomarker or an association is not an outcome**.

## Vitamin D, specifically

- **Established:** vitamin D prevents and treats rickets and osteomalacia
- **Established:** supplementation is appropriate for people at risk of deficiency (dark skin at high latitudes,
  little sun exposure, covered skin, breastfed infants, older adults in care homes)
- **Not supported:** routine high-dose vitamin D to prevent cancer, heart disease, depression, or fractures in
  people who are not deficient
- **Uncertain:** effects on cancer mortality; benefits in specific subgroups; optimal blood level (the US
  National Academies consider 20 ng/mL, 50 nmol/L, sufficient for most people)
- Toxicity (hypercalcaemia) occurs with chronic very high doses, usually above 10,000 IU/day

## Supplements with real evidence for specific groups

| Supplement | Who | Grade |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Folic acid 400 µg | Anyone who could become pregnant | **Established** |
| B12 | Vegans; many older adults | **Established** |
| Iron | Diagnosed iron deficiency; often in pregnancy | **Established** |
| Vitamin D | Breastfed infants; people at risk of deficiency | **Established** |
| Iodine | Pregnancy and breastfeeding where diet is low (many guidelines suggest 150 µg) | **Strong** |
| Creatine | Athletes in strength and power sports | **Strong** |
| Calcium + vitamin D | Older adults in institutional care | **Strong** for fracture reduction in this group |
