## Learning objectives

- Distinguish fatty-acid types and their effects on blood lipids
- Understand the substitution principle
- Separate fish from fish-oil supplements

## Types of fat

| Type | Main sources | Effect on LDL when replacing carbohydrate |
|------|--------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Saturated (SFA) | Butter, cheese, fatty meat, palm and coconut oil | Raises |
| Monounsaturated (MUFA) | Olive oil, canola oil, avocados, nuts | Slightly lowers |
| Polyunsaturated omega-6 (PUFA) | Sunflower, soybean, corn oil, walnuts | Lowers |
| Polyunsaturated omega-3 | ALA in flax, chia, walnuts; EPA/DHA in oily fish | Neutral on LDL; EPA/DHA lower triglycerides |
| Industrial trans | Partially hydrogenated oils (now banned in many countries) | Raises LDL, lowers HDL |
| Ruminant trans | Small amounts in dairy and beef | Small effect at normal intakes |

## Targets

- Total fat: **20–35%** of energy (US AMDR); WHO says 30% or less to prevent unhealthy weight gain
- Saturated fat: **below 10%** of energy (WHO; US Dietary Guidelines, including 2025–2030)
- Trans fat: **below 1%** of energy (WHO), ideally industrial trans fat eliminated

## The evidence

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| LDL cholesterol causes atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease | **Established** |
| SFA raises LDL relative to unsaturated fats | **Established** |
| Replacing SFA with PUFA reduces cardiovascular events | **Strong** |
| Industrial trans fats increase heart disease | **Established** |
| Eating oily fish once or twice a week is associated with lower cardiac death | **Strong** |
| Fish-oil supplements prevent heart disease in healthy people | **Not supported** by the largest trials |
| Seed oils are toxic or inflammatory at normal intakes | **Not supported** |
| Dairy fat in cheese and yoghurt behaves differently from butter (the "food matrix") | **Uncertain** — intriguing, mostly observational |
| Butter and beef tallow are healthy cooking fats | **Not supported** — they are high in SFA and raise LDL compared with vegetable oils |

## A note on the 2025–2030 US guidelines

The 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans (January 2026) kept the under-10% saturated-fat limit while
featuring butter, beef tallow and full-fat dairy in their graphic. Researchers at Harvard and the Center for
Science in the Public Interest pointed out that following the food recommendations could exceed the
saturated-fat limit. Science does not change because a graphic changes.
