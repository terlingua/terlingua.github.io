## Learning objectives

- Distinguish sugars, starches and fibres
- Know the fibre evidence and targets
- Understand the limits of glycaemic index

## Types of carbohydrate

| Type | Examples | Digestion |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Sugars (mono- and disaccharides) | Glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose | Rapid |
| Starches | Grains, potatoes, legumes | Rapid to slow depending on structure |
| Resistant starch | Legumes, cooled cooked starch, green bananas | Fermented in the colon |
| Fibre | Cellulose, pectin, beta-glucan, inulin | Not digested by human enzymes; partly fermented by bacteria |

## Targets

- Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range (US National Academies): **45–65%** of energy from carbohydrate
- Fibre: **14 g per 1,000 kcal** (US Adequate Intake); **30 g/day** for adults (UK SACN)
- WHO (2023) recommends adults eat at least 25 g/day of naturally occurring fibre and at least 400 g/day of
  fruit and vegetables

Healthy populations have thrived on diets ranging from about 10% to 80% carbohydrate. The **quality**
of carbohydrate matters more than the amount for most chronic-disease outcomes.

## What is known

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Higher fibre intake is associated with lower mortality, heart disease, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer | **Strong** |
| Whole grains in place of refined grains reduce cardiometabolic risk | **Strong** |
| Viscous soluble fibre lowers LDL modestly | **Established** |
| Fibre improves constipation | **Established** for many fibres, notably psyllium |
| Carbohydrate itself causes obesity | **Not supported** |
| Low-GI diets add benefit beyond whole-food, high-fibre diets | **Uncertain** |
| "Net carbs" on labels are a regulated, meaningful metric | **Not supported** — the term has no FDA definition |
