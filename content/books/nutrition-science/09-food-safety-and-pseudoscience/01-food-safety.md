## Learning objectives

- Understand the scale of foodborne illness
- Apply the four core food-safety practices
- Identify high-risk foods and groups

## The four rules

| Rule | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| Clean | Wash hands, surfaces and produce (not raw meat) |
| Separate | Keep raw meat away from ready-to-eat foods |
| Cook | Use a thermometer to reach safe internal temperatures |
| Chill | Refrigerate at 4 °C (40 °F) or below within two hours |

## Major pathogens

| Pathogen | Typical sources | Notes |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| Norovirus | Ill food handlers, shellfish | Most common cause of foodborne illness |
| Salmonella | Poultry, eggs, produce | Leading cause of hospitalisation and death in the US |
| Campylobacter | Undercooked poultry, raw milk | Can trigger Guillain-Barré syndrome |
| Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (O157:H7) | Undercooked ground beef, raw milk, leafy greens | Haemolytic uraemic syndrome and kidney failure in children |
| Listeria | Deli meats, soft cheese, raw milk | Dangerous in pregnancy |
| Clostridium botulinum | Improper home canning; honey for infants | Paralysis |

## High-risk groups

Pregnant people, infants and young children, adults over 65 and people with weakened immune systems are
at greatest risk. Their food-safety precautions are not optional extras.
