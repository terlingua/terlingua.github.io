## Learning objectives

- Recognise and refute common nutrition myths
- Understand why myths persist

## Myths judged

| Claim | Grade | Note |
|-------|-------|------|
| Detox diets, teas and cleanses remove toxins | **Not supported** | The liver and kidneys do this; no "toxin" is ever named or measured |
| Alkaline diet changes blood pH and prevents cancer | **Not supported** | Fenton & Huang 2016 systematic review |
| MSG is dangerous | **Not supported** | GRAS (FDA); double-blind trials inconsistent |
| Gluten-free is healthier for everyone | **Not supported** | Essential only for coeliac disease and related conditions |
| Eating late makes you fat independent of intake | **Uncertain** to **Not supported** | |
| Carbohydrates at night are worse | **Uncertain** | |
| Frequent small meals "stoke metabolism" | **Not supported** | Thermic effect depends on total intake |
| Microwaving destroys nutrients more than other cooking | **Not supported** | Short times and little water often preserve nutrients better than boiling |
| "Negative-calorie" foods (celery) | **Not supported** | Digestion costs only a fraction of the calories |
| Colloidal silver, homeopathy, celery juice cure disease | **Not supported** | |
| Spicy food causes ulcers | **Not supported** | H. pylori and NSAIDs are the main causes |
| Egg yolks or all fat must be avoided | **Not supported** in that absolute form | |

## Why myths persist

- **Placebo, natural recovery and regression to the mean:** people try remedies when they feel worst, so
  they usually feel better afterwards regardless
- **Confirmation bias:** successes are remembered, failures forgotten
- **Commercial incentives:** an unfalsifiable product never fails a trial
- **Identity:** diets become tribal, political or religious identities, which makes evidence feel like an attack
