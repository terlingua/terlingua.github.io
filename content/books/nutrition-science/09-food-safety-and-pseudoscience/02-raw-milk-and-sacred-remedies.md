## Learning objectives

- Explain why pasteurisation is one of public health's great successes
- Evaluate raw milk claims against data
- Apply the same standards to sacred and traditional remedies as to any other claim

## Why milk was pasteurised

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, milk was a leading vehicle for tuberculosis (Mycobacterium bovis),
typhoid, scarlet fever, diphtheria, brucellosis and infant diarrhoea. In the US in 1938, milk-borne outbreaks
caused about a quarter of all reported food- and water-borne outbreaks; today the share is below 1% (FDA), and
most dairy outbreaks involve unpasteurised products.

## Raw milk claims judged

| Claim | Grade | Evidence |
|-------|-------|----------|
| Raw milk carries a much higher risk of infection | **Established** | Outbreak surveillance; Costard 2017 |
| Pasteurisation substantially reduces nutritional value | **Not supported** | Small losses of vitamins C, B1, B12; milk's protein, calcium and fat are unaffected |
| Raw milk prevents lactose intolerance | **Not supported** | Mummah et al., randomised crossover trial, 2014 |
| Raw milk enzymes aid digestion | **Not supported** | Milk enzymes are digested like other proteins |
| Farm milk exposure is associated with less childhood asthma | **Uncertain** | Association in farming communities; not shown for shop-bought raw milk; not a recommendation |
| Raw milk is safe if the farm is clean | **Not supported** | Outbreaks occur at certified and "clean" farms; contamination comes from udder infection and faeces |

Groups most harmed — children, pregnant people, the elderly and the immunocompromised — are often the ones
encouraged to drink raw milk "for health".

## Sacred and traditional animal products

Many cultures attach religious or traditional significance to particular animals and their products. Such
beliefs are a matter of faith and deserve respect as beliefs. **Medical claims are different**: when someone
says a substance cures cancer, diabetes or infection, that claim is testable and must meet the same standard as
a drug.

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Cow urine or panchagavya cures cancer, diabetes, COVID-19 or other diseases | **Not supported** |
| Cow dung applied to skin or wounds protects against infection | **Not supported**; dung can carry tetanus spores |
| A product is safe because it comes from a revered animal | **Not supported** — sanctity is not sterility |
| Ingesting any animal's urine is a risk for zoonotic infection | **Strong** (leptospirosis and other pathogens) |

The same reasoning applies to rhino horn (keratin, like fingernails), bear bile, and countless other
traditional animal remedies whose demand drives species toward extinction without demonstrated benefit.
