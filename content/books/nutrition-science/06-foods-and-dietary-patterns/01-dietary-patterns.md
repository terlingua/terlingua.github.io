## Learning objectives

- Describe the main evidence-based dietary patterns
- Recognise the trade-offs of restrictive patterns
- Identify the common core of healthy diets

## Why patterns

People eat foods in combinations, so studying whole patterns avoids some pitfalls of single-nutrient research.
Pattern trials are also more realistic about what people can do.

## Patterns compared

| Pattern | Core features | Best evidence | Grade |
|---------|--------------|---------------|-------|
| Mediterranean | Olive oil, vegetables, legumes, fish, nuts, whole grains, moderate dairy | PREDIMED, Lyon Diet Heart, CORDIOPREV | **Strong** for cardiovascular benefit |
| DASH | Vegetables, fruit, low-fat dairy, low sodium | DASH and DASH-Sodium feeding trials | **Established** for blood-pressure lowering |
| Vegetarian / vegan | No meat (and for vegans no animal products) | Cohorts (Adventist Health Study 2, EPIC-Oxford); lipid trials | **Strong** for lower LDL and heart disease; higher stroke and fracture risk seen in EPIC-Oxford needs confirmation |
| Low-carbohydrate | Carbohydrate below about 26% of energy | Weight and glycaemic trials | **Strong** for short-term glycaemic control; long-term equivalence for weight |
| Ketogenic | Carbohydrate below about 20–50 g/day | Epilepsy RCTs | **Established** for drug-resistant epilepsy |
| Carnivore | Animal foods only | None of quality | **Not supported** |
| Raw food | Uncooked foods only | Small studies | **Not supported**; cooking improves digestibility and food safety |
| Paleo | "Pre-agricultural" foods | Small, short trials | **Uncertain**; real Palaeolithic diets varied enormously |

## The shared core

Every pattern with good outcome evidence emphasises minimally processed plants and limits processed meat,
sugary drinks and refined starches. Arguments over the margins — olive oil versus seed oil, dairy or no dairy,
fish or no fish — matter much less than this core.
