## Learning objectives

- Quantify the evidence for fruit and vegetables
- Address common fears about soy and lectins
- Recognise "superfood" marketing

## Evidence summary

| Food group | Main associations | Grade |
|-----------|------------------|-------|
| Vegetables and fruit | Lower cardiovascular disease and mortality | **Strong** |
| Whole grains | Lower heart disease, type 2 diabetes, colorectal cancer | **Strong** |
| Legumes | Lower LDL; associated with lower heart disease | **Strong** for LDL; cohort data moderate |
| Nuts | Lower cardiovascular disease | **Strong** |
| Soy foods | Lower LDL modestly; neutral or protective for breast cancer | **Strong** |
| Potatoes | Neutral when boiled or baked; fried potatoes associated with harm | **Uncertain** |
| Fruit juice | Neutral to modestly adverse | **Uncertain** |

## Fears that are not supported

- **Lectins and "anti-nutrients":** phytates and oxalates reduce some mineral absorption, but whole-food
  diets rich in legumes and grains are associated with better health. Cooking, soaking and fermenting reduce
  them. Those prone to calcium-oxalate kidney stones may need medical advice on oxalate
- **Nightshades and inflammation:** no good evidence that tomatoes, peppers or aubergines cause inflammation in
  people without a specific allergy
- **Fruit sugar:** whole fruit is not linked to obesity or diabetes
