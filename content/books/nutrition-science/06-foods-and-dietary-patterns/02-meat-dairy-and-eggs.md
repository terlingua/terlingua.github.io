## Learning objectives

- Interpret the IARC meat classifications
- Understand why scientists disagree about meat recommendations
- Weigh evidence on eggs and dairy

## Meat

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Processed meat increases colorectal cancer risk | **Strong** to **Established** (IARC Group 1) |
| Red meat increases colorectal cancer risk | **Strong** (IARC 2A, "probably") |
| Processed meat is associated with higher heart disease and diabetes risk | **Strong** (observational, consistent) |
| Unprocessed red meat increases heart disease risk | **Uncertain** to **Strong** — associations are weaker and more variable |
| The absolute risk increase is small for any one person | **Established** — but it applies across hundreds of millions of people |
| Meat is necessary for health | **Not supported** — well-planned vegetarian diets are adequate |
| Lean meat provides high-quality protein, iron, zinc and B12 | **Established** |

The World Cancer Research Fund recommends eating little, if any, processed meat and no more than about
350–500 g (cooked) of red meat per week.

## Dairy

| Claim | Grade |
|-------|-------|
| Dairy is a good source of calcium, protein, iodine, B12 | **Established** |
| Dairy probably lowers colorectal cancer risk | **Strong** (WCRF "probable") |
| Fermented dairy (yoghurt) is associated with lower type 2 diabetes risk | **Uncertain** to **Strong** |
| Full-fat dairy is healthier than low-fat | **Uncertain** — cohort data do not show clear harm from full-fat cheese and yoghurt; butter raises LDL |
| Milk causes mucus production | **Not supported** — trials found no increase in measured mucus |
| Raw milk is healthier than pasteurised | **Not supported** (see the food-safety chapter) |

## Eggs

One large egg contains about 186 mg cholesterol, 6 g protein and useful choline, lutein and vitamin D.
Grade for moderate egg intake causing heart disease in healthy people: **Uncertain**.
