## Learning objectives

- Define radical moral communitarian criminology and its Durkheimian basis
- Explain how it differs from Etzioni's communitarianism and New Labour's version
- Explain the negotiated balance of rights and responsibilities at macro and micro levels
- State the policy implications

## The ideas

**Definition.** "It is thus the work of Emile Durkheim and his observations on the moral component of the division of labour in society that provide the theoretical basis of radical moral communitarianism, which actively promotes the rights and responsibilities of both individuals and communities but in the context of an equal division of labour" (p. 456). The glossary: "a political philosophy which promotes the rights and responsibilities of both individuals and communities but in the context of an equal division of labour" (p. 507).

**Difference from Etzioni.** The equal division of labour "deviates significantly from the orthodoxy promoted by Amitai Etzioni - and which has been embraced and distorted in the UK by New Labour with its enthusiasm for a strong dictatorial central state apparatus to enforce its agenda" (p. 453). Etzioni's variant "overemphasizes responsibilities to the detriment of rights and thus calls for a rebalancing of the two". The aim is "a genuine moral communitarian way of life founded on notions of consensual interdependency with others we all recognize and identify as fellow citizens and social partners, and not as potential legitimate crime targets".

**Negotiated balance.** "Individuals have rights and responsibilities and, at the same time, so do communities, and it is important to maintain a negotiated balance between the two at both a macro level and micro level in society" (p. 453). "Policies should be introduced that recognize that people and communities have both rights and responsibilities and that there is a fine balance between them. This balance will invariably require negotiation and renegotiation" (p. 456).

**Basis in research.** Hopkins Burke was developing the concept, influenced by his study with Phil Hodgson on repeat antisocial behaviour in a large Midlands city (Chapter 18).

**Policy implications** (pp. 453–5). Table 22.1 summarizes basic rights citizens should enjoy alongside simultaneous responsibilities "in a moral communitarian society built on mutual trust and respect" (Hopkins Burke and Hodgson, 2013). Six paired rows (p. 454):

| Rights | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| An adequate income appropriate to one's stage of life | Play an active role in the economy while fit, healthy and of working age |
| Good-quality affordable housing of acceptable size with proper tenure | Be a good neighbour and not engage in antisocial behaviour |
| Fair and respectful treatment by all agencies, institutions and individuals regardless of age, disability, ethnicity, gender and religion | Treat others with fairness and respect on the same terms |
| Good-quality healthcare | Maintain a reasonable standard of health where possible |
| High-quality education and training | Engage fully in education and training and behave appropriately |
| Protection from crime and antisocial behaviour | Not engage in crime |

The text adds detail: rents linked to ability to pay, an end to the stigmatization of social housing, eviction to special accommodation for tenants who engage in antisocial behaviour, and appropriate sanctions for failing to treat others with respect (pp. 454–5). Crime reduction follows from reducing the inequality of a forced division of labour, integrating people into inclusive networks, and negotiating rather than imposing obligations. It links to **democratic criminology** (Chapter 24): root democracy and participation.

## Software lens

Radical moral communitarianism describes a healthy engineering culture: everyone has rights (to psychological safety, fair review, a say in decisions, reasonable on-call load) and responsibilities (code review, documentation, honest incident reporting, upholding norms), negotiated and renegotiated at org level (policies) and team level (working agreements). Etzioni-style imbalance shows up when organizations demand ownership, availability and "culture fit" while eroding rights; New Labour-style distortion shows up when "community values" are enforced top-down by management. The equal division of labour is the crux: if the glue work, on-call and maintenance fall on a subset while others get the visible, rewarded work, the community is forced, not moral.
