## Learning objectives

- Explain why W.I. Thomas marks a transition and describe his four wishes
- Explain Freud's 'anatomy is destiny' and its influence on Davis, Pollak and Konopka
- Describe Davis's functionalist account of prostitution
- Explain Pollak's 'hidden female crime' thesis and the chivalry hypothesis
- Explain how women who breach gender norms may be subjected to increased sanctions

## The ideas

**W.I. Thomas** marks "a transition from purely biological explanations of female criminality to a more sophisticated variant that embraces physiological, psychological and even sociological factors" (p. 182) — yet still rests on Lombrosian assumptions. Thomas (1907) attributed to men high sexual energy and to women maternal feelings, leading women to exchange sex for domesticity. Because women are marginal to production, they are no threat to commerce and are treated leniently in property cases, but rigorously prosecuted for sexual conduct (p. 170). In *The Unadjusted Girl* (1923) he named four fundamental **wishes** — new experience, security, response and recognition — and saw delinquent girls as using sex to satisfy them, calling for moral re-education rather than economic change.

**Freud.** His theory of women "is grounded in explicit biological assumptions about their nature, which is expressed in his famous dictum 'anatomy is destiny'" (p. 182): women are "anatomically inferior to men with a consequential inferior destiny as wives and mothers" (glossary, p. 502). Deviant women are maladjusted, seeking to be men (penis envy), rather than responding to social conditions.

**Freud's influence.** Kingsley Davis (1937), Otto Pollak (1950) and Gisela Konopka (1966) "used concepts of sexual maladjustment and neurosis to explain the criminality of women", defining healthy women as "masochistic, passive and sexually indifferent", criminal women as "sexual misfits", and ignoring socio-economic factors (p. 172).

**Davis on prostitution.** A structural functionalist account founded on assumptions about the "organic nature of man and woman". Prostitution is universal and functional — it meets male sexual demand that marriage cannot, protecting the family. The difference between wives and prostitutes is between legal and illegal roles; individually, prostitutes are maladjusted and neurotic. The book notes this implies society needs "a perpetually ill and maladjusted class of women", which Davis does not question (p. 174).

**Pollak's hidden crime.** Pollak argued official female crime rates are unreasonably low because female crime is **hidden**. Three factors (pp. 174–5): women are **inherently deceitful** — for physiological reasons (they can fake sexual response; they are socially required to conceal menstruation); women's **roles** as domestics, nurses, teachers and housewives give opportunities for undetectable crime (and women instigate crimes men carry out); and **chivalry** — "Men hate to accuse women... police officers dislike to arrest them, district attorneys to prosecute them, judges and juries to find them guilty". Women's crimes are personalized products of mental illness (kleptomania, false accusation from spite or neurosis); economic conditions are "virtually inconsequential".

**Breaching gender norms.** Criminal women are explained as having breached the dominant definition of female behaviour, and "some claim that this deviation from the norm justifies subjecting them to increasing sanctions" (p. 182; Carlen, 1983). Most studies find no sentencing bias, but Kennedy (1992) found young female offenders subjected to welfare interventions "considerably more invasive of their private lives than any applied to young men", treated "more as sexual miscreants than criminals" (p. 176). Clinical and sexual explanations are accepted for women's crimes even without a sexual basis, but rejected for men's even when there is one.

## Software lens

Pollak's chivalry hypothesis and the "breach of gender norms" thesis together show that the same group can be treated more leniently in one dimension and more harshly in another. Organizations see this too: women engineers may receive softer feedback on technical errors (benevolent sexism that withholds useful criticism) and harsher sanctions for behaviour that breaks gender expectations, such as assertiveness labelled "abrasive". Davis's functionalism is a cautionary example of an explanation that justifies an arrangement by its function for the powerful while pathologizing those who bear its costs — compare explanations of crunch culture that call it "necessary" while treating burned-out engineers as weak.
