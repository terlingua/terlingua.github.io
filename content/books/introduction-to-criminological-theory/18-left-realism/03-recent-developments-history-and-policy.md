## Learning objectives

- Explain John Lea's argument that New Labour absorbed left realism at the expense of the democratic imperative
- Explain Hopkins Burke's historical use of left realism and the left realist hybrid model
- State Lea and Young's three policy implications
- Evaluate situational crime prevention from a left realist perspective

## The ideas

**Lea's critique.** If left realism had really been influential after 1997, the integrative potential of the square of crime should have been pre-eminent. Instead, "the extent to which left realism was absorbed into the 'New' Labour consensus was at the expense of the democratic imperative, which had been essential to the original formulation of the theoretical perspective" (p. 362). The original project aimed "to defend criminal justice by making it democratically accountable, not to turn it into a device for the authoritarian regulation" of the poor (p. 360). Matthews (2009) calls for analysis that examines changing crime "by incorporating all four dimensions" of the square (p. 357). New Labour's ASBOs — "statutory measures that aim to protect the public from behaviour that causes or is likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress" (glossary, p. 502) — exemplify control rather than democratic accountability for critics.

**Left realism in history.** "Hopkins Burke (2012, 2013) has adapted and adopted left realist theory in an historical context as a significant component of his criminal justice theory, which seeks to explain the development of the criminal justice system in modern societies and in whose interest this has all occurred" (p. 362). The **left realist hybrid model** synthesizes the social progress, radical conflict and carceral surveillance models "with the additional significant recognition of the interest and collusion of us - the general public - in the creation of the increasingly pervasive socio-control matrix of the carceral society" (p. 361; developed in Chapter 23). Since the start of modernity, the public has had a material interest in order and has supported controls that also discipline the poor.

**Situational crime prevention revisited.** "While there remains a population of potentially determined and available criminals, there will continue to be an issue of crime displacement... locking all doors and bolting all windows is invariably a good idea but it is no universal panacea... Situational crime prevention is thus a sensible but incomplete crime control strategy" (p. 351) — exactly why the balance of intervention is needed.

**Policy implications.** Lea and Young's three policies (1984), "as relevant today as they were thirty years ago" (p. 361):

1. **De-marginalization** — legitimate alternatives to prison: community service orders, victim restitution, widespread release from prison — to avoid "the severance of the moral bond with the community"; institutions that control crime "must epitomize justice". Lea later suggests diversion conditional on participation in de-marginalization projects (work, collective community activity) rather than on admission of guilt (p. 360).
2. **Pre-emptive deterrence** — environmental and public precautions dismissed by left idealists as distractions are vital; "the organization of communities in an attempt to pre-empt crime is of the utmost importance".
3. **The minimal use of prison** — only where there is extreme danger to the community; life inside "as free and as 'normal' as possible".

## Software lens

Lea's critique — a framework absorbed by power "at the expense of the democratic imperative" — is how good engineering ideas get hollowed out. "Blameless postmortems" adopted as a slogan while leadership still uses them to assign blame; "zero trust" adopted as surveillance of employees rather than as better architecture. The three policies translate directly: **de-marginalization** (keep people who violate rules inside the community with restitution — fix what you broke — instead of expelling them); **pre-emptive deterrence** (invest in community-level prevention: secure defaults, peer review, threat modelling); **minimal use of prison** (account bans and dismissals only where danger is extreme, and keep any restriction as "normal" as possible).
