## Learning objectives

- Explain 'tough on crime, tough on the causes of crime' and its links to the three models
- Define reintegrative tutelage and its communitarian context
- Distinguish the behavioural, structural and process models of the underclass
- Explain Young's critique of the New Labour project

## The ideas

**'Tough on crime, tough on the causes of crime'.** Left realism "was extremely influential with the 'New' Labour Government elected in 1997" (p. 362), summarized by Tony Blair's sound bite as Shadow Home Secretary. "Being 'tough on crime' suggests that offenders should take responsibility for their actions and is in theoretical accordance with the prescriptions of the rational actor model. Taking a tough stance on the causes of crime suggests a targeting of both those individual and" structural factors that predispose people to offend — the predestined actor model — while recognizing the impact of social exclusion — the victimized actor model (p. 351).

**Reintegrative tutelage.** New Labour sought to reintegrate the socially excluded 'underclass' into mainstream society as a major government project that Hopkins Burke terms **'reintegrative tutelage'** (1999, 2008), incorporating insights from all three models — most visibly in youth justice reforms (p. 354). The glossary: "New Labour social policy strategy to reintegrate those sections of society socially excluded from mainstream society" (p. 508). It "could be situated in the context of a government commitment to the socio-political notion of communitarianism" (p. 362), emphasizing informal communal bonds and networks for maintaining social order (Chapter 22).

**The underclass: three models** (pp. 352–3):

- **Behavioural model** (Wilson and Herrnstein; Murray; Herrnstein and Murray) — associated with populist conservatives, rooted in the rational actor and biological predestined models: "state welfare erodes individual responsibility by giving people incentives not to work and provide for themselves and their family" (glossary, p. 503).
- **Structural model** — associated with the liberal left: "the collapse of manufacturing industry, traditional working-class employment and the subsequent retreat of welfare provision" provide "the structural preconditions for the creation of a socially excluded class" (glossary, p. 509).
- **Process model** (Hopkins Burke, 1999) — a left realist synthesis that "both locates the structural preconditions for the emergence of this social grouping, while at the same time examining the nature of their behavioural response to their found predicament" (p. 353): collapse of the economic basis of existence (structure) leads to changes in family living arrangements and subcultural strategies (behaviour). The glossary: identify "the structural preconditions... while at the same time considering and responding to the behavioural subcultural strategies" (p. 507).

**Young's critique.** "Jock Young has nevertheless questioned the capacity of the 'New' Labour reintegrative tutelage project to successfully tackle a crime problem that it so clearly identified with what is a difficult to empirically isolate socially excluded minority population" (p. 362). The 'underclass' is hard to identify empirically; focusing on it neglects broader insecurity, inequality and the crimes of the included.

## Software lens

The underclass models map onto how organizations explain chronically "problem" teams or user segments. The **behavioural** explanation says they lack responsibility and are enabled by lenient policy ("we're too soft on them"). The **structural** explanation says they were handed collapsing systems and starved of resources. The **process** model asks both: what structural conditions created the situation, and what coping strategies (workarounds, shadow IT, defensive cultures) did people develop in response that now need addressing? Reintegrative tutelage is a familiar programme shape: bring an excluded group back into the mainstream with support plus supervision. Young's critique is a warning about targeting a vaguely defined "problem population" rather than the broader conditions that affect everyone.
