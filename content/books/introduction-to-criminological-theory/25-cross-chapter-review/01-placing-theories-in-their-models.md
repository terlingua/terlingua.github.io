## Learning objectives

- Assign each major theory to the rational actor, predestined actor or victimized actor model, or to integrated or age-of-uncertainty perspectives
- Recognize theories that straddle models and explain why
- Identify the policy logic that follows from each model

## The ideas

This chapter is not in the book. It tests whether you can hold the whole map in your head. Use this summary table as a reference; the questions then probe the edges.

| Model | Core claim | Typical theories | Policy logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rational actor | People choose crime; they can be deterred | Classical (Beccaria, Bentham), neo-Classical, right realism (Wilson), deterrence, rational choice (Cornish and Clarke), routine activities (Cohen and Felson) | Deterrence, incapacitation, situational prevention |
| Predestined actor | Crime is caused by factors beyond the person's control | Biological (Lombroso, twins, XYY, biochemistry), psychological (Freud, Bowlby, Eysenck, Sutherland, Akers), sociological (Durkheim, Chicago School, Merton, subcultures) | Treat the individual or transform the environment |
| Victimized actor | The poor and powerless are criminalized by the powerful; choices are constrained | Labelling (Becker, Lemert), conflict (Sellin, Vold, Turk, Quinney), radical (Chambliss, New Criminology), feminist, critical, peacemaking | Decriminalize, reduce inequality, restrain enforcement, restorative justice |
| Integrated | Deliberately combine elements across models | Biosocial, environmental, control theories (Hirschi, Braithwaite, Gottfredson and Hirschi, Tittle), SAT, desistance, left realism | Multi-level interventions |
| Age of moral uncertainty | Modernist grand theory is in doubt | Constitutive, anarchist, cultural, risk society, communitarian, penal society, democratic criminology | Varied: de-invest from oppression, appreciate meaning, rebalance rights and responsibilities |

Boundary cases recur: Matza (drift) bridges positivism and choice; cognitive learning theories edge toward the rational actor; left realism uses all three models; control theories have roots in both rational and predestined models; the CCCS moved subculture theory into the victimized actor model.

## Software lens

Before you can critique an explanation in a postmortem, a policy memo or a vendor pitch, you need to know which model it is using. "The attacker chose an easy target" is rational actor; "the engineer was burned out and inexperienced" is predestined actor; "the rule was written by and for another team and only enforced against contractors" is victimized actor; "all of these interacted" is integrated.
