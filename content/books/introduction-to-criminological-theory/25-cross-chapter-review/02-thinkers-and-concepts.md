## Learning objectives

- Match key thinkers to their central concepts across the book
- Distinguish frequently confused pairs (two anomies, two realisms, labelling versus conflict, guardians versus handlers)
- Use concepts precisely

## The ideas

A quick reference for frequently confused pairs:

- **Durkheim's anomie** (normlessness during rapid change) vs **Merton's anomie** (structural gap between goals and means).
- **Right realism** (Wilson: crime is evil; certainty, incapacitation, order maintenance) vs **left realism** (Lea and Young: take crime seriously; balance of intervention; square of crime).
- **Labelling** (micro: how reaction shapes deviant identity) vs **conflict/radical** (macro: who makes the law and in whose interests).
- **Capable guardian** (protects the target) vs **intimate handler** (restrains the offender) — Felson.
- **Reintegrative** vs **stigmatizing shaming** — Braithwaite.
- **Primary** vs **secondary deviance** — Lemert.
- **Old penology** (individual guilt and treatment) vs **new penology** (managing risk groups) — Feeley and Simon.
- **Ontogenetic** vs **sociogenic** desistance — Maruna's dichotomy; **condemnation** vs **redemption** scripts.
- **Technocratic**, **genealogical** and **democratic** public criminology — Fichtelberg and Kupchik.

## Software lens

Precision with concepts prevents category errors in design debates — for example proposing more monitoring (guardianship) when the real gap is an absent handler (no manager who knows the person), or responding to secondary deviance (a disengaged, labelled engineer) with more deterrence.
