## Learning objectives

- Apply the kiddie tax tiers and ages
- Weigh custodial accounts against financial aid, control and gifting rules
- Choose sensible investments for education money

## The ideas

**Kiddie tax** (p. 556): for 2025, a child's first $1,350 of unearned income is tax-free, the next $1,350 is taxed at the child's rate, and anything above $2,700 at the parents' rate. Applies to children under 18 at year-end; 18-year-olds and full-time students 19–23 whose earned income doesn't exceed half their support (Chapter 17).

**Custodial accounts (UGMA/UTMA)** (pp. 556–557): irrevocable gifts; the child gets control at 18 or 21 (state law); assessed heavily for aid. Only for families who'll pay full freight and accept the control issue. Keep a child's unearned income under $2,700. Parents can instead hold investments and gift them to an adult child in a lower bracket (annual exclusion $19,000 per donor in 2025–2026), being careful that the kiddie tax runs to age 23 for students.

**Investments for education** (pp. 557–558): low-cost stock and bond funds or ETFs (age-based glide paths in 529s); skip zero-coupon Treasuries in taxable accounts (phantom interest taxed annually), cash-value life insurance and low-yield savings.
