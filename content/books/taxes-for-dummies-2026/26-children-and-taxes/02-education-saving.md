## Learning objectives

- Understand how retirement versus non-retirement assets affect aid
- Use 529 plans (with OBBBA's K-12 change and front-loading gifts) and 529-to-Roth rollovers
- Know Coverdell ESA limits and savings bond rules

## The ideas

**Financial aid** (pp. 549–550): aid formulas ignore retirement accounts but count non-retirement savings, especially in the child's name (assessed far more heavily than parent assets). Prioritising retirement contributions saves tax and can raise aid (the book's Kent-and-Marian versus Rick-and-Liz story). Since the simplified FAFSA (2024–25), grandparent-owned 529s and cash support from grandparents are no longer reported.

**529 plans** (pp. 550–552): after-tax contributions grow tax-free and come out tax-free for qualified expenses — college tuition, fees, books, room and board (at least half-time), plus up to $10,000 a year of K-12 tuition (*update:* OBBBA raises the K-12 limit to **$20,000 from 2026** and broadens K-12 expenses beyond tuition; the book doesn't mention the cap), apprenticeships and up to $10,000 lifetime of student loan repayment. Nonqualified withdrawals: earnings taxed plus 10%. **Front-loading**: up to five years of annual gift exclusions at once ($95,000 per donor for 2025, $190,000 per couple) with a gift tax return election. Many states give deductions for in-state plans.

**529-to-Roth** (p. 552): up to $35,000 lifetime to the beneficiary's Roth IRA, if the 529 has been open 15+ years, excluding contributions (and earnings) from the last 5 years, within the annual IRA limit and the beneficiary's earned income.

**Coverdell ESAs** (pp. 552–553): $2,000 per beneficiary per year until 18; contributor income limits ($190,000–$220,000 joint; $95,000–$110,000 single) — a grandparent or the child can contribute instead; K-12 and college expenses; must be used or rolled to a family member by 30.

**Savings bonds** (pp. 555–556): EE and I bond interest can be excluded (Form 8815) if used for higher education by owners meeting the income test in the year of redemption, bonds owned by the parent (not the child), and the owner was 24+ when issued. Don't elect to report interest annually if you plan to use the exclusion.
