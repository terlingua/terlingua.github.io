## Learning objectives

- Recall AOTC and LLC rules (with corrections on indexing and the expired deduction)
- Coordinate credits with 529 and ESA withdrawals
- Apply the student loan interest deduction
- Choose sensible ways to fund college (with a correction on home equity)

## The ideas

**AOTC** (p. 553): 100% of the first $2,000 + 25% of the next $2,000 = $2,500 per student for the first four years, 40% refundable. **LLC**: 20% of up to $10,000 per return. Both phase out at MAGI $80,000–$90,000 single and $160,000–$180,000 joint. *Corrections:* these ranges are **not** indexed for inflation (fixed since 2021), contrary to the book (p. 553), and the "tuition and fees deduction" the book mentions (p. 553) expired after 2020.

**No double-dipping**: expenses used for a credit can't also be paid tax-free from a 529 or ESA; plan withdrawals so that $4,000 of tuition is paid from other money (to claim the full AOTC) and the rest from the 529.

**Student loan interest** (pp. 549–550): up to $2,500 above the line; 2025 phase-out $85,000–$100,000 single, $170,000–$200,000 joint.

**Paying for college** (pp. 554–555): don't aim to pay it all unless affluent; apply for aid anyway (unsubsidized and PLUS loans aren't need-based); save in your name, not the child's; have kids work. *Correction:* the book suggests home equity borrowing because "the interest is generally tax-deductible" (p. 555); home equity interest is deductible only if the money improves the home (Chapter 25), so a home equity loan for tuition generally gives no deduction (a qualified student loan might). 401(k) loans must be repaid or become taxable distributions.
