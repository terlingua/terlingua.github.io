## Learning objectives

- Know the SSN requirement and why personal exemptions no longer help
- Apply the dependent care credit and account rules (with a correction)
- Weigh a second income after taxes and childcare costs
- Know the new Trump accounts for children

## The ideas

**SSNs** (p. 546): needed for the child tax credit (OBBBA also requires a parent's SSN), EITC and dependency claims. *Correction:* the book mentions an "extra personal deduction that you can take with each new child" (p. 546); personal exemptions have been zero since 2018 and OBBBA repealed them permanently — the child benefit now comes through credits.

**Dependent care credit** (pp. 546–547; Chapter 16): 20–35% of up to $3,000/$6,000 of care expenses so you can work; household services like a housekeeper count if partly for the qualifying person's care. *Correction:* the book says the credit begins to phase down at $125,000 AGI and disappears at $438,000; those were 2021-only rules. For 2025 the rate falls from 35% to a 20% floor by AGI $43,000 and never phases out; from 2026 OBBBA raises the top rate to 50% and adds a second step-down above $75,000 ($150,000 joint).

**Dependent care FSA** (p. 547): up to $5,000 for 2025 ($7,500 from 2026), saving income tax and FICA; use-it-or-lose-it unless the plan has a grace period; reduces the credit dollar for dollar.

**Child tax credit** $2,200 (refundable up to $1,700) and **adoption credit** $17,280 with up to $5,000 refundable (p. 548).

**Second income** (p. 548): the second earner's pay is taxed at the couple's marginal rate plus FICA and state tax, and childcare, commuting and work costs reduce the net. Compare honestly, remembering non-financial benefits and career continuity.

**Trump accounts** (new, OBBBA): a tax-advantaged account for children under 18, opened with Form 4547. The Treasury makes a one-time $1,000 deposit for U.S.-citizen children born from 2025 through 2028. Contributions began July 4, 2026, up to $5,000 a year (indexed) from parents and others, and employers can contribute up to $2,500 a year tax-free. Funds must be invested in low-cost U.S. stock index funds and generally can't be withdrawn before 18; afterwards the account follows traditional IRA rules.
