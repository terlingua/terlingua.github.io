## Learning objectives

- Compute the saver's credit and apply the distribution lookback
- Apply the residential clean energy and energy efficient home improvement credits and their end dates
- Know that the clean vehicle credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025

## The ideas

**Saver's credit** (Form 8880; p. 176). For 2025, AGI under $79,000 joint, $59,250 HOH or $39,500 others. The credit is 50%, 20% or 10% (depending on AGI) of up to $2,000 of retirement contributions per person, so at most $1,000 per person ($2,000 per couple). You can't be a full-time student or someone's dependent, and you must be 18. Contributions are reduced by retirement distributions received during a lookback period: the two preceding years, the tax year and up to the filing due date (for 2025: from January 1, 2023; the book says 2022). From 2027, SECURE 2.0 replaces it with a government matching contribution (the "saver's match").

**Residential clean energy credit (section 25D)**: 30% of the cost of solar electric, solar water heating, wind, geothermal heat pumps, fuel cells and battery storage installed on a US home you live in (main or second home, not a rental). No dollar cap (fuel cells aside). Unused credit carries forward. OBBBA ended it for expenditures made after December 31, 2025 (p. 177). Example: $30,000 solar → $9,000 credit; the home's basis rises by $30,000 − $9,000 = $21,000.

**Energy efficient home improvement credit (section 25C)**: 30% of costs, capped **per year** at $1,200 (with sub-limits: $600 for windows and skylights, $250 per exterior door and $500 for all doors, $600 for qualifying central air, water heaters, furnaces or panel upgrades, $150 for a home energy audit), plus a separate $2,000 for heat pumps, heat pump water heaters and biomass stoves — $3,200 a year in total. It applies only to an existing home that is your **main** residence, and it has **no carryforward**. It ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. *Corrections:* the book calls the $3,200 a “total cumulative credit” (it's annual), says both energy credits apply to vacation homes, and says unused amounts of both carry over (pp. 177).

**Clean vehicle credits** (Form 8936; p. 178): up to $7,500 for a new EV (price caps $55,000/$80,000; MAGI $150,000/$225,000/$300,000) and up to $4,000 for a used EV, ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025 (a binding contract and payment by that date counts even if delivery came later). The book's statement that “a new credit will be introduced for 2026” requiring North American sourcing is not correct: OBBBA terminated the credits, and no replacement credit was enacted. (The commercial clean vehicle credit and the EV charger credit also ended, the charger credit for property placed in service after June 30, 2026.)
