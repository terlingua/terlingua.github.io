## Learning objectives

- Distinguish credits from deductions and refundable from nonrefundable credits
- Decide between the foreign tax credit and deduction, and use the simplified election

## The ideas

**Credits beat deductions.** A deduction reduces taxable income, so it's worth your marginal rate; a credit reduces tax dollar for dollar. “A $1,000 tax deduction reduces the tax for someone in the 24 percent federal income tax bracket by only $240. A $1,000 tax credit reduces that same person's federal income tax by $1,000” (p. 172).

**Nonrefundable vs refundable.** Nonrefundable credits (Schedule 3 Part I) can reduce tax to zero but not below; some unused amounts carry forward. Refundable credits (EIC, additional child tax credit, 40% of the AOTC, net premium tax credit) can produce a refund beyond tax paid (Part II and Form 1040 lines 27–31) (pp. 172, 179).

**Foreign tax credit** (Form 1116; p. 173). Foreign taxes on income the US also taxes can be taken as an itemized deduction or a credit (generally better). Investors in international funds often have small amounts on 1099-DIV box 7. **Simplified election**: if all foreign income is passive (dividends, interest and the like), reported on 1099s, and foreign tax is $300 or less ($600 joint), you can claim it directly on Schedule 3 line 1 without Form 1116. Above that, Form 1116's limitation (foreign income ÷ total income × US tax) applies, with carrybacks and carryforwards. You can't take both a credit and a deduction for the same year's taxes.
