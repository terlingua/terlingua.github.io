- id: excess-ss
  prompt: In 2025 Lee worked for two employers. Employer A withheld $7,000 of Social Security tax and employer B withheld $5,000. What happens?
  explanation: Each employer withholds 6.2% up to the wage base without knowing about the other, so two jobs can over-withhold. The 2025 maximum is 6.2% × $176,100 = $10,918 (p. 181). Total withheld $12,000 − $10,918 = $1,082 is credited on the return. If a single employer withheld more than the maximum, you get it back from that employer, not on your return. (There is no wage base for Medicare, so no excess Medicare arises.)
  options:
  - text: Lee claims the $1,082 excess over the $10,918 maximum as a credit on Schedule 3 line 11
    correct: true
  - text: Lee owes more Social Security tax
  - text: Nothing; the extra is lost
  - text: Employer B must refund $5,000

- id: prior-year-amt
  prompt: You paid AMT in 2024 because you exercised and held ISOs. In 2025 you're not subject to AMT. What may help you?

AMT from deferral items (ISOs, depreciation) generates the credit; AMT from exclusion items (SALT, standard deduction) does not. The credit carries forward indefinitely and is limited each year to the excess of regular tax over tentative minimum tax. Selling ISO shares typically creates the room to use it.

## Learning objectives

- Compute the credit for the elderly or disabled correctly
- Understand mortgage credit certificates and the prior-year minimum tax credit
- Compute excess Social Security withholding and other Part II payments

## The ideas

**Credit for the elderly or disabled** (Schedule R; p. 178). For people 65+ or permanently and totally disabled with low income. The *initial amount* is $5,000 (single, or joint with one qualifying spouse), $7,500 (joint, both qualifying) or $3,750 (MFS), reduced by nontaxable Social Security and pensions and by half of AGI above $7,500 / $10,000 / $5,000. The credit is **15%** of what remains, so at most $750 / $1,125. *Correction:* the book says the credit “can amount to as much as $7,500” (p. 178); $7,500 is the starting base, not the credit.

**Mortgage credit certificate** (Form 8396): a state or local program credit of 10%–50% of mortgage interest; above 20% the credit is capped at $2,000 a year; reduce your mortgage interest deduction by the credit (p. 178).

**Prior-year minimum tax credit** (Form 8801): recovers AMT caused by timing items (p. 178).

**General business credit** (Form 3800): research, work opportunity, small-employer plan start-up, and many others (p. 177).

**Part II payments and refundable credits** (pp. 179–182): net premium tax credit (Form 8962); amount paid with an extension (Form 4868); **excess Social Security tax** withheld by two or more employers (maximum for 2025: 6.2% × $176,100 = $10,918.20; a single employer that over-withholds must refund it); credit for federal tax on off-highway fuels (Form 4136); undistributed capital gains of a mutual fund (Form 2439); the claim-of-right credit; and elective payments for certain credits.
