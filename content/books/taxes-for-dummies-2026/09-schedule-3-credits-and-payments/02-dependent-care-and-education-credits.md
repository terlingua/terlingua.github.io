## Learning objectives

- Know what care costs qualify for the dependent care credit
- Compare the American opportunity and lifetime learning credits, with correct rates
- Understand who may claim an education credit and the effect of refunds and Form 1098-T

## The ideas

**Child and dependent care credit** (Form 2441; pp. 173–174): for care of a child under 13 (or a disabled dependent or spouse of any age) so that you (and your spouse) can work or look for work. Daycare, after-school care, babysitters and summer *day* camp qualify; overnight camp, lessons and activities don't. Details in Chapter 16. OBBBA raises the credit from 2026.

**American opportunity credit (AOTC)** (p. 174): up to $2,500 per eligible student per year — 100% of the first $2,000 and 25% of the next $2,000 of tuition, required fees and course materials — for the first four years of post-secondary education, at least half-time, without a felony drug conviction. 40% (up to $1,000) is refundable. Phase-out: modified AGI $80,000–$90,000 single, $160,000–$180,000 joint. Not available to MFS filers.

**Lifetime learning credit (LLC)**: **20%** of up to $10,000 of qualified expenses per *return* (maximum $2,000), any number of years, any course to acquire or improve job skills, no half-time requirement, nonrefundable, same phase-out as the AOTC. *Correction:* the book says 25% (p. 174); the statute (section 25A(c)) is 20%.

**One credit per student per year**, but different students in one family can use different credits (p. 174).

**Who claims?** Whoever claims the student as a dependent claims the credit, even if the student or a third party (such as a grandparent) paid. If no one claims the student, the student can (pp. 175–176).

**Refunds and 1098-T.** Reduce expenses for refunds received before filing; refunds received after filing require recapturing the excess credit in the later year. Form 1098-T reports tuition, but compute the credit from what you actually paid in the year (p. 175).
