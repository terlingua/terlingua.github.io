## Learning objectives

- Know the 2026 administrative changes that affect filing and penalties
- Know the status of the premium tax credit and the first 2027 figures

## The ideas

- **IRS Direct File** wasn't offered for the 2026 filing season (2025 returns); use IRS Free File partners, VITA/TCE or commercial software (Chapter 2).
- **Automatic Exemption from Penalty (AEP)** (IR-2026-83, July 8, 2026): automatic first-time relief from failure-to-file, failure-to-pay and failure-to-deposit penalties for taxpayers with three clean prior years, starting with 2025 original returns; it fully replaces First Time Abate for returns originally due from January 1, 2027. Doesn't cover estimated tax or accuracy penalties.
- **Paper refund checks** were phased out from September 30, 2025 (Executive Order 14247); provide direct deposit details, and expect electronic payment requirements to expand.
- **The redesigned 2025 Form 1040**: AGI on line 11a and 11b; the standard deduction on line 12e; the QBI deduction on line 13a; the new Schedule 1-A deductions on line 13b. Line references in the book (written before the final form) are often off.
- **Form 1099-DA**: digital asset brokers reported gross proceeds for 2025 and add cost basis for covered assets acquired from 2026; Form 8949 boxes G–L.
- **Premium tax credit**: the enhanced credits expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, eligibility again ends at 400% of the federal poverty line (the "cliff"), required contributions rose, and (under OBBBA) excess advance credits must be repaid in full with no caps. A House-passed extension (January 2026) stalled in the Senate.
- **Trump accounts**: contributions began July 4, 2026 (Form 4547; $1,000 Treasury pilot deposit for children born 2025–2028).
- **2027 figures** (Rev. Proc. 2026-35): standard deduction $33,200 joint, $16,600 single, $24,925 HOH; CTC $2,300 (refundable $1,800); gift exclusion $20,000; estate exemption $15.48 million; 37% bracket above $661,375 single, $793,650 joint.
