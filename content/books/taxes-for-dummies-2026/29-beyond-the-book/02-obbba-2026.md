## Learning objectives

- Recall the 2026 changes to charitable giving, itemized deductions, dependent care, 529s and information reporting
- Know which new deductions are temporary (2025–2028)

## The ideas

**Starting in 2026:**

- **Charitable**: non-itemizers can deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) of cash gifts to public charities (not DAFs); itemizers' charitable deductions are reduced by 0.5% of AGI; corporate gifts face a 1% floor.
- **Itemized deductions** for 37%-bracket taxpayers are worth at most 35 cents per dollar (the "2/37" rule).
- **Mortgage insurance premiums** are deductible as mortgage interest again.
- **Dependent care**: FSA limit $7,500; credit rate up to 50% for low incomes, with a second step-down above $75,000 ($150,000 joint).
- **529 K-12 limit** $20,000; broader K-12 expenses.
- **1099-NEC/MISC threshold** $2,000 (indexed after 2026); 1099-K threshold restored to $20,000 and 200 transactions.
- **QBI**: wider phase-in ranges and a $400 minimum deduction.
- **Employer-provided meals** for the employer's convenience become nondeductible.
- **Clean energy credits**: residential (25C, 25D), clean vehicle and commercial EV credits ended in 2025.

**Temporary, 2025 through 2028**: the $6,000 senior deduction (phase-out above $75,000/$150,000 MAGI), the qualified tips deduction ($25,000), the qualified overtime premium deduction ($12,500/$25,000), and the car loan interest deduction ($10,000). Also SALT's $40,000+ cap reverts to $10,000 in 2030.

**Permanent**: TCJA brackets and standard deduction, $750,000 mortgage limit, zero personal exemptions, CTC ($2,200 indexed), QBI, 100% bonus depreciation, $2.5 million section 179, estate exemption ($15 million indexed), AMT changes.
