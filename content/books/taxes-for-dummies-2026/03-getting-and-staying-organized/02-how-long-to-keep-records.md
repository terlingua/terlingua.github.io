## Learning objectives

- State the three-year, six-year and unlimited assessment periods
- Explain when the clock starts for timely, extended and late returns
- Know which records to keep indefinitely, and why state rules matter

## The ideas

**Three years.** The IRS generally has three years after a return is filed to assess more tax. A return filed early is treated as filed on the due date. So a 2023 return due April 15, 2024 can be adjusted until April 15, 2027 (p. 36). If you file under an extension, or late, the three years run from the actual filing date.

**Six years** if you omit more than 25% of the gross income you reported (p. 36). **No limit** if there is fraud or no return was filed.

**Refunds run the other way.** To claim a refund you generally must file within three years of filing the return or two years of paying the tax, whichever is later.

**Keep asset records as long as you own the asset**, plus the limitations period for the year of sale: stock and fund purchases (including reinvested dividends), home purchase and improvements, collectibles and valuable personal property. Video or photograph your home's contents and store the file off-site or in the cloud (p. 36).

**States may differ.** Some states have longer audit periods, and non-tax disputes (a contractor suing you) can make old receipts valuable (p. 35).
