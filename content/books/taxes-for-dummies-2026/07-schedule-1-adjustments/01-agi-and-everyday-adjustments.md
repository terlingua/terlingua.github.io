## Learning objectives

- Explain what adjustments are and why AGI matters
- Apply the educator expense, early-withdrawal penalty, jury duty, attorney-fee and other line 24 adjustments

## The ideas

**AGI = total income − adjustments.** Adjustments ("above-the-line deductions") are available whether or not you itemize (p. 130). AGI is the benchmark for floors and phase-outs throughout the return: the 7.5% medical floor, the taxability of Social Security, IRA deductibility, credits, and more. A dollar of adjustment is often worth more than a dollar of itemized deduction, because it also lowers AGI.

**Everyday adjustments:**

- **Educator expenses** (line 11): up to $300 for 2025 ($600 if both spouses are educators) for K–12 teachers, instructors, counselors, principals and aides working at least 900 hours a school year; supplies, books, software, professional development, and protective equipment (p. 131). Reduced by tax-free education benefits (savings bond interest exclusion, 529 or Coverdell withdrawals). From 2026, OBBBA adds an itemized deduction for unreimbursed K–12 educator expenses above the $300.
- **Certain workers' business expenses** (line 12, Form 2106): armed forces reservists traveling more than 100 miles, qualified performing artists (two or more employers, $200 from each, expenses over 10% of income, AGI $16,000 or less), fee-basis state and local officials, and employees with impairment-related work expenses (pp. 131–132).
- **Moving expenses** (line 14): only active-duty military moving under orders (p. 133).
- **Penalty on early withdrawal of savings** (line 18): the CD penalty in 1099-INT box 2, deductible even if it exceeds the interest (p. 138).
- **Line 24**: jury pay turned over to the employer; expenses of renting personal property for profit; excluded Olympic prize money; reforestation; attorney fees for unlawful discrimination claims and IRS whistleblower awards (up to the amount of the award); the foreign housing deduction for the self-employed; and excess deductions on termination of an estate or trust from a final Schedule K-1 (pp. 152–156).
