## Learning objectives

- Understand why the elderly or disabled credit rarely applies
- Recall the education credit phase-outs and which credit fits which student

## The ideas

**Credit for the Elderly or the Disabled** (Schedule R, pp. 359–360): for people 65+ or permanently disabled with taxable disability income. *Clarification:* the $5,000 / $7,500 / $3,750 the book calls maximum credits are the *initial amounts* the credit is computed from; the credit is 15% of that amount after reductions, so at most $750 single, $1,125 joint. Reduce the base by nontaxable Social Security and pensions and by half of AGI over $7,500 single ($10,000 joint); no credit at AGI of $17,500 single or $25,000 joint (both eligible). Almost nobody qualifies because the figures have never been indexed.

**Education credits** (Form 8863, pp. 360–361; details in Chapter 9 of the companion): AOTC up to $2,500 per student (40% refundable), first four years, at least half-time; Lifetime Learning up to $2,000 per return, any courses for a degree or to improve job skills. Both phase out between MAGI of $80,000–$90,000 ($160,000–$180,000 joint) and require Form 1098-T. Different students can use different credits in the same year, but not both for one student.
