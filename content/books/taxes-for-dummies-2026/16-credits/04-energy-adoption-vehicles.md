## Learning objectives

- Know which energy credits ended in 2025 and their limits
- Apply the adoption credit's timing, limits, phase-out and new refundable portion
- Know the clean vehicle credit cutoff and eligibility rules

## The ideas

**Residential energy credits** (Form 5695, p. 364), ended by OBBBA:

- **Energy efficient home improvement credit (25C)**: 30% of costs, *annual* limit $1,200 per year (with sub-limits: windows $600, doors $500, audits $150), plus a separate $2,000 for heat pumps and biomass stoves — up to $3,200 a year. *Correction:* the book describes an "aggregate lifetime maximum credit of $500-$600"; that was the pre-2023 rule. No carryforward of unused 25C credit. Ends for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.
- **Residential clean energy credit (25D)**: 30% of solar electric, solar water heating, fuel cells, small wind, geothermal heat pumps and (since 2023) battery storage, no dollar cap (except fuel cells), unused credit carries forward. Ends for expenditures made after December 31, 2025. (Biomass now falls under 25C, not 25D as the book lists.)

**Adoption credit** (Form 8839, pp. 364–366): up to **$17,280** per child for 2025 (2026: $17,670), phased out between MAGI $259,190 and $299,190. Eligible child under 18 or incapable of self-care; not a stepchild. Domestic adoptions: expenses paid before the year of finalization are claimed the following year; foreign adoptions only when final. Failed domestic adoptions count. An employer adoption benefit exclusion up to the same limit can be used in addition (not for the same expenses). *Correction:* the book calls the credit nonrefundable; from 2025, **up to $5,000 per child is refundable** (2026: $5,120), with the rest nonrefundable and carried forward up to five years.

**Clean vehicle credits** (Form 8936, pp. 366–367): new clean vehicle up to $7,500; used up to $4,000 (30% of a price up to $25,000). Ended for vehicles **acquired after September 30, 2025** (a binding contract and payment by that date counts). MAGI limits (current or prior year): new — $300,000 joint, $225,000 HOH, $150,000 others; used — half those. Price caps (MSRP): $55,000 cars, $80,000 vans, SUVs and pickups. The credit depends on critical mineral and battery component rules ($3,750 each), not "kilowatt capacity" as the book says, and the April 17 date the book cites is from 2023. Since 2024 buyers could transfer the credit to the dealer at sale; recapture if over the income limit.
