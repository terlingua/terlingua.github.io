## Learning objectives

- Distinguish credits from deductions, and refundable from nonrefundable credits
- Apply the dependent care credit's qualifying-person, earned-income and expense rules
- Compute the credit, including deemed income for a student or disabled spouse
- Coordinate with an employer dependent care account, including 2026 changes

## The ideas

A **credit** reduces tax dollar for dollar; a deduction reduces taxable income (p. 355). A **refundable** credit pays out even beyond your tax (the book lists EITC, the refundable part of the American opportunity credit, the additional child tax credit and the premium tax credit; from 2025, up to $5,000 per child of the adoption credit is refundable too). A **nonrefundable** credit can only reduce tax to zero (p. 356).

**Child and dependent care credit** (Form 2441, pp. 356–359): for care of a child under 13, or a spouse or dependent unable to care for themselves, so that you (and your spouse) can work or look for work. Qualifying costs include daycare, nursery school, before/after-school programs and day camps — not overnight camp, and not kindergarten or school tuition. Payments to a relative count unless they're your dependent, or your child under 19 at year end. Both spouses need earned income; a full-time student or incapable spouse is deemed to earn $250 a month (one qualifying person) or $500 (two or more). Married filing separately generally can't claim it.

**Computation for 2025**: expenses capped at $3,000 (one) or $6,000 (two or more), and at the lower earner's income; times a rate of 35% at AGI up to $15,000, falling 1 point per $2,000 to 20% at AGI over $43,000. So most middle-class families get 20%: $600 or $1,200. Nonrefundable.

**Employer dependent care accounts** (box 10 of the W-2; Part III): up to $5,000 excluded for 2025 ($2,500 MFS), reducing the expenses available for the credit. At a 22% bracket plus 7.65% FICA, $5,000 through the account saves far more than the 20% credit. Nanny paid under the table: no credit. (Household employee threshold for 2025: $2,800; the book's $2,600 is out of date; Chapter 17.)

**2026 changes (OBBBA)**: the dependent care account limit rises to **$7,500** ($3,750 MFS), and the credit rate starts at 50% for AGI up to $15,000, stepping down to 35%, then from AGI over $75,000 ($150,000 joint) down to a 20% floor.
