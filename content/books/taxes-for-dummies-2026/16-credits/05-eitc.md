## Learning objectives

- Identify earned income for EITC
- Apply the 2025 phase-out ranges, investment income limit and age rules
- Use the combat pay election and tie-breaker rules

## The ideas

The **EITC** (pp. 368–370) is a refundable credit for low- and moderate-income workers. It's based on earned income (wages, tips, net self-employment earnings, nontaxable combat pay if elected), not on pensions, Social Security, unemployment, interest or child support. The credit rises with earnings, plateaus, then phases out by the greater of earned income or AGI. 2025 maximums: about $649 (no children), $4,328 (one), $7,152 (two), $8,046 (three or more). Completely phased out at (single/HOH vs joint): no child $19,104 / $26,214; one $50,434 / $57,554; two $57,310 / $64,430; three $61,555 / $68,675 (Table 16-1).

**Rules**: valid SSNs; U.S. citizen or resident all year; no Form 2555; investment income no more than **$11,950** in 2025 (*the book also says $11,000 in one list, p. 369 — the correct 2025 figure is $11,950*; 2026: $12,200). Married filing separately is allowed only for a spouse who lived apart from the other spouse for the last six months of the year (or is legally separated) and had a qualifying child live with them more than half the year. Without a qualifying child: age 25 to 64, U.S. home more than half the year, not a dependent.

**Tie-breakers** (p. 370): a parent beats a nonparent; between parents who don't file jointly, the one the child lived with longer, then higher AGI; among nonparents, the highest AGI (and a nonparent can claim only if their AGI is higher than any eligible parent's).

The IRS can't issue EITC/ACTC refunds before mid-February (PATH Act), and erroneous claims can lead to a 2- or 10-year ban.
