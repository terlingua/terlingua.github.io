## Learning objectives

- Apply section 1245 and 1250 recapture on Form 4797
- Compute installment-sale gain recognition
- Know when to elect out of the installment method, and the imputed interest rule

## The ideas

**Depreciation recapture** (pp. 316–317). Depreciation deductions offset ordinary income, so the tax law claws part of them back on sale:

- **Equipment and other personal property (section 1245)**: gain up to the depreciation taken is ordinary income; only the excess is capital gain. Example: machine cost $10,000, depreciation $7,120, basis $2,880, sold for $12,000: $7,120 ordinary + $2,000 section 1231 gain.
- **Real estate (section 1250)**: for post-1986 straight-line depreciation, gain equal to depreciation is "unrecaptured section 1250 gain" taxed at up to 25%; the rest at 0/15/20%. Example: bought $200,000, depreciation $100,000, sold $400,000: $100,000 at up to 25% and $200,000 at capital gain rates.

Business property sold at a loss: ordinary (section 1231) loss, fully deductible.

**Installment sales** (Form 6252; p. 318): gain is recognised as payments arrive, using the gross profit ratio. Land sold for $60,000 (basis $15,000, profit $45,000 → 75%), paid $10,000 a year: $7,500 of each payment is gain, $2,500 return of basis. You can elect out to report everything in the sale year (useful if you'd otherwise waste deductions or expect higher rates later). Not available for inventory or publicly traded stock. Recapture income is reported in full in the year of sale. Adequate interest must be charged, or part of the price is recharacterized as interest.
