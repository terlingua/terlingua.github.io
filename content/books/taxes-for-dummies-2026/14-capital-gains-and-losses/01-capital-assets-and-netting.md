## Learning objectives

- Identify capital assets and know which losses are nondeductible
- Determine long-term versus short-term holding periods, including gifts, inheritances and option exercises
- Net gains and losses and apply the $3,000 limit and carryovers
- Know the 25% and 28% rates

## The ideas

**Capital assets** include stocks, bonds, funds, crypto, collectibles, your home and personal property. Gains on personal-use property are taxable; losses on personal-use property (your home, car, jewelry, furniture) are not deductible (p. 305).

**Holding period** (p. 310): long-term if held *more than one year*. Count from the day after the trade date of purchase through the trade date of sale (settlement date doesn't matter). Bought April 16, 2024: a sale on April 16, 2025 is short-term; April 17, 2025 is long-term. Gifts taking the donor's basis also take the donor's holding period; inherited property is always long-term; shares from exercising an option start their holding period the day after exercise.

**Rates**: long-term gains 0/15/20% (Chapter 10); collectibles up to 28%; unrecaptured section 1250 gain (prior depreciation on real estate) up to 25% (p. 306); short-term gains at ordinary rates; plus 3.8% NIIT at high incomes.

**Netting** (Schedule D, pp. 314–321): net short-term gains and losses; net long-term; then combine. A net loss offsets up to **$3,000** of ordinary income a year ($1,500 MFS); the rest carries forward indefinitely, keeping its short- or long-term character. Capital gain distributions from funds go on line 13 (or directly on Form 1040 line 7 if they're your only capital item).
