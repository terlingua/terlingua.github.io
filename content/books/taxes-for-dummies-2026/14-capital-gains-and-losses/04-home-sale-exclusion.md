## Learning objectives

- Apply the ownership and use tests and the two-year limit
- Compute a partial exclusion for job, health or unforeseen-circumstance moves
- Handle depreciation, nonqualified use, surviving spouses and military service
- Compute the gain and report it

## The ideas

**Exclusion** (pp. 321–323): up to **$250,000** of gain ($500,000 joint) on a principal residence if, during the five years before the sale, you **owned** it at least two years and **used** it as your main home at least two years (not necessarily consecutive or the same two years). Joint filers: either spouse can meet ownership, but both must meet use (and neither used the exclusion in the prior two years) for $500,000. Usable once every two years. A vacation home doesn't qualify.

**Partial exclusion** (p. 323) if you fail the two-year tests because of a job change (new job 50+ miles farther away), health, or unforeseen circumstances (death, divorce, multiple births, job loss, disaster): exclusion × months qualified ÷ 24. Example: single, owned and lived 12 months, gain $200,000: 12/24 × $250,000 = $125,000 excluded; $75,000 taxable.

**Computing gain** (pp. 324–326): sale price − selling costs (commissions, legal, title) − adjusted basis (purchase price + purchase closing costs + improvements).

**Not excludable** (pp. 327): depreciation claimed after May 6, 1997 for a home office or rental (taxed up to 25%). Also, since 2009, gain allocable to "nonqualified use" periods (for example, years it was a rental before you moved in) isn't excludable — a rule the book doesn't mention.

**Special cases** (pp. 322, 325): a surviving spouse can claim $500,000 if the sale is within two years of the death and other tests are met; the deceased spouse's half gets a stepped-up basis (all of it in community property states). Military and Foreign Service members on qualified extended duty can suspend the five-year period for up to ten years. A home transferred in divorce counts the ex-spouse's ownership time.

**Reporting** (p. 326): if you get a Form 1099-S, report the sale on Form 8949 and show the exclusion (code H); if the whole gain is excluded and no 1099-S was issued, you don't have to report it. Losses aren't deductible.
