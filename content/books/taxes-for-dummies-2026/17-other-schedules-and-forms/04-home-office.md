## Learning objectives

- Apply the regular-and-exclusive-use and principal-place-of-business tests
- Compute the regular deduction, its income limit and carryover
- Compare the simplified method (with a correction)
- Depreciate the office over 39 years and understand recapture

## The ideas

**Qualification** (pp. 381–382): a specific area used **regularly and exclusively** for your business, and either your principal place of business (including where you do administrative or management work if you have no other fixed location for it), a place to meet clients, or a separate structure. Storing inventory or samples qualifies without exclusive use if it's your only fixed business location. Employees can't deduct home offices after 2017 (OBBBA made this permanent). A dining table you also eat at fails exclusivity.

**Regular method**: business percentage (office square feet ÷ home square feet, e.g., 300 ÷ 1,500 = 20%) × indirect expenses (mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs, rent) + 100% of direct expenses (painting the office). **Depreciation**: on the lower of adjusted basis or value when you start, excluding land, over **39 years** (Table 17-1: 2.564% a year; a partial first year by month). **Income limit**: the deduction can't create a loss; it's taken in order (interest and taxes first, then operating, then depreciation) and the excess carries forward.

**Simplified method** (pp. 382–383): *correction*: **$5** per square foot up to 300 square feet, **maximum $1,500** — the book says $6 and $1,800 (p. 383). No depreciation (no recapture), no carryover of excess; full mortgage interest and taxes go on Schedule A. You can switch methods year by year.

**Depreciation recapture** (p. 386): depreciation "allowed or allowable" reduces basis, and on sale the post-May 6, 1997 depreciation is taxed (up to 25%) even if you skipped claiming it.
