## Learning objectives

- Explain the pay-as-you-go principle and the underpayment penalty
- Apply the 90%-of-current-year and 100%/110%-of-prior-year safe harbors
- Know the 2026 due dates (with a correction)
- Use the extension trick for the first-quarter estimate

## The ideas

The U.S. system is **pay-as-you-earn** (p. 371): withholding and quarterly estimates. You avoid the underpayment penalty (Form 2210) if you owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, or if your timely payments total at least the smaller of:

- **90% of the current year's tax**, or
- **100% of the prior year's tax** (the "safe harbor"), or **110%** if the *prior year's* AGI exceeded $150,000 ($75,000 MFS). The prior year must have been a full 12-month year with a return filed.

*Correction:* the book says the 110% rule applies "If your 2026 adjusted gross income is more than $150,000" (p. 372). The test is the **prior year's** AGI (2025 AGI for 2026 estimates). The book's example is otherwise right: a 2025 tax of $100,000 with high income needs $110,000 of 2026 payments; then even a $150,000 2026 tax carries no underpayment penalty, though the balance is still due April 15, 2027.

**Withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year** (whenever it actually happened), while estimates count when paid — so increasing withholding late in the year can cure an underpayment; a big December estimate can't fix earlier quarters (unless you use the annualized method).

**2026 due dates**: April 15, **June 15**, **September 15**, 2026 and January 15, 2027. *Correction:* the book lists June 18 and September 16 (p. 373). You can skip the January payment if you file and pay the full 2026 tax by January 31 (February 1, 2027, since January 31 is a Sunday).

**Extension trick** (p. 373): when filing Form 4868, pay your estimated 2025 balance plus the first-quarter 2026 estimate together; apply any overpayment to 2026. That protects you if 2025's tax turns out higher, because late payment interest and penalties on 2025 tax are costlier than a small 2026 underpayment penalty.

Pay electronically (IRS Online Account, Direct Pay, EFTPS) to avoid misapplied mailed checks.
