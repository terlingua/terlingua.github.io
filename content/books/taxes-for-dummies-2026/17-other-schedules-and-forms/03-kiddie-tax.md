## Learning objectives

- Determine who's subject to the kiddie tax
- Apply the 2025 $1,350 / $2,700 tiers
- Choose between Form 8615 and Form 8814

## The ideas

The **kiddie tax** (pp. 380–381) taxes a child's unearned income above a threshold at the *parents'* rate, to stop income shifting. It applies to children under 19 at year end, or full-time students under 24, whose earned income doesn't exceed half their support, with at least one living parent.

For 2025: the first **$1,350** of unearned income is offset by the child's standard deduction; the next **$1,350** is taxed at the child's rate; unearned income over **$2,700** is taxed at the parents' rate. (Some passages of the book use the old $1,100 figure, p. 381; for 2025 it's $1,350.) A child with more than $1,350 of unearned income must file. Children's earned income is never subject to the kiddie tax.

**Form 8615** goes on the child's return, using the parent's tax rate (custodial parent; for married separate filers, the higher taxable income). **Form 8814** lets parents report a child's interest and dividends (only, under $13,500 for 2025) on their own return — simpler, but usually a bit more tax, and it raises parental AGI, which can cut credits and deductions.

Strategies: growth assets with low dividends, 529 plans, Roth IRAs for children with earned income, and tax-exempt bonds.
