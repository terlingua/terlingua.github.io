## Learning objectives

- Apply the 2025 gross-income filing thresholds, and correct the book's table for people 65 and older
- Know the April 15, June 15 and October 15 dates and what an extension does and doesn't do
- Know the consequences of not filing
- Compare e-filing and paper filing

## The ideas

**Filing thresholds (gross income, 2025).** You must file if gross income is at least the standard deduction for your status, including the extra amount for age 65 but not for blindness, and not including the new senior deduction:

| Status | Under 65 | 65 or older |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $15,750 | $17,750 |
| Head of household | $23,625 | $25,625 |
| Married filing jointly | $31,500 | $33,100 (one 65+), $34,700 (both) |
| Married filing separately | $5 | $5 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $31,500 | $33,100 |

*Correction:* the book's Table 4-3 (pp. 64–65) adds the $6,000 senior deduction and the blindness amount to these thresholds (for example, $23,750 for a single filer 65 or older). IRS Publication 501 for 2025 uses the amounts above. Other triggers apply regardless of income: $400 or more of net self-employment earnings, advance premium tax credit payments, owing special taxes, and so on. You should also file to get withholding back or claim refundable credits.

**Deadlines** (p. 66). April 15, 2026 for 2025 returns. Citizens and residents living abroad get an automatic extension to June 15 (interest still runs from April 15 on unpaid tax). Form 4868 extends filing to October 15; a designated extension payment by Direct Pay, EFTPS or card counts as the extension request. **An extension extends the time to file, not to pay.** The late-payment penalty is 0.5% a month, and the book notes the underpayment interest rate was 7% in 2025.

**Not filing** (p. 66) can bring a substitute-for-return assessment by the IRS (without your deductions), failure-to-file penalties up to 25%, failure-to-pay penalties up to 25%, interest, and in egregious cases the 75% fraud penalty or prosecution. File on time even if you can't pay.

**How to file** (pp. 67–68). E-filing (about 94% of individual returns for tax year 2023) is faster — refunds in about ten days with direct deposit — and confirms receipt. Paper filers should use certified mail (postmark = filing date under the mailbox rule) or a designated private delivery service. Refund anticipation loans are costly.
