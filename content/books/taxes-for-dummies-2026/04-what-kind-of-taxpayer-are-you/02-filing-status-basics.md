- id: nra-spouse
  prompt: Your spouse is a nonresident alien with income only from abroad. What are your options?
  explanation: If one spouse is a nonresident alien, the couple “may not choose the married filing jointly tax status” unless they make the special election to treat the nonresident spouse as a resident (p. 48; IRS Publication 519). The election can lower tax when the foreign spouse has little income (you gain the joint brackets), but it subjects the spouse's worldwide income to US tax. Without it, the US person files MFS. A US citizen married to a nonresident alien may be "considered unmarried" for head of household purposes if other HOH tests are met.

## Learning objectives

- List the five filing statuses and explain why status matters
- Apply the December 31 rule
- Explain joint and several liability and the innocent spouse rule
- Know the rules for a nonresident alien spouse

## The ideas

**Five statuses**: single, married filing jointly (MFJ), married filing separately (MFS), head of household (HOH) and qualifying surviving spouse (the IRS's current name for "qualifying widow(er)"). Each has its own brackets and standard deduction. Joint and surviving-spouse status are generally cheapest; MFS is generally the most expensive (p. 46). You choose afresh each year.

**The December 31 rule.** Your marital status on the last day of the year governs the whole year. Married on December 31 → married for the year. Divorced or legally separated under a decree by December 31 → unmarried. If your spouse died during the year, you are considered married for that year and may file jointly (p. 47). Unmarried couples, however long together, cannot file jointly.

**Joint and several liability.** Both spouses sign, and “you're both responsible for seeing that all taxes are paid. That means if your spouse doesn't pay the tax due, you may have to” (p. 47). The innocent spouse rules (Chapter 20) can relieve a spouse who didn't know about the other's understatement.

**No going back.** After the due date you cannot amend a joint return into separate returns (an executor of a deceased spouse can, in limited cases); you *can* amend separate returns into a joint return within the refund period (p. 52).

**Nonresident alien spouse.** Normally you can't file jointly unless you elect to treat the spouse as a US resident, which subjects the spouse's worldwide income to US tax (p. 48; IRS Publication 519).

**Wedding timing.** Because status is set on December 31, a December wedding makes the whole year joint; some couples with a marriage penalty postpone to January (p. 48).
