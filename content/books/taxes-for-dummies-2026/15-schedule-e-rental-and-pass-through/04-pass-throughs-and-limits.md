## Learning objectives

- Explain pass-through taxation and Schedule K-1
- Apply the order of loss limits: basis, at-risk, passive (and excess business loss)
- Report each K-1 item in the right place

## The ideas

**Pass-through entities** (partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships, S corporations) don't pay income tax; each owner reports their share on **Schedule K-1**, with every item keeping its character (p. 350). Ordinary business income (box 1) and rental income go on Schedule E Part II; interest and dividends on Schedule B; capital gains on Schedule D; charitable contributions on Schedule A; section 179 deductions on Schedule E — *correction:* that's box 12 of a partnership K-1 and box 11 of an S corporation K-1, not lines 9 and 8 as the book says (p. 353). Estate and trust K-1s go to Part III. You're taxed on your share whether or not it's distributed (phantom income).

**Loss limits apply in order** (pp. 350–352):

1. **Basis**: you can't deduct more than your investment (plus, for partners, your share of entity debt; for S corporations, loans you made to it). S corporation shareholders attach Form 7203.
2. **At-risk** (Form 6198): you can deduct only what you could actually lose; nonrecourse notes don't count (qualified nonrecourse real estate financing is an exception). $10,000 cash + $20,000 nonrecourse note = $10,000 at risk.
3. **Passive** (Form 8582): if you don't materially participate.
4. Then the **excess business loss** limit (Chapter 13).

If you work in the business, your share is nonpassive (p. 352).
