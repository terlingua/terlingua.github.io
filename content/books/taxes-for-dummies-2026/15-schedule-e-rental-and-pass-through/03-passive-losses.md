## Learning objectives

- Explain why passive losses are suspended and when they're released
- Compute the $25,000 special allowance and its phase-out
- Know who can skip Form 8582
- Know the real estate professional and short-term rental exceptions (not in the book)

## The ideas

Since 1986, a **passive activity** (a business in which you don't materially participate, and most rentals) can produce deductible losses only against passive income (p. 347). Unused losses are **suspended** and carried forward indefinitely; when you sell your entire interest to an unrelated party in a taxable sale, all suspended losses become deductible (p. 349). Multiple passive activities are combined: a $6,000 loss offsets a $5,000 profit, with $1,000 carried over.

**$25,000 special allowance** (pp. 346–347): if you **actively participate** in rental real estate (own 10% or more and make management decisions such as approving tenants and expenses; a manager may handle day-to-day work), up to $25,000 of rental losses can offset nonpassive income. It phases out by 50 cents per dollar of modified AGI over $100,000, gone at $150,000. Married filing separately and living apart all year: $12,500, phased out between $50,000 and $75,000; living together at any time: zero. MAGI here excludes taxable Social Security, IRA deductions, student loan interest, half of SE tax and passive losses (p. 346).

**Skipping Form 8582** (p. 345) requires all of: rental real estate is the only passive activity; active participation; loss within $25,000; no prior-year suspended losses; MAGI ≤ $100,000; MFS lived apart; no limited-partner or estate/trust rental interests.

**Not in the book**:

- **Real estate professionals** (more than 750 hours and more than half of personal services in real property trades, and material participation in the rental) treat rentals as nonpassive — line 43 of Schedule E (p. 354).
- **Short-term rentals** with an average stay of 7 days or less aren't "rental activities" under the passive rules; if you materially participate (e.g., 100+ hours and more than anyone else), the losses are nonpassive even without professional status.
- Converting your home to a rental: after three years of renting, you lose the home sale exclusion (p. 347).
