## Learning objectives

- Know when the Taxpayer Advocate can take your case
- Prepare a Taxpayer Advocate request (Form 911)
- Know about civil damages for unauthorized collection

## The ideas

The **Taxpayer Advocate Service** (TAS, pp. 425–427) is an independent organisation within the IRS, led by the National Taxpayer Advocate, who reports to Congress. Local advocates don't give tax advice; they fix procedural, refund, notice and collection problems that normal channels haven't — tracing payments, abating penalties, releasing levies, expediting refunds.

**When TAS helps** (p. 426): you contacted the IRS and got no response within 30 days; a refund is more than 60 days late; the IRS missed a promised date; or you face economic harm (loss of livelihood, housing, credit) or an IRS system or procedure isn't working.

**How**: Form 911 (Request for Taxpayer Advocate Service Assistance), or call 877-777-4778, with a clear description, copies of notices, prior letters, call logs and proof. An advocate can issue a **Taxpayer Assistance Order** to stop an IRS action. Filing Form 911 suspends the relevant statute of limitations while it's pending (p. 423).

**Damages**: separately, section 7433 lets you sue for damages if IRS employees recklessly or intentionally disregard the law in collection (up to $1 million; $100,000 if negligent), after exhausting administrative remedies. (The book links this to Form 911, p. 423; it's a separate administrative claim and court action.)
