## Learning objectives

- Compute a QBI deduction correctly (with a corrected example) and know the 2026 changes
- Choose between immediate expensing and depreciation
- Apply 2025 vehicle limits and the meal and entertainment rules

## The ideas

**QBI deduction (section 199A)** (pp. 499–500): up to 20% of qualified business income from pass-throughs, made permanent by OBBBA. *Correction to the book's example:* the deduction is 20% of the *lesser* of QBI or taxable income before the deduction (less net capital gain), and QBI is net of the deductible half of SE tax, SE health insurance and retirement contributions. A single filer with $70,000 of Schedule C profit and no other income has QBI of about $65,054 and taxable income before QBI of about $49,304 (after the $15,750 standard deduction), so the deduction is about **$9,861**, not $14,000. For specified service businesses and the W-2/property limits, 2025 thresholds are $197,300 single ($394,600 joint) with a $50,000 ($100,000) phase-in; from 2026 the phase-in widens to $75,000 ($150,000), and a $400 minimum deduction applies to taxpayers with at least $1,000 of QBI from businesses they materially participate in.

**Expensing vs depreciation** (p. 500): section 179 allows up to $2,500,000 for 2025 (OBBBA), limited to business income, and 100% bonus depreciation is back for property acquired after January 19, 2025. Immediate write-offs aren't always best: in low-income early years a deduction is worth little; depreciating lets you use deductions when your rate is higher. Without expensing, 5-year property like computers uses MACRS with a half-year convention (not a flat $600 a year as the book's straight-line example suggests).

**Vehicles** (p. 501): 70 cents a mile for 2025 (72.5 cents for 2026) or actual costs plus depreciation, which is capped for passenger cars: 2025 first-year $12,200 ($20,200 with bonus), then $19,600, $11,800 and $7,060.

**Travel, meals, entertainment** (pp. 501–502): travel must be primarily for business; a Saturday stay that lowers total cost is deductible; companions aren't. Business meals are 50% deductible; entertainment and club dues aren't deductible. *Update:* from 2026, employer-provided on-site meals and de minimis meals for the employer's convenience are no longer deductible (with narrow OBBBA exceptions).

**Office** (p. 502): choose space for business reasons; a home office adds modest deductions (and depreciation recapture).
