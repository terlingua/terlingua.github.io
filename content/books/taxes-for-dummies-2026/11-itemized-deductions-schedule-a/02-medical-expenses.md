## Learning objectives

- Compute the medical deduction with the 7.5% floor
- Identify deductible and nondeductible items
- Apply rules for travel, lodging, insurance premiums, long-term care premiums, home improvements and reimbursements

## The ideas

**Floor.** Only unreimbursed medical and dental expenses above **7.5% of AGI** are deductible (p. 209). Expenses paid for you, your spouse and dependents (including someone who would be a dependent but for the income test or a joint return); either divorced parent may deduct what he or she pays for a child (pp. 210, 213). Deduct in the year paid; a credit card charge counts when charged (p. 203).

**Deductible** (pp. 210–212): doctors, dentists, hospitals, labs, prescription drugs and insulin, glasses and contacts, hearing aids, LASIK, ambulances, guide dogs, wheelchairs, smoking-cessation programs and prescribed drugs, addiction treatment, weight-loss programs for diagnosed obesity, medical equipment and supplies, nursing services, special schools for disabilities, medical care in a nursing home (including meals and lodging when the main reason is care), and health insurance premiums paid with after-tax money (including Medicare Parts B and D, and long-term care premiums up to age-based limits: $480 at 40 or under, up to $6,020 over 70, for 2025) (p. 215).

**Not deductible** (p. 212): over-the-counter medicines (other than insulin), general-health items (gym dues, vitamins without a specific prescription, diet foods), cosmetic surgery (unless correcting a deformity or injury), teeth whitening, funeral costs, maternity clothes, household help, life insurance, premiums paid pre-tax.

**Travel and lodging** (pp. 213–215): 21 cents a mile for 2025 (or actual gas and oil), plus parking and tolls; trips to specialists; travel of a necessary companion; medical conferences for a chronic condition (not meals or lodging). Lodging away from home for care: up to $50 a night per person.

**Home improvements** (p. 217): deductible to the extent cost exceeds the increase in the home's value; accessibility modifications (ramps, railings, widened doors, chairlifts) generally don't increase value and are fully deductible.

**Reimbursements** reduce expenses; reimbursement received in a later year is income only to the extent the earlier deduction gave a benefit (p. 215).
