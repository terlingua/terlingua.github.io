## Learning objectives

- Identify qualified organisations and nondeductible gifts
- Meet substantiation and appraisal rules
- Value property gifts (FMV vs basis) and apply AGI limits, with a correction for donor-advised funds
- Use QCDs and know the 2026 charitable changes

## The ideas

**Qualified donees** (pp. 229–231): US churches and other religious organisations, schools, publicly supported charities, veterans' groups, governments for public purposes. Not deductible: gifts to individuals, political groups, lobbying organisations, social clubs, unions, homeowners' associations, foreign charities (with treaty exceptions for Canada, Israel and Mexico), raffle and bingo tickets, tuition, the value of your time or blood. Check status with the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (formerly Publication 78).

**Timing.** A check mailed by December 31 or a card charge in December counts for that year (p. 229).

**Volunteer costs**: unreimbursed out-of-pocket costs, 14 cents a mile, parking and tolls, and travel away from home without significant personal pleasure (p. 229).

**Substantiation** (pp. 230–232): any cash gift needs a bank record or written receipt; $250 or more needs a contemporaneous written acknowledgment stating whether goods or services were received; noncash gifts over $500 need Form 8283; over $5,000 need a qualified appraisal (not for publicly traded stock). Clothing and household goods must be in good used condition. Vehicles worth over $500 that the charity sells: the deduction is limited to the gross proceeds (Form 1098-C) (p. 233).

**Property** (pp. 231–232): long-term capital gain property is deductible at fair market value (avoiding tax on the appreciation); short-term or ordinary income property (inventory, your own artwork) only at basis. Depreciated property: sell it to claim the loss, then donate cash.

**AGI limits** (p. 234): cash to public charities 60% of AGI; appreciated capital gain property to public charities 30%; cash to private nonoperating foundations 30%, appreciated property to them 20%. Excess carries forward five years. *Correction:* the book puts donor-advised funds at 30% (p. 234); a DAF's sponsor is a public charity, so cash to a DAF gets the 60% limit (30% for appreciated property).

**QCDs** (pp. 234–235): from age 70½, up to $108,000 for 2025 ($111,000 for 2026) transferred directly from an IRA to public charities (not DAFs or private foundations) is excluded from income and counts toward RMDs. Report on line 4a with 0 on 4b and "QCD".

**From 2026** (OBBBA): non-itemizers can deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) of cash gifts to public charities (not DAFs); itemizers can deduct charitable gifts only above 0.5% of AGI; and the 37%-bracket cap limits their value to 35%.
