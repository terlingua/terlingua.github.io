## Learning objectives

- Apply the $750,000/$1 million acquisition debt limits and the home-equity rule
- Deduct points on purchases and refinances correctly
- Know the PMI change for 2026
- Apply the investment interest limit and the capital gain election

## The ideas

**Qualified residence interest** (pp. 222–226): interest on debt secured by your main or second home and used to buy, build or substantially improve it. Limits on acquisition debt: **$750,000** ($375,000 MFS) for debt incurred after December 15, 2017; **$1 million** ($500,000) grandfathered for older debt; unlimited for debt before October 14, 1987. OBBBA made the $750,000 limit permanent. Home equity borrowing used for anything else (cars, tuition, credit cards) isn't deductible, even if secured by the home. Refinancing up to the old balance keeps the old status; extra cash out is deductible only if used for the home. Late charges and prepayment penalties count as interest. Form 1098 reports interest of $600 or more.

**Points** (p. 226): one point = 1% of the loan. Points to buy or build your **main** home are deductible when paid (even seller-paid points, which reduce your basis). Points on a second home or a refinance are amortized over the loan term, except the part of refinance points attributable to improvements (if paid with separate funds). When you refinance again or pay off a loan, deduct the remaining unamortized points.

**Mortgage insurance premiums (PMI)**: not deductible for 2025 (p. 227); OBBBA permanently restores the deduction (as mortgage interest) from 2026.

**Investment interest** (Form 4952; pp. 227–228): margin and other investment interest is deductible only up to net investment income (interest, nonqualified dividends, royalties, short-term gains), with the excess carried forward indefinitely. You may elect to treat qualified dividends and long-term gains as investment income, but then those amounts lose the preferential rate. Interest to carry tax-exempt bonds isn't deductible.
