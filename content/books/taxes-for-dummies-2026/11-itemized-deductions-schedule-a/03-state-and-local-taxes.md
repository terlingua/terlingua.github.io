## Learning objectives

- Identify deductible state and local taxes, including the income-or-sales-tax choice
- Compute the 2025 SALT cap with the high-income phase-down
- Handle property taxes at purchase and sale, special assessments and refunds

## The ideas

**What counts** (pp. 217–221): the greater of state and local income taxes *or* general sales taxes (actual receipts or the IRS table, plus tax on a big purchase such as a car or boat); real estate taxes on homes (not on business or rental property, which go on Schedules C or E); and personal property taxes based on value (car excise tax based on value, not flat registration fees). Income taxes include withholding, estimated payments and balances paid in the year for prior years, an overpayment applied to this year, and mandatory state disability and paid family leave contributions. Federal income and Social Security taxes are never deductible.

**The cap** (OBBBA; p. 218): **$40,000** for 2025 (**$20,000** MFS), rising 1% a year through 2029 ($40,400 for 2026), back to $10,000 in 2030. Reduced by 30% of modified AGI above $500,000 ($250,000 MFS) for 2025, but never below $10,000 ($5,000 MFS). The same $40,000 applies to single and joint filers — a marriage penalty.

**Property tax proration** (pp. 219–220): at a sale, taxes are split by days of ownership; the buyer can't deduct back taxes owed by the seller (they're added to basis). Assessments for local improvements (sidewalks, sewers) aren't deductible; they add to basis. Water, sewer, trash and HOA charges aren't taxes. Refunds of property tax paid in the same year reduce the deduction.
