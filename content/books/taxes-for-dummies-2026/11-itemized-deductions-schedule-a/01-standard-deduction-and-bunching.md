## Learning objectives

- State the 2025 standard deduction amounts, including age and blindness additions
- Explain how the senior bonus deduction fits in
- Decide whether to itemize, and use bunching

## The ideas

**Standard deduction 2025** (p. 204): $15,750 single and MFS, $31,500 joint and qualifying surviving spouse, $23,625 HOH. Add $2,000 (single, HOH) or $1,600 (married, per person) for each of age 65+ and blindness. Table 11-1 (p. 206): single 65+ $17,750; joint both 65+ $34,700. Dependents: greater of $1,350 or earned income + $450, capped at the regular amount (p. 206).

**Senior bonus deduction** (pp. 204, 206): $6,000 per person 65 or older (2025–2028), reduced by 6% of modified AGI above $75,000 / $150,000, available “in addition to your itemized deductions or on top of the standard deduction”. Technically it's a separate Schedule 1-A deduction, not part of the standard deduction, which is why it doesn't change the filing threshold (Chapter 4). A couple both 65+ under $150,000 gets $31,500 + $3,200 + $12,000 = $46,700 of total deductions (p. 204).

**Itemize or not?** Take the larger. Itemized deductions (pp. 208): medical above 7.5% of AGI; state and local taxes up to the cap; mortgage and investment interest; charity; casualty losses from declared disasters; a few "other" items. You may elect to itemize even when it's smaller (line 18), which can help on a state return (p. 240).

**Bunching.** Because the standard deduction is large, alternating years — concentrating two years of charitable gifts (or elective medical work, or property tax timing where possible) into one year and taking the standard deduction the next — can raise total deductions. Donor-advised funds make bunching easy: a large contribution in one year, grants out over several.

**Changing your mind**: amend with Form 1040X; MFS spouses must both change (p. 209).
