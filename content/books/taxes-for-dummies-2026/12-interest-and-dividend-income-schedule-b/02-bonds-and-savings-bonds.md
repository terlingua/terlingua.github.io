## Learning objectives

- Choose between deferral and annual reporting of EE/I bond interest
- Know the tax treatment of T-bills, Treasuries, TIPS and zero-coupon bonds, with a correction on TIPS
- Handle bond premium and market discount
- Apply the education savings bond exclusion

## The ideas

**EE and I savings bonds** (pp. 246–248): interest accrues; you can defer reporting until redemption or final maturity (30 years), or elect to report it annually (the election applies to all your EE/I bonds). Annual reporting can make sense for a child whose interest falls under the $1,350 kiddie threshold. At death, accrued interest can be reported on the decedent's final return or left for the heir (income in respect of a decedent). Federal tax only; state-exempt.

**T-bills** are sold at a discount and taxed when they mature (p. 247): a 6-month bill bought in December 2025 for $9,700 and maturing in 2026 at $10,000 gives $300 of interest in 2026 — a deferral tool. Treasury interest is exempt from state tax (p. 248).

**TIPS and I bonds.** I-bond interest is deferrable. TIPS pay semiannual interest on an inflation-adjusted principal; *both* the interest and the annual inflation adjustment to principal are taxable each year as ordinary income (the adjustment as OID), even though the adjustment isn't paid until maturity. *Correction:* the book says the inflation increase is subject to capital gains tax (p. 248).

**Zero-coupon bonds**: OID is taxable annually though no cash is received (p. 248), so many investors hold them in IRAs.

**Premiums and discounts** (pp. 245, 249–250). A taxable bond bought above face value: you may elect to amortize the premium each year against the interest (reducing basis); otherwise it's part of basis and produces a capital loss at maturity. Premium on tax-exempt bonds must be amortized (not deductible). A bond bought at a **market discount** below face value: the accrued discount is **ordinary interest** when you sell or redeem (not capital gain), even for municipal bonds bought after April 30, 1993, unless you elect to include it annually. (A tiny discount under 0.25% × years to maturity is treated as capital gain.) The book's example: a $1,000, 10-year bond bought for $900 and sold after five years for $960 → $50 of interest and $10 of capital gain.

**Education savings bond exclusion** (Form 8815; p. 251): interest on EE/I bonds issued after 1989 to owners 24 or older at purchase is excluded if redeemed to pay qualified tuition and fees (or contributed to a 529 or Coverdell) for you, your spouse or dependents. 2025 phase-out: modified AGI $99,500–$114,500 single, $149,250–$179,250 joint; not for MFS. No double dipping with education credits.
