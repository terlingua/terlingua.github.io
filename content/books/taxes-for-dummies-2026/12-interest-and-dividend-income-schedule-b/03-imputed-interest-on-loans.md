## Learning objectives

- Explain imputed interest on interest-free or low-rate loans
- Apply the $10,000 and $100,000 exceptions for gift loans
- Recognise the gift tax side

## The ideas

**Imputed interest** (p. 252). If you lend money interest-free or below the applicable federal rate (AFR), the tax law treats you as having received interest at the AFR (income to you) and the borrower as having paid it (deductible only if, for example, it's investment or qualified mortgage interest). In a family loan, the forgone interest is also treated as a gift, which can exceed the annual exclusion ($19,000 for 2025) on large loans.

**Exceptions for gift loans between individuals** (p. 252):

- **$10,000 or less** outstanding: ignored (unless used to buy income-producing assets).
- **Up to $100,000**: imputed interest is limited to the borrower's net investment income for the year, and is zero if that is $1,000 or less. Example: you lend your son $50,000 interest-free; his investment income is $800 → nothing imputed; $1,200 → $1,200 imputed even if the AFR interest would be $4,000.
- **Over $100,000**: full imputation.

Attach a statement with computation and counterparties' SSNs (p. 253). The practical answer for family lending is a written note charging at least the AFR (published monthly by the IRS).
