## Learning objectives

- Recall the book's ten overlooked savings and their 2025–2026 figures
- Apply the foreign earned income exclusion basics
- Avoid the home-equity deduction trap

## The ideas

The book's list (pp. 579–584): keep cash in higher-yielding (possibly tax-free) money market funds; hold growth assets whose returns come as deferred, long-term gains; fund retirement accounts; use a backdoor Roth when income is too high (beware the pro-rata rule on existing IRAs); consider overseas work; check whether you can itemize every year (and bunch); trade consumer debt for mortgage debt (*correction:* the interest on a cash-out refinance used to pay consumer debt isn't deductible — only home-acquisition or improvement debt qualifies; the lower rate can still help); claim charitable mileage (14 cents a mile) and noncash donations at fair market value with documentation; and claim all legitimate self-employment expenses.

**Foreign earned income exclusion** (p. 581): up to **$130,000** for 2025 ($132,900 for 2026) of foreign earned income if you meet the **physical presence** test (330 full days abroad in any 12-month period) or the **bona fide residence** test, plus a housing exclusion; foreign tax credits cover income above it. Federal government employees abroad don't qualify. Americans abroad get an automatic extension to June 15. Excluded income still counts for figuring the tax rate on other income ("stacking"), and self-employment tax still applies.

**Value-based car registration fees** are deductible personal property taxes if you itemize (p. 582). If you're married filing separately and your spouse itemizes, you must too.
