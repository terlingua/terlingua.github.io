## Learning objectives

- Evaluate a tax advisor's services, focus, conflicts and aggressiveness
- Understand fee structures, credentials and insurance

## The ideas

Educate yourself first so you can judge competence (p. 585). Ask (pp. 586–589):

1. **What services do you offer?** Look for proactive advisors who probe your situation, not just data entry.
2. **Do you specialise?** Match expertise to your needs (small business, equity comp, estates).
3. **What else do you sell?** Avoid advisors who sell commissioned financial products.
4. **Who will prepare my return?** Junior staff are fine if supervised and billed at lower rates.
5. **How aggressive or conservative are you?** Neither an IRS enforcer nor a corner-cutter.
6. **What's your audit experience?** A preparer claiming zero audits over many years may be untruthful, inexperienced or too timid. (The book cites a benchmark below 0.5% of returns.)
7. **How do you charge?** Hourly ($100–$300+) or flat; are follow-up questions and audit support included? Total time matters more than the rate.
8. **What qualifies you?** CPA, EA or attorney (with unlimited representation rights) plus experience; verify credentials (state CPA boards, the IRS's Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers).
9. **Do you carry errors-and-omissions insurance?** And have you been sued?
10. **Can you give references from clients like me?**
