## Learning objectives

- Recognise the drawbacks of limited partnerships, cash-value life insurance and annuities
- Understand dividend reinvestment and dollar-cost averaging record-keeping

## The ideas

**Limited partnerships** sold by brokers (pp. 518–520): high commissions (up to 10%), ongoing fees, illiquidity (7–10 years), yields sometimes padded by return of capital, and passive loss rules that neutralise most tax benefits. Never put them in an IRA.

**Cash-value life insurance** (p. 520): inside buildup is tax-deferred, but there's no deduction, high commissions, and Roth and deductible accounts beat it; its real tax use is estate planning (e.g., an irrevocable life insurance trust) for large estates.

**Annuities** (pp. 520–521): see Chapter 22: no deduction, ordinary income on earnings, high fees; if appropriate, buy no-load annuities direct.

**Reinvesting and dollar-cost averaging** (pp. 518–519): reinvested distributions are taxable when paid and add basis; many small purchases complicate lot tracking — keep year-end statements; fund companies report average cost for covered shares.
