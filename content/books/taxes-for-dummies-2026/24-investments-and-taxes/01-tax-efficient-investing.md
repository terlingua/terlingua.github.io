## Learning objectives

- Apply the 2025 long-term capital gain and qualified dividend brackets correctly (with a correction)
- Compare taxable and tax-free yields
- Place assets in the right accounts and choose tax-efficient funds

## The ideas

Outside retirement accounts, interest is taxed as ordinary income (Treasuries exempt from state tax), qualified dividends and long-term gains get 0/15/20% rates, and higher earners add the 3.8% NIIT (MAGI over $200,000 single, $250,000 joint) (p. 512).

**2025 rates on qualified dividends and long-term gains** — *correction to Table 24-1* (p. 512), which mixes old and new figures. Based on **taxable income**: **0%** up to $48,350 single ($96,700 joint, $64,750 HOH); **15%** up to $533,400 single ($600,050 joint, $566,700 HOH); **20%** above. (The book's 15% band starting at $44,625/$89,250 uses 2022 figures, and the text says "adjusted gross income" where taxable income is the measure, p. 513.)

**Tax-wise moves** (pp. 513–518): hold over a year; pay off high-interest debt (a guaranteed after-tax return; OBBBA's new car loan interest deduction is a narrow exception); fund retirement accounts first; in the 32%+ brackets favour tax-free money market and municipal bond funds (compare **tax-equivalent yield** = muni yield ÷ (1 − marginal rate)); prefer low-turnover index funds and ETFs; hold bond and high-dividend funds in retirement accounts; avoid buying fund shares just before a December capital gain distribution ("buying the dividend"). Never put tax-free bonds in an IRA (p. 511).
