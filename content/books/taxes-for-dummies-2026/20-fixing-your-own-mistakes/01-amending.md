## Learning objectives

- Know when and how to amend with Form 1040X, and what can't be changed
- Apply the amendment deadline
- Understand NOL carryforwards and the tax benefit rule

## The ideas

You can't claim a forgotten deduction on a later year's return; you **amend** the year it belongs to on **Form 1040X** (pp. 433–434), which can now be e-filed for recent years. Deadline for refunds: 3 years from filing (early returns count as filed on the due date) or 2 years from payment, whichever is later (Chapter 19). If you owe, amend and pay promptly to stop interest.

**What you can change**: separate to joint (but *not* joint to separate after the due date, except by an executor); itemized to standard and vice versa; errors like a wrongly claimed dependent or head of household status. Some elections are irrevocable (p. 434). An amended return generally doesn't change the estimated tax penalty already computed.

**Net operating losses** (p. 434): losses arising after 2017 carry forward indefinitely and offset only 80% of taxable income in a carryforward year; general carrybacks are gone (farm losses can still be carried back two years).

**Tax benefit rule** (p. 435): a recovery of something deducted earlier (a medical reimbursement, a state tax refund) is income only to the extent the earlier deduction reduced your tax. A state refund for a year you took the standard deduction isn't income.
