## Learning objectives

- Understand why filing on time matters even if you can't pay (with a correction on the failure-to-file penalty)
- Choose between installment agreements, currently-not-collectible status and an offer in compromise
- Know the basics of discharging taxes in bankruptcy (with a correction)

## The ideas

**File even if you can't pay** (p. 435). *Correction:* the book says the nonfiling penalty is 0.5% a month; the **failure-to-file penalty is 5% of the unpaid tax per month** (or part), up to 25%, versus **0.5% a month** for failure to pay (also capped at 25%). When both apply, the filing penalty is reduced by the payment penalty for the same month (4.5% + 0.5%). For 2025 returns more than 60 days late, the minimum filing penalty is the lesser of $525 or 100% of the tax due. Interest (federal short-term rate + 3%, compounded daily) runs on tax and penalties.

**Paying a bill**: no interest if paid within 21 days of notice (10 business days if $100,000 or more) (p. 436).

**Installment agreements** (pp. 436–438):

- **Guaranteed**: owe $10,000 or less, can pay within 3 years, and filed and paid on time for the past 5 years.
- **Streamlined**: owe $50,000 or less and pay within 72 months (and within the collection period): usually approved online without financial statements, with a setup fee (lower for direct debit and low income).
- Larger: financial statement (Form 433-A or 433-F); partial payment agreements possible.
- On a timely filed return, the failure-to-pay penalty drops to 0.25% a month during an agreement. Keep current on future taxes or the agreement defaults.

**Currently not collectible** (p. 438): if you can't pay basic living expenses, the IRS suspends collection; interest and penalties continue, the 10-year collection clock keeps running, and it reviews you periodically.

**Offer in compromise** (pp. 438–439; Form 656): settles for less when you can't pay in full (doubt as to collectibility), there's doubt the tax is owed, or for effective tax administration. Offers are based on "reasonable collection potential" (equity in assets + future disposable income); use the IRS pre-qualifier; there's an application fee and an initial payment (waived for low-income filers). You must stay compliant for five years after acceptance.

**Bankruptcy** (pp. 439–440): stops collection (automatic stay). Income taxes can be discharged in Chapter 7 if the return was due (with extensions) more than 3 years before filing, was filed more than 2 years before, and was assessed more than 240 days before, with no fraud. *Correction:* the law requires a credit counselling briefing within the 180 days before filing, not "six months of credit counseling" as the book says.
