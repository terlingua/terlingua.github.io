## Learning objectives

- Explain why advance premium tax credits are reconciled on Form 8962
- Apply the 2025 rules (no 400% cliff, repayment caps) and the 2026 changes
- Use the correct poverty-line year

## The ideas

**Advance payments.** Marketplace subsidies (advance premium tax credits, APTC) are paid monthly to insurers based on *estimated* income. On the return you compute the actual premium tax credit for your actual household income and reconcile: excess APTC is repaid (Schedule 2 line 2), a shortfall is a refundable credit (Schedule 3 line 9) (pp. 160–163, 180). Form 1095-A supplies the premiums, benchmark (second-lowest-cost silver) premium and APTC.

**The formula.** Household income (MAGI of you and dependents required to file: AGI plus untaxed Social Security, tax-exempt interest and excluded foreign income) as a percentage of the federal poverty line determines an "applicable percentage" of income you're expected to pay for the benchmark plan; the credit covers the rest.

**Which poverty line?** The credit for coverage in a year uses the poverty guidelines published for the *previous* year. For 2025 coverage that means the 2024 guidelines ($15,060 for one person in the 48 states); the book lists the 2025 guidelines ($15,650) (p. 161), which apply to 2026 coverage.

**2025 rules.** The enhanced credits from the American Rescue Plan and Inflation Reduction Act applied through 2025: there was **no 400% income cliff** (above 400% FPL, you paid at most 8.5% of income for the benchmark plan). The book's statement that you are entitled only if income is under 400% (p. 161) describes the pre-2021 and post-2025 law. Repayment of excess APTC was capped below 400% FPL (2025 caps: $375/$750 under 200%, $975/$1,950 for 200–299%, $1,625/$3,250 for 300–399%, single/other); the book's table shows 2024 caps (p. 163).

**2026 and later.** The enhanced credits expired on December 31, 2025 (a House-passed extension stalled in the Senate as of late 2026), restoring the 400% cliff; and OBBBA **eliminated the repayment caps** for tax years after 2025, so all excess APTC must be repaid.
