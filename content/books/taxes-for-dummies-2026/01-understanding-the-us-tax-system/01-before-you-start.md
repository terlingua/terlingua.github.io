## Before you start

This companion follows *Taxes 2026 For Dummies* by Eric Tyson and Chris Eck (Wiley, 2025). Page numbers are those of the book. Read the title carefully, because it is the first trap in tax: a book called "2026" is about the **2025 tax year**, the return most people filed between January and April 2026. The year in a tax book's title is the year you file, not the year you earned the money.

This is not tax or legal advice. It teaches the concepts so you can read your own return, ask a preparer better questions and recognise when you need one. Tax law changes every year; where a figure matters, the year it belongs to is stated.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish the tax year from the filing year
- Know what the book covers (federal income tax, plus some state and payroll taxes) and what it leaves to professionals
- Know how this companion marks figures, corrections and later changes

## How this companion works

**Two years at once.** The book gives 2025 figures. This companion uses those figures in its questions unless a question says otherwise, and it checks them against IRS sources. The book went to press in October 2025, three months after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) rewrote much of the code. Several OBBBA provisions were still being interpreted when the book was written, and the IRS redesigned the 2025 Form 1040 after the book was printed. Where the book's description is out of date, incomplete or wrong, the explanation says so. Chapter 29 collects the most consequential corrections and the tax year 2026 figures (which apply to returns filed in 2027), and Chapter 30 works through complete returns step by step.

**Line numbers.** The book walks through Form 1040 line by line. The final 2025 form moved some lines (for example, adjusted gross income appears on lines 11a and 11b, the standard deduction on line 12e, and a new line 13b carries the new Schedule 1-A deductions). Line numbers change almost every year; concepts don't. This companion asks about what a line *means*, not its number.

**Quotations.** Passages inside “ ” are quoted from the book and followed by a page number.

## What the book promises

The authors say most people's tax concerns fall into three groups: “filling out their forms properly, legally minimizing their taxes, and avoiding interest and penalties” (p. 2). The book is organised to match: Parts 1–3 are preparation (organising, filing status, the forms), Part 4 is dealing with the IRS (notices, audits, mistakes), Part 5 is year-round planning, and Part 6 is short lists. They suggest using it as a reference, as an advisor and, if you have the discipline, as a textbook (p. 2).
