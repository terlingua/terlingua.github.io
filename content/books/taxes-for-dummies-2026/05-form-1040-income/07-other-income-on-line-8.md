## Learning objectives

- Know when capital gain distributions skip Schedule D
- Recognise taxable "other income": barter, cancelled debt, prizes, treasure trove, gambling, illegal income
- Recognise exclusions: life insurance, gifts, many injury-related payments, the foreign earned income exclusion

## The ideas

**Line 7** carries net capital gain or loss from Schedule D, or capital gain distributions alone (p. 107). A loss on the sale of your home or other personal-use property is not deductible.

**Line 8** carries Schedule 1's other income (pp. 107–110):

- **Barter**: fair market value of goods or services received is income. The book's carpenter who builds a dentist's cabinets in exchange for braces has income equal to what the dentist would charge (p. 108).
- **Cancelled debt** is income unless it's a gift (or an exclusion applies — bankruptcy, insolvency, certain home or student debt; Chapter 6).
- **Life insurance death benefits** are tax-free. Accelerated death benefits for the terminally ill are tax-free; for the chronically ill, up to a per-diem limit ($420 a day for 2025; the book cites an older $380) or actual long-term care costs.
- **Prizes, awards, lottery, game show winnings** are taxable; certain employee achievement awards (length of service, safety) up to $400, or $1,600 under a qualified plan, are not (p. 109).
- **Gambling winnings** are taxable; losses are deductible only as an itemized deduction up to winnings (from 2026, only 90% of losses).
- **Treasure trove**: the diamond ring in the auction sofa is income at its value when found (p. 109).
- **Illegal income** is taxable — the Al Capone lesson.
- **Jury duty pay** is taxable; if you turn it over to your employer, the repayment is an adjustment.
- **Damages**: compensatory damages for physical injury or sickness are excluded; damages for discrimination or emotional distress without physical injury are taxable (since August 1996) (p. 102).
- **Foreign earned income exclusion**: up to $130,000 for 2025 with Form 2555 (p. 110).
