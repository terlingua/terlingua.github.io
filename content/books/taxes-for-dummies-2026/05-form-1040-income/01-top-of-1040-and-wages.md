- id: digital-assets-question
  prompt: Which situation requires answering "Yes" to the digital assets question on the 2025 Form 1040?
  explanation: The question asks whether, during the year, you received a digital asset (as a reward, award or payment for property or services) or sold, exchanged or otherwise disposed of one. - id: allocated-tips
  prompt: A server's W-2 shows $3,000 in box 8 (allocated tips). How is it handled?

You report it and figure the employee FICA on Form 4137 (Chapter 8). Good tip records can rebut the allocation. - id: statutory-employee
  prompt: Why is it good news when "Statutory employee" is checked in box 13 of your W-2?
  explanation: Statutory employees (certain full-time life insurance agents, commission drivers, traveling salespeople and homeworkers) have FICA withheld like employees but report income and expenses on Schedule C like the self-employed (p. 80). Because miscellaneous itemized deductions — including unreimbursed employee expenses — are gone, ordinary employees get no deduction for job costs. Statutory employees can deduct travel, car and other business costs against that income. They don't owe self-employment tax on it, because FICA was already withheld.

## Learning objectives

- Complete the identity, filing status, digital asset and dependent sections correctly
- Define income and distinguish it from gifts
- Read the important W-2 boxes and explain why boxes 1, 3 and 5 differ

## The ideas

**The top of the form** (pp. 71–74). Filing status; names and SSNs exactly as on Social Security cards (a transposed digit gets an e-filed return rejected); the $3 Presidential Election Campaign Fund checkbox, which doesn't change your tax; the **digital assets question**, which you must answer yes if you received, sold, exchanged or otherwise disposed of crypto, stablecoins or NFTs (merely holding them all year is "no"); boxes for whether someone can claim you as a dependent, whether your spouse itemizes separately, age 65+ (for 2025, born before January 2, 1961 — the book's “January 2, 1959” on p. 74 is a typo) and blindness; and the dependents list, with an SSN and a checkbox for the child tax credit or credit for other dependents.

**What income is.** “Something of value that you receive regardless of whether you work for it or you have an investment that works for it” (p. 74). Gifts are not income to the recipient, because the giver gives up all rights without getting anything in return; but money paid to you for doing things — even by your parent — is compensation (p. 75). Code section 61 starts from "all income from whatever source derived" and the exclusions are carved out by specific provisions.

**Form W-2** arrives by January 31. If it's wrong, ask the employer to correct it; if it never comes, use Form 4852 (substitute W-2 or 1099-R) (pp. 75–77). Key boxes:

- **Box 1** — wages subject to income tax: salary, bonuses, commissions, taxable fringe benefits, excluding pre-tax 401(k) deferrals and pre-tax health premiums. Goes to line 1a.
- **Box 3** — Social Security wages (capped at $176,100 for 2025). Higher than box 1 if you made 401(k) deferrals, because those are still subject to FICA.
- **Box 5** — Medicare wages, no cap.
- **Box 8** — allocated tips: tips assigned to you by the 8% rule that you didn't report; they are *not* in box 1, so you add them (line 1c) and pay FICA via Form 4137 (p. 77).
- **Box 10** — dependent care benefits; up to $5,000 excludable for 2025 ($7,500 from 2026).
- **Box 12** — codes: D (401(k) deferral), W (employer HSA contributions), AA/BB (Roth 401(k)/403(b)), DD (cost of health coverage, informational), C (taxable group-term life over $50,000) and others.
- **Box 13** — statutory employee (report on Schedule C and deduct expenses there), retirement plan participant (affects IRA deductibility), third-party sick pay.

**Tax-free fringe benefits** mentioned in the book: up to $5,250 of employer educational assistance; parking and transit up to $325 a month each for 2025 (p. 79).

**New for 2025**: tips and overtime stay in income on line 1; the deductions come later on Schedule 1-A (p. 77).
