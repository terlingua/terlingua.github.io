## Learning objectives

- Compare the standard mileage rate and actual expenses
- Apply the commuting rules, including home offices, second jobs and temporary job sites
- Keep adequate records

## The ideas

**Two methods** (pp. 269–271): the **standard mileage rate** (70 cents a mile for 2025; 72.5 cents for 2026), which covers gas, depreciation, insurance and repairs, plus parking and tolls; or the business share of **actual expenses** (gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration, depreciation or lease payments, garage rent). For a car you own, choose the standard rate in the first year to keep the option; if you start with actual expenses (MACRS depreciation), you can't switch to the mileage rate later. Self-employed people can deduct the business share of car loan interest.

**Records**: a log of business miles, purpose and odometer readings, kept contemporaneously (weekly entries are acceptable) (p. 269).

**Commuting** (pp. 269–270): home to a regular workplace is personal, however far. Business calls and ads on the car don't change that. But:

- With a home office as your principal place of business, trips from home to clients are business.
- Without a regular office, the first and last trips of the day are commuting.
- Travel between two jobs on the same day is deductible.
- With a regular workplace, trips from home to a temporary work site are deductible.
