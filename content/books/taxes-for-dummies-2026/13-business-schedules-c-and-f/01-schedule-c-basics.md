## Learning objectives

- Know who reports on Schedule C (sole proprietors, single-member LLCs, statutory employees, qualified joint ventures)
- Choose between cash, accrual and hybrid methods and know the small-business gross receipts test
- Answer the material participation question
- Know the 1099 filing duties and thresholds

## The ideas

**Who files** (p. 261): a sole proprietor or a single-member LLC not electing corporate treatment. A married couple who co-own an unincorporated business and both materially participate can elect to be a "qualified joint venture", each filing a Schedule C (and SE) for their share rather than a partnership return, so both get Social Security credit.

**Accounting methods** (pp. 263–264):

- **Cash**: income when received, expenses when paid (a credit card charge counts when charged). Gives control over timing: delay December invoices to January, prepay some expenses in December.
- **Accrual**: income when earned/billed, expenses when incurred.
- **Hybrid**: accrual for inventory items, cash for the rest.
- Businesses with average annual gross receipts at or below the inflation-adjusted threshold for the prior three years can use cash even if they sell goods. The book says $25 million (p. 263), the original 2018 figure; for 2025 it is $31 million. Merchandise is still deducted only when sold, and materials that aren't incidental (a roofer's shingles) are deducted when used or paid for, whichever is later.

**Material participation** (p. 264): if you didn't materially participate, losses are passive (Chapter 15). Seven tests, including 500+ hours, being substantially the only participant, 100+ hours and no one else more, and participation in five of the last ten years.

**Information returns** (p. 265): you must file Forms 1099-NEC (contractors) and 1099-MISC (rent, attorneys, prizes, medical payments) for business payments of $600 or more in 2025 to non-corporate payees (attorneys even if incorporated), by January 31. Payments by card or through payment apps are reported by the processor on 1099-K, so don't double-report. From 2026, the threshold for 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC rises to $2,000 (indexed after 2026).
