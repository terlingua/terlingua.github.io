## Learning objectives

- Apply MACRS recovery periods and the half-year and mid-quarter conventions
- Compare Section 179 expensing and bonus depreciation, with the 2025 limits
- Apply the luxury auto limits, the heavy-vehicle rules and the 50% business-use test

## The ideas

**Depreciation** recovers the cost of business assets with a useful life over a year (p. 272). Land and art aren't depreciable. MACRS classes (p. 273): computers, cars, light trucks 5 years; office furniture 7; land improvements 15; residential rental buildings 27.5 (straight-line, mid-month); nonresidential buildings 39; qualified improvement property 15; intangibles like goodwill amortized over 15.

**Conventions** (pp. 277–278): half-year (everything placed in service treated as mid-year: 5-year property 20%, 32%, 19.2%, 11.52%, 11.52%, 5.76%); mid-quarter if more than 40% of the year's property (excluding real estate) was placed in service in the fourth quarter.

**Section 179** (pp. 273, 278–279): elect to expense up to **$2,500,000** of qualifying property for 2025 (OBBBA), reduced dollar for dollar above **$4,000,000** of purchases (gone at $6,500,000). Limited to taxable income from active businesses (including wages), with the excess carried forward; can't create a loss. MFS spouses split one limit.

**Bonus depreciation** (pp. 272–273): automatic unless you elect out; can create a loss (NOL). OBBBA restored **100%** permanently for property *acquired after January 19, 2025*; property acquired earlier but placed in service in 2025 gets the phase-down rate of 40%. (The book describes 100% for all 2025 property.)

**Vehicles** (pp. 279–283): passenger autos (6,000 lbs gross vehicle weight rating or less) are capped: for 2025, first-year $12,200, or $20,200 with bonus; then $19,600, $11,800 and $7,060 a year. Multiply caps by business-use percentage. Business use of 50% or less: straight-line only, no 179 or bonus; dropping to 50% or below later triggers recapture. Heavier SUVs, trucks and vans (over 6,000 lbs **gross vehicle weight rating**, up to 14,000) escape the caps; but Section 179 on an SUV is capped at **$31,300** for 2025 (bonus can cover the rest). *Corrections:* the book describes the 6,000-pound test as unloaded weight (it's the loaded GVWR) and says a $42,000 business share of a heavy SUV can be fully expensed under Section 179; the SUV Section 179 cap is $31,300, though 100% bonus depreciation (for vehicles acquired after January 19, 2025) can deduct the rest.
