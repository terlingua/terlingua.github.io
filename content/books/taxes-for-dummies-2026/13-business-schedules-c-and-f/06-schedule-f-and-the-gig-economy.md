## Learning objectives

- Know what Schedule F covers and its special rules
- Apply the farmer estimated tax rule and income averaging
- Use the farm optional method for SE tax and the rules for hiring your children
- Treat gig work as a business

## The ideas

**Schedule F** (pp. 295–303) reports crops, livestock, dairy, poultry, fish farming, orchards, nurseries and similar income. Most farmers use the cash method. Special items: conservation expenses (deductible up to 25% of gross farm income if consistent with a government plan, excess carried forward); custom hire; depreciation options including the alternative depreciation system; farm NOLs that may be carried back two years.

**Income averaging** (Schedule J) lets farmers and fishermen spread elected farm income over the prior three years' brackets (p. 295).

**Estimated tax** (p. 301): if at least two-thirds of gross income is from farming or fishing, one payment by mid-January (the smaller of 66⅔% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's) suffices — or none if you file and pay by March 1.

**Farm optional method for SE tax** (p. 302): with low gross or net farm income you may report two-thirds of gross farm income (up to a limit) as SE earnings to build Social Security credits and increase earned-income-based credits.

**Hiring your children** (p. 301): wages paid by a parent's sole proprietorship (or a partnership of the parents) to a child under 18 aren't subject to Social Security and Medicare tax, are deductible if reasonable for real work, and are sheltered by the child's standard deduction ($15,750 for 2025; the book uses $15,000). Wages can also fund a Roth IRA for the child.

**Hobby presumption for horses**: two profitable years of seven (p. 302).

**Gig economy** (p. 304): rideshare and delivery drivers, short-term rental hosts and freelancers are in business: track income from all platforms and deduct expenses (mileage, phone, fees, supplies), pay self-employment tax and quarterly estimates. (Short-term rentals of a home may go on Schedule E or C depending on services provided; Chapter 15.)
