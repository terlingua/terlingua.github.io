## Learning objectives

- Know which form feeds each of Schedule 1 lines 3–7
- Handle unemployment compensation and its repayment
- Explain the claim-of-right rule for repayments over $3,000

## The ideas

**Lines 3–6** are summaries of other schedules (pp. 115–116): line 3 business income from Schedule C (Chapter 13); line 4 gains and losses on business property from Form 4797 (for example, selling a depreciated rental); line 5 rental real estate, royalties, partnerships, S corporations, estates and trusts from Schedule E (Chapter 15); line 6 farm income from Schedule F. An occasional fee with no expenses can go on line 8z as other income, but if you are in business, Schedule C lets you deduct expenses (and requires self-employment tax).

**Line 7: unemployment compensation** is fully taxable (Form 1099-G box 1). You can choose 10% withholding (Form W-4V). Repaid in the same year → report the net and write "REPAID" (p. 117).

**Repaid in a later year.** If the repayment is more than $3,000, the claim-of-right rule (section 1341) lets you choose the better of an itemized deduction or a credit equal to the tax the income caused in the earlier year. *Correction:* for repayments of $3,000 or less the book says to deduct them on Schedule A (p. 117); that was a miscellaneous itemized deduction subject to the 2% floor, which has been unavailable since 2018, so small repayments generally give no federal deduction.
