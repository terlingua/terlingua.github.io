## Learning objectives

- Apply the bona fide residence and physical presence tests
- Know the treatment of HSA, ABLE and 529 distributions, prizes, hobbies and personal property rentals
- Know how stock options and ESPP shares are taxed

## The ideas

**Foreign earned income exclusion** (pp. 120–121). Up to $130,000 for 2025. Qualify by being a **bona fide resident** of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period including a full calendar year, or **physically present** abroad 330 full days in any 12 consecutive months. Prorate in partial years. US government employees and work in US territories or Antarctica don't qualify. You may need an extension while waiting to meet a test. Report wages on line 1 and the exclusion as a negative on Schedule 1 line 8d.

**HSA distributions** not used for qualified medical expenses are taxable (line 8f) and, if you are under 65 and not disabled, subject to an extra 20% tax. **Archer MSAs** and per-diem long-term care benefits above actual costs or the per-diem limit go through Form 8853 (line 8e).

**Hobbies** (p. 122). Income from an activity not engaged in for profit goes on line 8j. *Correction:* the book says hobby expenses can be deducted on Schedule A up to hobby income; since 2018 those expenses have been nondeductible miscellaneous itemized deductions (OBBBA made it permanent). Only the cost of goods sold reduces gross income. So a hobby with $3,000 of sales and $3,000 of expenses (other than goods sold) produces $3,000 of taxable income.

**Prizes and awards** are taxable at fair market value (line 8i); employer awards go on the W-2. **Olympic and Paralympic medals and US Olympic Committee prize money** are excluded if AGI is $1 million or less ($500,000 MFS) (p. 124).

**Stock options** (p. 123):

- **Incentive stock options (ISOs)**: no regular tax at grant or exercise; the spread at exercise is an AMT adjustment. A **qualifying disposition** (sold more than two years after grant and one year after exercise) makes the whole gain long-term capital gain; a disqualifying disposition turns the spread into ordinary income. Form 3921 reports exercises.
- **Employee stock purchase plans (Section 423)**: discount and holding-period rules split the eventual gain between ordinary income and capital gain; Form 3922.
- **Nonstatutory options (NSOs)**: almost always no readily ascertainable value at grant, so the spread at exercise is ordinary wage income (on the W-2 for employees), and later appreciation is capital gain.

**Other items** (pp. 124–127): rental of personal property for profit when not in that business (8l); taxable ABLE distributions (8q); scholarships used for room, board or stipends (8r); Medicaid waiver difficulty-of-care payments, which are included and then backed out (8s); nonqualified deferred compensation (8t); prison wages (8u). Not taxable: child support, gifts and bequests, life insurance proceeds.
