## Learning objectives

- Report gambling winnings and deduct losses correctly
- Explain when cancelled debt is income and the main exclusions
- Distinguish recourse and nonrecourse foreclosures

## The ideas

**Gambling** (p. 119). All winnings are income (line 8b), whether or not you get a Form W-2G; withholding is 24% when required. Losses are deductible only as an itemized deduction ("other itemized deductions", Schedule A line 16), only up to winnings, and only with records: losing tickets, logs, statements. Non-itemizers pay tax on gross winnings. From 2026, OBBBA limits the deduction to 90% of losses (still capped at winnings).

**Cancellation of debt (COD)** (pp. 119–120). Forgiven debt is generally income (Form 1099-C for $600+). Main exclusions (Form 982): bankruptcy; insolvency (to the extent liabilities exceed assets just before the discharge); qualified farm and real property business debt; certain student loan discharges (death or disability, public service and profession-based forgiveness, closed-school and borrower-defense discharges); and **qualified principal residence indebtedness** up to $750,000 — which *expired* for discharges after December 31, 2025 (unless under a written agreement entered into before 2026). The broad 2021–2025 exclusion for all student loan forgiveness also ended after 2025; OBBBA made the death-and-disability exclusion permanent.

**Foreclosures.** With a **recourse** loan (you're personally liable), the transaction splits in two: the excess of debt over the property's fair market value is COD income, and FMV minus basis is a gain or loss on sale. With a **nonrecourse** loan (lender can only take the property), there is no COD income; the whole debt is the amount realised, which may produce a gain (p. 119). A home sale gain may qualify for the $250,000/$500,000 exclusion; a loss on a personal residence is not deductible. Lenders sometimes leave the FMV box blank on Form 1099-C — check it.
