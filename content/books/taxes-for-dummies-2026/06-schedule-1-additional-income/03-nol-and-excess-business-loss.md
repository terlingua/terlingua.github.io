## Learning objectives

- Explain what an NOL is and how carryovers work after the TCJA
- Apply the 80% limit
- Apply the excess business loss limit, with the 2025 and 2026 thresholds

## The ideas

**NOL.** When business losses exceed income for the year, the excess is a net operating loss (p. 118). For losses arising in 2018 and later, NOLs carry **forward indefinitely** (no carryback, except farming losses — two years — and the special five-year carryback for 2018–2020 losses under the CARES Act). For years after 2020, an NOL carryforward can offset at most **80% of taxable income** (computed before the NOL and QBI deductions). Pre-2018 NOLs are not subject to the 80% limit. The carryforward appears as a negative amount on Schedule 1 line 8a.

**Excess business loss (EBL), section 461(l).** Noncorporate taxpayers can't use net business losses beyond a threshold to offset nonbusiness income (wages, investment income) in the current year. The disallowed excess becomes an NOL carryforward (p. 124). OBBBA made the rule permanent. The book cites $270,000/$540,000; the 2025 thresholds are **$313,000** single and **$626,000** joint. OBBBA reset the base, so for 2026 they are **$256,000** and **$512,000**.
