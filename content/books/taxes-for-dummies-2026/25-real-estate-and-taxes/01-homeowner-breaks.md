## Learning objectives

- Apply the mortgage interest and SALT limits as of 2025–2026
- Apply the home sale exclusion, including the nonqualified use trap (a major correction)
- Know that home losses aren't deductible

## The ideas

**Mortgage interest** (p. 528): deductible on up to **$750,000** of acquisition debt on a main and second home ($375,000 MFS); $1 million for debt incurred before December 16, 2017. OBBBA made the $750,000 limit permanent and, from 2026, treats mortgage insurance premiums as deductible interest.

**SALT** (p. 528): property taxes plus state income (or sales) taxes are capped at **$40,000** for 2025 ($20,000 MFS), rising 1% a year ($40,400 for 2026) through 2029, reduced by 30% of MAGI above $500,000 ($505,000 in 2026) but not below $10,000; back to $10,000 in 2030.

**Home sale exclusion** (pp. 528–529): $250,000 / $500,000 if owned and used as a main home 2 of the last 5 years; excess gain is taxable (inflation isn't indexed into the limits, so keep improvement records). Holding until death wipes out the gain via step-up (p. 529).

**Converting a rental to your home** (p. 530): *Correction:* the book says that moving into a rental for two years shields the profit. Since 2009, gain is allocated to periods of **nonqualified use** (time after 2008 when the property wasn't your main home, before you moved in) and that part isn't excludable. Depreciation claimed after May 6, 1997 is also taxable (the book says May 7). Example: own a rental for 6 years, then live in it 4 years, gain $300,000: 6/10 of the gain ($180,000) is nonqualified-use gain and taxable (plus depreciation recapture); the rest can be excluded. (Time after you move *out* within the 5-year window doesn't count as nonqualified use, so a home rented for up to three years after you leave keeps the full exclusion.)

**Losses** on your home aren't deductible (p. 530).
