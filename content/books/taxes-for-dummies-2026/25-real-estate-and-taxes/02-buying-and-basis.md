## Learning objectives

- Estimate the tax value of owning, including when the standard deduction makes it zero
- Add closing costs and improvements to basis; distinguish repairs
- Know the 14-day rental exclusion

## The ideas

**Tax savings** (pp. 531–532): the book's shortcut multiplies your marginal rate by mortgage interest and property tax. That only applies to amounts that push your itemized deductions above the standard deduction ($31,500 joint for 2025), so many buyers get much less benefit than the shortcut implies. Adjust withholding after buying.

**Buy only if you'll stay** at least three to five years: transaction costs mean a home must appreciate about 15% to break even (p. 530). Lenders tell you the maximum you can borrow, not what you can afford (p. 532).

**Basis** (pp. 533–535): purchase price plus settlement costs such as title fees, legal fees, recording fees, surveys, transfer taxes, owner's title insurance and amounts you paid that the seller owed; plus **improvements** (additions, new roof, alarm system, landscaping, fences, new appliances) including contractor labor (not your own time). Repairs and maintenance (fixing leaks, repainting) don't add basis.

**Renting your home under 15 days** a year (p. 535): rent is tax-free and not reported (Chapter 15).

**Vacation homes** (p. 534) are mostly consumption, not investments; no exclusion applies to their gains.
