## Learning objectives

- Evaluate cash-value life insurance and ILITs for estate liquidity
- Know what living trusts do and don't do
- Use qualified disclaimers and charitable trusts
- Find objective advice

## The ideas

**Life insurance** (pp. 566–567): most people need term insurance. Cash-value policies cost far more and pay high commissions. An **irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT)** keeps proceeds out of the taxable estate (new policies owned by the trust from the start; transfers of existing policies within three years of death are pulled back in). Useful when an illiquid estate (a family business) faces estate tax.

**Living trusts** (p. 568): revocable trusts avoid probate (cost, delay, publicity) and help with incapacity, but **don't reduce estate taxes** by themselves. Beneficiary designations, joint tenancy with survivorship and payable-on-death accounts also avoid probate. Most useful for older, single people with probate assets; small estates may use simplified probate.

**Crummey trusts** (p. 564): gifts qualify for the annual exclusion because beneficiaries get a temporary withdrawal right (typically 30–60 days).

**Disclaimers** (p. 569): a beneficiary can refuse an inheritance so it passes as if they'd predeceased — useful for post-mortem planning (e.g., filling a bypass trust or using a state exemption). A qualified disclaimer must be in writing, irrevocable, made **within nine months** of death (or of turning 21), before accepting any benefit, and the disclaimant can't direct where the assets go.

**Charitable trusts** (p. 569): a **charitable remainder trust** pays you (or others) income for life or years, then goes to charity; you get a partial deduction now and can contribute appreciated assets the trust sells without immediate tax. A **charitable lead trust** pays charity first and passes the remainder to heirs at reduced transfer-tax cost.

**Advice** (p. 570): avoid conflicted sources (insurance sellers, money managers, free seminars); use fee-only estate attorneys or tax advisors; software like Nolo's can handle simple wills and trusts. Talk with your family.
