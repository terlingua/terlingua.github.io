## Learning objectives

- Know the federal exemption and rate for 2025–2027
- Understand state estate and inheritance taxes (with updates)
- Compute a taxable estate in outline

## The ideas

**Federal estate tax** (p. 560): exemption **$13.99 million** per person for 2025; OBBBA set it permanently at **$15 million** for 2026, indexed ($15.48 million for 2027). Top rate **40%**. The exemption is unified with lifetime taxable gifts. *Correction to the book's history* (p. 560): in 2010 estates could elect no estate tax (with carryover basis instead of step-up), and for 2011 the exemption became **$5 million**, not $1 million as the book says.

**State taxes** (pp. 560–561): many states tax estates far below the federal level (Oregon $1 million, Massachusetts $2 million). As of 2025, 12 states and DC levy estate taxes and **five** states levy inheritance taxes (Iowa repealed its inheritance tax for deaths from 2025, so the book's "six" is out of date); Maryland has both. Washington raised its top estate tax rate to **35%** (with a $3 million exemption) for deaths from July 1, 2025, above the 20% the book cites.

**Taxable estate** (p. 561): everything you own or control at death — homes, investments, retirement accounts, business interests, and life insurance you own — minus debts, funeral and administration expenses, the unlimited marital deduction and charitable bequests, plus prior taxable gifts in the computation.

**Wills and powers of attorney** (p. 562): wills don't save tax or avoid probate, but control distribution and name guardians for minor children; dying intestate lets state law decide. Add a living will, health care power of attorney and durable financial power of attorney.
