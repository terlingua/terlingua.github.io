## Learning objectives

- Use the annual exclusion, the tuition and medical exclusions and Form 709
- Decide what assets to give versus hold until death
- Apply the unlimited marital deduction and its limits
- Compare bypass trusts with portability (an important omission)

## The ideas

**Annual exclusion** (pp. 563–564): **$19,000** per recipient for 2025 and 2026 ($20,000 for 2027), per donor; spouses can each give. Gifts above it require **Form 709** but usually no tax — they reduce your lifetime exemption. Unlimited exclusions for **tuition** and **medical** expenses paid directly to the institution or provider. Gifts to political organizations and (generally) to charity aren't taxable gifts.

**GST tax** (p. 563): *clarification* — the generation-skipping transfer tax doesn't hit every gift to a grandchild above $19,000; transfers to skip persons beyond the annual exclusion first use your **GST exemption** (equal to the estate exemption, $15 million in 2026), and only above that is the 40% GST tax due.

**What to give** (p. 564): cash or assets that haven't appreciated; sell losers first and give the cash (to use the loss); **hold highly appreciated assets until death** for the stepped-up basis (or give them to charity). Gifts carry over the donor's basis.

**Marital deduction** (p. 565): unlimited for transfers to a **U.S. citizen** spouse. A non-citizen spouse needs a qualified domestic trust (QDOT) at death; lifetime gifts to a non-citizen spouse are limited to an enhanced annual exclusion ($190,000 for 2025).

**Bypass trust vs portability** (pp. 565–566): the book recommends a bypass (credit shelter) trust so both spouses' exemptions are used. *Important omission:* since 2011 a surviving spouse can **elect portability** of the deceased spouse's unused exemption (DSUE) by filing a timely estate tax return (Form 706), even if no tax is due; the IRS allows a simplified late election within five years of death. Portability makes bypass trusts unnecessary for many couples at the *federal* level, but bypass trusts remain useful for state estate taxes (most states don't allow portability), shielding post-death growth, GST planning, asset protection and blended families. Portability doesn't give a second step-up for assets in a bypass trust; assets left outright get a step-up at the survivor's death.
