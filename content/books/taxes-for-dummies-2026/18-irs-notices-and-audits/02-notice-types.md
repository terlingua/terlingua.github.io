## Learning objectives

- Respond to CP2000 and nonfiler notices
- Know the backup withholding rate (with a correction)
- Understand liens, levies, exempt property and the bank-levy holding period (with corrections)
- Use Collection Due Process hearings

## The ideas

**CP2000 / CP2501** (pp. 398–399): the IRS's matching program compares your return with W-2s, 1099s and 1098s, usually 12–15 months after filing. It *assumes* third-party data are right; often it isn't (the book's $820 entered as $82,000, p. 398). Common innocent causes: income reported on a different schedule, a child's account under your SSN, income in a different year, basis missing from a 1099-B. Respond by the deadline with an explanation and documents.

**Nonfiler notices** (CP515, CP518): the IRS thinks you had to file. Answer even if you weren't required to.

**Backup withholding** (pp. 399–400): payers must withhold if you don't provide a correct TIN or the IRS says you underreported interest and dividends. *Correction:* the rate is **24%** since 2018, not 28% as the book says (p. 399).

**Federal tax lien** (pp. 400–401): arises automatically on all your property when you don't pay after demand; the IRS files a public Notice of Federal Tax Lien to get priority over other creditors. It must be released within 30 days after the debt is paid. *Correction:* the book says liens stay on credit reports for 7 to 15 years; since 2018 the three national credit bureaus don't include tax liens in credit reports (though liens are public records and still affect borrowing and sales of property).

**Collection Due Process** (p. 401): after a lien filing or a final levy notice, you have **30 days** to request a CDP hearing (Form 12153) with IRS Appeals, where you can propose an installment agreement, offer in compromise or innocent spouse relief, and challenge the tax itself only if you never had a prior chance. Appeals' determination can go to the Tax Court.

**Levies** (pp. 401–403): a bank levy seizes what's in the account on that day — *correction:* the bank must hold the funds for **21 days** before sending them (not 30); use that window to resolve it. A wage levy is continuous, leaving you only an exempt amount based on your standard deduction and dependents (IRS Publication 1494). Exempt: a principal residence for levies of $5,000 or less (larger ones need court approval), clothing and schoolbooks, limited furniture and personal effects and tools of a trade (amounts indexed annually), child support you must pay, unemployment benefits, certain disability and welfare payments.
