## Learning objectives

- Trace the sequence from first notice to final notice of intent to levy
- Read a notice and get your account transcript
- Know when a Statutory Notice of Deficiency is and isn't required
- Apply the 36-month interest suspension rule

## The ideas

**Three Ps**: promptness, persistence and patience (p. 393). Notices usually start with a proposed adjustment — a **CP2000** (income mismatch), a math-error notice (e.g., CP11/CP12/CP13 "we changed your account"), or a **30-day letter** after an audit (p. 394). If you don't respond or don't win, the IRS issues a **Statutory Notice of Deficiency** (90-day letter) before it can assess additional tax from changes to income, deductions or credits. Math or clerical errors and penalties don't require one (but you can request abatement of a math-error assessment within 60 days, forcing normal deficiency procedures).

**Balance-due sequence** (pp. 394–395): first notice (CP14) → reminders (CP501/CP503) → **CP504** (intent to levy, including state refunds) → **Final Notice of Intent to Levy and Notice of Your Right to a Hearing** (Letter 1058 / LT11), sent by certified mail, giving 30 days to pay or request a Collection Due Process hearing. Interest runs throughout; penalties grow.

**Reading a notice** (p. 396): check the SSN, tax year, form, amounts, payments credited. If unclear, get your **account transcript** from your IRS Online Account (faster than calling).

**36-month interest suspension** (p. 403): if you filed on time and the IRS doesn't send a notice of additional tax within 36 months of filing, interest stops after 36 months until 21 days after the notice. Doesn't apply to failure-to-file/pay penalties, fraud, or (per the book) audit adjustments.

**Scams**: the IRS initiates contact by mail. It doesn't demand payment by gift card, wire or crypto, or threaten immediate arrest by phone. Verify notices in your Online Account.
