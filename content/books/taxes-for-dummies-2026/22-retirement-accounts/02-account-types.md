## Learning objectives

- Know the 2025 limits for 401(k), 403(b), SIMPLE, SEP, solo 401(k) and IRAs (with corrections)
- Understand plan coverage rules for employees and startup credits
- Decide between traditional and Roth contributions

## The ideas

**2025 limits** (pp. 477–482):

- **401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b)** employee deferrals: **$23,500**; catch-up age 50+ **$7,500**; ages 60–63 **$11,250** instead (if the plan allows). *Correction:* the book says 403(b) deferrals are limited to "20 percent or $23,500 of their salaries, whichever is less" — there's no 20% cap on deferrals (the overall cap is 100% of pay up to $70,000 including employer money); and the 50+ figure of $31,000 it labels 2023 is the 2025 figure. 403(b) employees with 15+ years at certain employers may add up to $3,000 a year ($15,000 lifetime).
- **SIMPLE IRA**: $16,500; 50+ $20,000; 60–63 $21,750. Employer: 3% match or 2% nonelective for all eligible.
- **SEP-IRA**: up to 25% of pay (about 20% of a self-employed person's net SE earnings) up to **$70,000**; employer-only money; must cover eligible employees at the same percentage.
- **Solo (individual) 401(k)**: for owners (and spouses) without other eligible employees. *Correction:* the book's description (p. 480) is muddled. The owner can make an **employee deferral** (pre-tax or Roth) of $23,500 (+ catch-up) **plus** an **employer profit-sharing** contribution of up to 20% of net SE earnings, with the total capped at $70,000 (+ catch-up). That's why a solo 401(k) usually beats a SEP at moderate incomes.
- **Defined benefit plans**: actuarially determined, can far exceed $70,000 for older high earners.
- **IRAs**: $7,000 (+$1,000 at 50+), combined across traditional and Roth.

**Coverage**: a business owner with a plan must cover eligible employees (p. 479); SECURE (2019) and SECURE 2.0 offer startup credits up to $5,000 a year for three years, plus auto-enrollment and employer contribution credits.

**Traditional vs Roth** (pp. 478, 483–484): the book generally favours pre-tax contributions ("the sure tax break"), and Roth when your current bracket is low or you already have large pre-tax balances. Roth IRA 2025 phase-outs: $236,000–$246,000 joint, $150,000–$165,000 single; the backdoor route remains available. Roth accounts have no lifetime RMDs (including Roth 401(k)s from 2024). Note that from 2026, catch-up contributions for employees who earned over $145,000 (indexed; $150,000 for 2026) in FICA wages from the employer the prior year must be Roth.
