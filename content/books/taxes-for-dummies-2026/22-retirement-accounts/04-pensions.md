## Learning objectives

- Decide when to start a pension and whether to take a lump sum
- Compare single-life and joint-and-survivor options

## The ideas

**Start age** (p. 489): earlier starts pay less per month; actuaries price the options fairly on average, so decide based on need, health and whether you're still earning (pension income on top of wages is taxed at higher rates).

**Lump sum vs annuity** (p. 490): a lump sum rolled directly into an IRA isn't taxed until withdrawn; it gives control and inheritability, but you bear investment and longevity risk. Monthly payments insure against outliving your money.

**Married options** (pp. 490–491): single life pays most but nothing to the survivor; 50%, 66⅔%, 75% and 100% joint-and-survivor options pay less now and more later. Federal law requires a spouse's written, notarised consent to waive the survivor annuity for qualified plans. The book warns against "pension maximisation" (single life plus life insurance), which usually costs more than choosing a survivor option (p. 492).
