## Learning objectives

- Roll over employer plans without 20% withholding or the once-a-year IRA trap
- Understand annuities' tax trade-offs
- Apply RMD ages, tables and the still-working exception (with corrections)
- Use QCDs from age 70½ and apply the 10-year inherited account rules

## The ideas

**Nonqualified annuities** (pp. 484–485): unlimited after-tax contributions grow tax-deferred, no RMDs during life, but earnings are taxed as ordinary income (losing capital gain rates and the step-up at death), fees are high, and the 10% penalty applies before 59½. Consider only after maxing other accounts, and for long horizons (15–20+ years).

**Rollovers** (pp. 485–487): use a **direct rollover** or trustee-to-trustee transfer. If a plan pays you, it must withhold **20%**, and you must replace that 20% from other funds within **60 days** to roll over the full amount. IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollovers are limited to **one per 12 months** (direct transfers are unlimited). (FINRA has replaced the NASD the book mentions.)

**RMDs** (pp. 481, 488): begin for the year you reach **73** (75 if born in 1960 or later); the first can be delayed to April 1 of the next year. *Corrections:* most owners must use the IRS **Uniform Lifetime Table** — you don't choose between single and joint life expectancy (the Joint Life table applies only if your spouse is the sole beneficiary and more than 10 years younger). The **still-working exception** delays RMDs only from the *current* employer's plan (and not for 5%+ owners); IRAs, including SEP and SIMPLE IRAs, still require RMDs. Missed RMDs incur a 25% excise tax (10% if corrected timely).

**Qualified charitable distributions** (p. 488): *Correction:* available from **age 70½** (not 73), directly from an IRA to charity, up to **$108,000** for 2025 ($111,000 for 2026), excluded from income and counting toward RMDs — valuable for those taking the standard deduction.

**Inherited accounts** (p. 488): for deaths after 2019, most nonspouse beneficiaries must empty the account by the end of the **10th year**; if the owner died after their required beginning date, annual RMDs are also required in years 1–9 (enforced from 2025). Eligible designated beneficiaries (spouse, the owner's minor child until 21, disabled or chronically ill, not more than 10 years younger) can stretch.
