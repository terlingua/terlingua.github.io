## Learning objectives

- Answer Atwood's riddle using men's fear of other men
- State Kimmel's four rules of masculinity
- Explain misogyny as part of culture, not only a personality flaw
- Explain Terrence Real's "normal traumatisation of boys"
- Explain Bruce's insight that a polite request can carry the threat of violence

## The ideas

**Atwood answered.** Boys and men are the main victims and perpetrators of general violence, so they are acutely aware of their place in the pecking order (p. 128). Being humiliated by a woman means being "emasculated, revealed as weak, and made vulnerable to the ridicule, control and violence of other men". "Nothing represents 'weakness' under patriarchy like being a girl."

**Rules.** Kimmel: rule one is "No sissy stuff" — masculinity is "the relentless repudiation of the feminine". Then: be a big deal; be a "sturdy oak" (boys don't cry); give 'em hell (take risks) (pp. 128–129). Pressure to prove you're not a girl is "propulsion fuel for misogyny".

**Misogyny as sea.** Johnson: misogyny is "part of patriarchal culture. We're like fish swimming in a sea laced with it" (p. 129); it shapes women's beliefs too (that women are less competent, better suited to care).

**Normal traumatisation.** Terrence Real's three-year-old son came downstairs as the "good witch" in a white dress; older boys' silent stares said "You are not to do this"; he never touched the dress again (p. 129). Real: "The way we 'turn boys into men' is through injury"; "Disconnection is not fallout from traditional masculinity. Disconnection is masculinity" (pp. 129–130). Tim Winton hears boys pressured "to enlist … and join the Shithead Army". Kimmel: if autonomy is the highest goal, men hate their dependent parts and may hate women who elicit them — "You make me weak in the knees … I hate you for it" (p. 130).

**Bruce.** Taught as a small boy never to cry ("I'll give you something to cry about"), he was violent towards his wife, slapping and later attacking her weekly (pp. 130–131). Being told he hated women "did not help at all"; his problem was a "very limited vocabulary" for emotion (p. 132). His insight: "If you've only got two modes … one of which is a polite request, the other being violence—the polite request is the threat of violence." With years of therapy he has stopped being violent and remains on guard.
