## Learning objectives

- Explain how the chapter connects patriarchy, eroticised subordination and modern pornography
- Explain why two studies found aggression in porn at 88% versus 1.9%
- State what is and is not known about links between porn and sexual violence

## The ideas

Patriarchy trains men to "put women in their place"; that power has "an erotic quality", which the porn industry has turned into a business (p. 132). Hardcore "gonzo" has gone mainstream: slapping, choking, gagging and name-calling; "aggression is now the default setting" (pp. 132–133). Veteran performers describe escalation over a decade.

**Two numbers.** One study of the top fifty videos found **88%** of scenes included physical aggression, 94% directed at women, who were shown as not minding or liking it. Another found **1.9%**. The difference is **definition**: the second counted aggression only if clearly intended to harm and resisted by the performer — but in porn, women are scripted not to resist (p. 133). Gail Dines (*Pornland*): a fantasy world where women are turned on by degradation; it "looks more like sexual assault than making love". Choking — a homicide red flag — is common.

**Children.** An American campaign group says the average age of first exposure is eleven; a BBFC study found over 60% of 11–13-year-olds had seen porn "unintentionally" and over half wanted to be locked out of adult sites (p. 134). The Children's Commissioner for England heard young people's expectations of sex drawn from porn and found "compelling evidence that too many boys believe that they have an absolute entitlement to sex".

**What's known.** "Research is inconclusive on whether there is a direct link" between hardcore porn and sexual violence (p. 134). Sexual assault services report rising severity; the Gold Coast Centre Against Sexual Violence reported a 56% rise in emergency referrals over five years and victims commonly said abusers were regular porn users. Hill argues that if we accept that uncritical portrayals of violence and sexism shape norms, porn deserves the same scrutiny (p. 135).

## If you build things

The 88% vs 1.9% gap is a lesson in operational definitions. A metric that requires visible "resistance" will undercount coercion in any context where the coerced party has been scripted or pressured not to resist — a point that applies equally to incident-reporting systems and content moderation.
