## Learning objectives

- Explain why "gender inequality" alone cannot explain domestic abuse (the Nordic data)
- Define patriarchy as Kimmel's "dual system" and Allan Johnson's four elements
- Explain why individual men can feel powerless while men as a group are dominant
- Explain "humiliation plus entitlement" and the incel phenomenon

## The ideas

**An invisible mainframe.** Patriarchy "sets parameters around 'acceptable' behaviour for both genders": men "strong, independent, unemotional, logical and confident", women "expressive, nurturant, weak and dependent", and makes injustices seem natural (p. 123). After the 1970s we "stopped talking about it"; "gender equality" became the polite euphemism. Bob Pease: gender inequality "cannot capture the nuance, complexity and multidimensional nature of patriarchy".

**Nordic data.** If equality were a panacea, the Nordic countries should have low rates; instead about 30% of women report physical or sexual partner violence, above the EU average of 22% (p. 123).

**#MeToo** (late 2017) made the "invisible mainframe" visible, and showed many men what women face (David Leser: "I had absolutely no idea") (p. 124). Men, unlike women, have clung to old definitions; Michael Ian Black: boys "trapped" without language for their feelings (pp. 124–125). Patriarchy is "a dud deal for men, too". Others are seething — "you're not really included in anything" — and taking it out on families.

**Men sold a lie.** Obey masculinity's rules and you get power and a house with a white picket fence; that system no longer exists (and never did for some). Frustrated men may look "to their own home as a place to restore their lost power" (p. 125).

**Kimmel's definition.** "A dual system of power: men's power over women, and some men's power over other men" (p. 126).

**Allan Johnson's four elements** (*The Gender Knot*): society is (1) **male-dominated** (men hold power); (2) **male-identified** (masculinity as the norm: control, strength, competitiveness, logic, autonomy; "feminine" values — cooperation, empathy, vulnerability, intuition — undervalued); (3) **male-centred** (attention on men's exploits); (4) organised around an **obsession with control** (p. 126). Control is "the standard by which men are measured"; on a spectrum, the same behaviour that is abuse at one end defines a "successful man" at the other. Kay Schubach: corporate traits — barking orders, no remorse — are "completely inappropriate in the family" (p. 127).

**Felt powerlessness.** "While men are powerful as a group, they do not necessarily feel powerful as individuals" (p. 127). Kimmel: the essence of patriarchal masculinity is not that men feel powerful but that they **feel entitled to power**. Women are shamed too but "don't go off on shooting sprees" because they don't feel entitled to power: for men "it's humiliation plus entitlement … 'I don't feel empowered, but I should'" (pp. 127–128).

**Incels.** "Involuntarily celibate" men in online forums blame "Stacys" for denying them sex as a right; since 2014 three self-proclaimed incels, including Elliot Rodger, have carried out mass shootings in North America (p. 128).
