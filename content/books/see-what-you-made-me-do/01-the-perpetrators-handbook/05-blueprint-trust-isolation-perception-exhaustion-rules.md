## Learning objectives

- Explain why establishing love and trust is the first stage of coercive control
- Describe how isolation and monopolising perception work in domestic settings
- Define gaslighting and explain how it produces debility and exhaustion
- Explain the function of trivial, ever-changing rules

## The ideas

Hill maps Biderman's chart onto domestic abuse. The techniques are used "to varying extents by all abusers"; it is their "cumulative effect" that devastates, and "The longer a woman stays, the harder—and more dangerous—it becomes to leave" (p. 23). Women with insecure reactors will recognise some, not all.

**1. Establish love and trust.** In Korea the "most insidious" technique was false friendship: a handshake and a pat on the back (p. 23). Domestic abusers usually do not court with intent to control; when an abusive man says he loves her "he probably means it", but it is a love "defined and distorted by his deeply held sense of entitlement". Lundy Bancroft: what he feels as love is "largely … the desire to have you devote your life to keeping him happy with no outside interference" (p. 24).

**2. Isolate.** "As long as the victim maintains meaningful social and emotional connections, the abuser's influence is diluted" (p. 24). Unlike POWs, domestic victims are isolated gradually: "Jasmine", seventeen, was told white pants made her look like a "tart"; advice "calcified into hard and fast rules". Methods: geographic moves; making visits difficult; behaving so badly that friends stop coming; colluding with a parent; or the victim isolating herself out of shame. Weary loved ones "do the perpetrator's job for him" (p. 25). Possessiveness is framed as passion; cutting ties becomes proof of love.

**3. Monopolise perception.** Like a magician, the abuser redirects attention "away from his abuse to her faults: if she wasn't so this, he wouldn't be so that" (p. 26). If he seems kind to others, she concludes she must be provoking him. Guilt becomes **shame** — not "I did something bad" but "I am bad" — which cuts her off from her instincts and makes his opinion more important; each act against her instincts multiplies shame and dependency. She searches for explanations (drink, stress, a cheating ex) that become excuses; she tries to fix him. "The abuser's most skilful trick is to make his abuse invisible" (p. 27).

**4. Induce debility and exhaustion.** Biderman: captors relied on "the poor understanding of the victim of what is happening to him" (p. 27). The domestic name is **gaslighting**: "when an abuser knowingly denies, fabricates and manipulates situations to make his partner doubt her own memory and perception", after the 1944 film *Gaslight*. Examples: Terri, pushed to the ground and then persuaded she wasn't; Kay Schubach, whose keys and cash vanished while he asked "Are you losing your mind?", and whose abuser kept an antidepressant prescription to discredit her in court (p. 28). Also sleep deprivation, interrogations, emptying the hot water before a child's bath, loving texts followed by punishment for replying in kind, silent treatment for days or years. The result is "walking on eggshells" — hypervigilance — and abuse so finely woven that, without visible injuries, "it's her word against his" (p. 29).

**5. Enforce trivial demands.** Trivial rules "develop the habit of compliance"; they may be themed or arbitrary, "ever-changing and often contradictory" (p. 29). To avoid punishment she must see through his eyes and predict his demands. Jasmine had to photograph which room she was in. Stark: rules about vacuum lines, bedspread height, bath temperature; "Since the only purpose of the rules is to exact obedience, they are continually being revised." Compliance can become so exhausting that it does not occur to her she is being abused (p. 30).

## If you build things

Isolation, monopolised perception and exhaustion now run partly through devices: controlling which contacts are allowed, reading messages, requiring location check-ins, flooding a phone with calls. Product decisions about shared accounts, location sharing and message visibility can make these easier or harder.
