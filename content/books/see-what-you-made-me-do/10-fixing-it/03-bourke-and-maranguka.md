## Learning objectives

## The ideas

**Justice reinvestment.** Texas shelved $523 million for 14,000 prison beds and invested in treatment and post-release support; parole revocations fell 25%, prison growth was 90% below projection, and a prison closed (pp. 291–292). Don Weatherburn: "You really need to offer the carrot and the stick" (p. 290).

**Bourke.** A town of 2,600 at the edge of the Outback, once called the most dangerous town; a third Indigenous from over twenty language groups, the legacy of missions (p. 290). Elder Alistair Ferguson saw services funded in isolation and competing (p. 291). Maranguka ("caring for others" in Ngemba) and the **Bourke Tribal Council** — representatives of all 27 groups — plus a youth council made it "their programme" (p. 292). Data showed 62% of youth offences between 6 pm and 6 am — hours when youth services were closed. Unlicensed driving was a key "circuit breaker": a donated car and eight off-duty police instructors cut under-25 arrests for unlicensed driving by 72% (p. 293). "Our Place" raised school attendance 25% and cut suspensions 79%.

**Operation Solidarity.** Superintendent Greg Moore rejected "it's just black love": "it's a loving culture" (p. 294). Police visit high-risk perpetrators and victims to check orders and ask what would help (jobs, substance treatment, parenting). With a local Aboriginal role model, police "wore down" a man who beat his wife; he joined a men's group and had no repeat incident for over a year (pp. 294–295). Daily 9.30 am check-ins at the Maranguka hub; on Thursdays all agencies triage together (p. 295).

**Results.** By 2017 domestic violence assaults fell 39%; drug prosecutions 39%, driving offences 34%, non-DV assaults 35%; high school completion up 31% (pp. 295–296). Across the command, domestic homicides went from seven in 2016 to zero for eighteen months; repeat victimisation down a third; cooperation with legal action from 68% to 85%; conviction rate 75%; savings about $3.1 million in 2017 (p. 296).

**Common features.** Community-led; deep collaboration; seeing perpetrators "as individuals capable of rationality and redemption"; victim protection first; long-term rather than political-cycle programmes; "place-based", not one-size-fits-all (p. 297). "Revolutions are impossible, until they are inevitable" (p. 298).
